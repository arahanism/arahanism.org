## Overview

This repository contains the official textual corpus and structured data archive of **Arahanism.org**, the digital research platform dedicated to recovering Gautama's original liberation teaching.

Published by **Arahanist Press**, the archive preserves original research, textual translations, philosophical investigations, and systematic presentations of Arahanism—the Liberation Path of Gautama, the Supreme Arahant.

Rooted in the early Theravāda Sutta-Vinaya tradition, Arahanism emphasizes literal philological precision, etymological investigation, and the recovery of Gautama's original path of renunciation and liberation.

Its interpretive methodology is grounded in three complementary principles:

1. **Sutta-Vinaya Originalism**
2. **Sutta-Vinaya Textualism**
3. **Sutta-Vinaya Supremacy**

The path of liberation progresses through **Sīla → Samādhi → Paññā → Nibbidā → Virāga → Vimutti**, culminating in complete liberation from worldly bondage and attainment of the Deathless (*Nibbāna-dhātu*).

## Core Themes & Terminology Framework

This archive preserves the distinctive terminology and interpretive framework of Arahanist scholarship.

- **Attā / Ātmā / Appā:** Soul-Spirit, distinguished from the five aggregates and worldly phenomena.

- **Citta:** Mind-Heart, distinguished from sense-generated consciousness (*viññāṇa*).

- **Viññāṇa:** Sense-generated consciousness, arising in dependence upon sensory conditions.

- **Anattā:** The five aggregates and conditioned worldly phenomena are not Attā (Soul-Spirit). Arahanism rejects the interpretation of *anattā* as a categorical denial of Soul-Spirit.

- **Nibbāna-dhātu:** The Deathless realm beyond the conditioned universe, understood as the ultimate destination of liberation.

- **Rāga, Dosa, Moha:** Fundamental roots of worldly bondage, preserved in their original Pāli terminology.

- **Bhikkhu / Bhikkhunī:** Fully ordained renunciants undertaking the complete discipline leading toward Arahantship.

- **Brahmacariya:** The complete practice for Arahantship, founded upon renunciation, celibacy, restraint, and spiritual discipline.

- **Pabbajjā:** Renunciation, marking the formal departure from worldly life into the monastic path.

These interpretations represent the original research and doctrinal conclusions of Arahanist scholarship.
