**Dhammapada: The Eternal Treasury of Wisdom**

The Dhammapada is considered a direct transmission of Buddha's original and true wisdom, offering profound insights into the nature of existence and the path to liberation. It is an anthology of concise, memorable verses covering a wide range of ethical and spiritual topics. It is a timeless treasure trove of wisdom, suitable to both monastics and lay practitioners. It guides us on the path to liberation by emphasizing mindfulness, self-control, understanding impermanence of the worldly, and cultivating wholesome qualities. Its verses continue to inspire and guide practitioners on the path to peace and enlightenment. Here\'s a summary of its key points, emphasizing its connection to Gautama Buddha\'s core doctrine:

**1. The Centrality of the Mind in Experience (Buddha\'s Emphasis on Mental Culture):**

- **Mind as the source of all experience:** Echoing Gautama Buddha\'s emphasis on the mind\'s role, the Dhammapada begins by stating that our thoughts and mental states are the primary force shaping our actions and experiences. This aligns with the Buddha\'s teachings on Dependent Origination, where mental processes are key links in the chain of cause and effect. \"Will is the forerunner of all dhamma. Will is their chief; they are all produced by will.\" (Verse 1)

- **Mental discipline as the path to liberation:** Consistent with Gautama Buddha\'s focus on self-mastery, the Dhammapada stresses the importance of cultivating a disciplined and controlled mind as essential for achieving happiness and liberation from suffering.

- **Strong Zeal and Self-Control:** The Dhammapada strongly emphasizes the importance of a strong zeal (Appamāda) and self-control. It highlights the power of the mind and the need to cultivate wholesome mental states while abandoning unwholesome ones. Verses often stress the consequences of our thoughts and actions, emphasizing personal responsibility for our own happiness and suffering.

**2. The Inexorable Law of Cause and Effect (Kamma - A Core Buddhist Principle):**

- **Actions have consequences (Kamma):** The Dhammapada reinforces Gautama Buddha\'s teaching on Kamma, explaining that every action, whether of body, speech, or mind, has consequences that will inevitably manifest. This reflects Gautama Buddha\'s analysis of how our actions create the conditions for future experiences. \"By oneself is evil done; by oneself is one defiled; by oneself is evil avoided; by oneself is one purified. Purity and impurity depend on oneself; no one can purify another.\" (Verse 165)  

- **The Importance of Effort and Practice:** The Dhammapada stresses the importance of personal effort and consistent practice on the path to liberation. It's not enough to simply hear the teachings; one must actively cultivate them in one's own life.

- **Individual responsibility (A Key Aspect of Buddha\'s Doctrine):** In line with Gautama Buddha\'s emphasis on self-reliance, the Dhammapada underscores that individuals are solely responsible for their actions and the resulting consequences.

**3. The Universal Principle of Impermanence (Anicca) and Unsatisfactoriness (Dukkha) for all Dhamma (A Fundamental Buddhist Teaching):**

- **Impermanence (Anicca) of all dhamma (phenomena):** The Dhammapada reflects Gautama Buddha\'s profound insight into the nature of the reality we are living in, highlighting that all things in the world (dhamma) are subject to change, arising and passing away. Recognizing this impermanence (Anicca) for all dhamma is crucial for developing detachment from the world and wisdom, as taught by the Buddha. \"Everything arise from the mind and their worldly manifest are impermanent; when one sees this with wisdom, then one becomes dispassionate towards the worldly life; this is the path to purity.\" (Verse 277)

- **Unsatisfactoriness (Dukkha):** The text acknowledges the reality of **Dukkha** in human samsāric existence. It identifies craving (Taṇhā) as the root cause of the samsāric existence, and points towards the path to its cessation.

**4. The Path to Liberation (Nibbana - The Ultimate Goal in Buddhism):** The Dhammapada outlines the path to liberation, primarily through ethical conduct (Sila), mind development (Samadhi), and wisdom (Pañña). It emphasizes the importance of cultivating virtues such as compassion, non-violence, honesty, and selflessness.

- **The Goal of Nibbāna:** While not always explicitly named, the ultimate goal hinted at throughout the Dhammapada is Nibbāna, the cessation of suffering and the attainment of perfect peace.

- **The Eightfold Path (The Buddha\'s Prescription for Ending Suffering):** The Dhammapada implicitly guides the reader towards the Eightfold Path, Gautama Buddha\'s practical guide to ending suffering and attaining Nibbana. This path encompasses Right Understanding, Right Thought, Right Speech, Right Action, Right Livelihood, Right Effort, Right Mindfulness, and Right Concentration.  

- **Overcoming craving and attachment** (The Cause of our Involuntary Reincarnation, as Taught by Gautama Buddha): The teachings of the Dhammapada align with Gautama Buddha\'s concept of dukkha as inherent in the world, leading to the birth, aging, and sufferings for all worldly existence. The root cause of our perpetual cycle of birth, aging and death in this world is our attachment and craving for worldly possessions. By transcending these desires, one can attain liberation from this cycle and reach Nibbana-dhātu, a state of eternal bliss and freedom from suffering. Nibbana-dhātu represents the ultimate goal of Gautama Buddha\'s teachings, offering a permanent sanctuary free from the burdens of aging and death.

**5. Ethical Conduct (Sila - A Cornerstone of Buddhist Practice):**

- **Importance of morality (Sila):** The Dhammapada emphasizes the importance of ethical conduct (Sila), aligning with Gautama Buddha\'s teachings on moral discipline as a foundation for spiritual progress. This includes principles like non-violence, honesty, and self-control. \"Avoid all evil, cultivate good, and purify your mind --- this is the teaching of the Buddhas.\" (Verse 183)

- **Compassion and loving-kindness (Essential Qualities in Gautama Buddha\'s Teachings):** Cultivating compassion (Karuna) and loving-kindness (Metta) towards all beings, as advocated by Gautama Buddha, is highlighted in the Dhammapada as essential for spiritual growth.

**6. Wisdom and Self-Awareness (Panna - The Culmination of the Buddhist Path):**

- **Importance of wisdom (Panna):** The Dhammapada stresses the development of wisdom (Panna) and insight into the true nature of reality, echoing Gautama Buddha\'s emphasis on wisdom as the key to liberation.

- **The Nature of the Wise and the Foolish:** Many verses contrast the characteristics of the wise (Paṇḍita) and the foolish (Bāla). The wise are those who are mindful, disciplined, and strive for liberation, while the foolish are those who are attached to worldly pleasures and engage in unwholesome actions.

- **Self-awareness and introspection (Essential Tools for Self-Transformation in Buddhism):** Examining one\'s own thoughts and actions, as encouraged in the Dhammapada, reflects Gautama Buddha\'s teaching on the importance of self-awareness and introspection for self-improvement and spiritual progress.

**Why it's a Timeless Treasure:**

- **Universality:** The Dhammapada's teachings on ethics, mindfulness, and the nature of reality are universal and applicable to people of all backgrounds and time periods.

- **Conciseness and Memorability:** The verses are short, poetic, and easy to memorize, making them ideal for reflection and contemplation.

- **Practical Guidance:** The text provides practical guidance for living a more ethical, mindful, and meaningful life.

- **Direct Access to the Buddha's Wisdom:** As a collection attributed to the Buddha, the Dhammapada is considered a direct transmission of his original and true wisdom, offering profound insights into the nature of existence and the path to liberation.

In essence, the Dhammapada serves as a concise and practical guide to Gautama Buddha\'s original teachings, offering a path to living a moral, meaningful life, cultivating mental discipline, and ultimately achieving liberation from suffering. It emphasizes personal responsibility, the power of the mind, and the importance of wisdom and compassion, all of which are central to Gautama Buddha\'s doctrine.

## 《法句经》概要：永恒的智慧宝藏

《法句经》被认为是佛陀原始和真实智慧的直接传承，它提供了对存在本质和解脱之路的深刻见解。它是一部包含423首简洁、易记的偈颂的诗集，涵盖了广泛的伦理和精神主题。它是永恒的智慧宝藏，适合僧侣和在家修行者。它通过强调正念、自制、理解世间无常和培养善的品质来指导我们走上解脱之路。它的偈颂继续激励和指导修行者走向和平与觉悟。以下是其要点的总结，重点强调其与乔达摩佛陀核心教义的联系：

**1. 经验中心的关键是心（佛陀对心性的强调）：**

- **心是所有经验的根源：** 《法句经》呼应了乔达摩·佛陀对心的作用的强调，开篇就指出，我们的思想和精神状态是塑造我们行为和经验的主要力量。这与佛陀关于缘起的教导一致，其中精神过程是因果链中的关键环节。"意为法之先导，意为彼等之主，一切由意所生。"（第一颂）

- **精神训练是解脱的道路：** 与乔达摩·佛陀对自我控制的关注一致，《法句经》强调培养有纪律和受控的心对于获得幸福和从痛苦中解脱至关重要。

- **强烈的热忱和自制力：** 《法句经》强烈强调强烈热忱（Appamāda）和自制力的重要性。它突出了心的力量以及培养健康的心理状态同时放弃不健康的心理状态的必要性。偈颂经常强调我们思想和行为的后果，强调个人对自身幸福和痛苦的责任。

**2. 不可避免的因果法则（业------佛教的核心原则）：**

- **行为有后果（业）：** 《法句经》强化了乔达摩·佛陀关于业的教导，解释说，无论是身、语、意，每一个行为都有必然显现的后果。这反映了乔达摩·佛陀对我们的行为如何为未来的经验创造条件的分析。"恶自作，自受污；恶不作，自清净。净不净依自身，无人能净他人。"（第 165 颂）

- **努力和实践的重要性：** 《法句经》强调个人努力和在解脱道路上持续实践的重要性。仅仅听闻教义是不够的；一个人必须在自己的生活中积极地培养它们。

- **个人责任（佛陀教义的关键方面）：** 与乔达摩·佛陀对自力更生的强调一致，《法句经》强调个人对自己的行为和由此产生的后果负全部责任。

**3. 一切法（现象）的普遍无常（Anicca）和苦（Dukkha）原则（佛教的基本教义）：**

- **一切法（现象）的无常（Anicca）：** 《法句经》反映了乔达摩·佛陀对我们所生活现实本质的深刻洞察，强调世间万物（法）都受制于变化、生起和消逝。正如佛陀所教导的那样，认识到一切法的这种无常（Anicca）对于培养对世界的超脱和智慧至关重要。"诸法意先行，意主使意造；若以净意行，或乐或苦随。"（第 1、2 颂，此为意译，与原文稍有出入，但表达了类似的思想）

- **苦（Dukkha）：** 经文承认人类轮回存在中苦的现实。它将渴爱（Taṇhā）确定为轮回存在的根本原因，并指出了通往其止息的道路。

**4. 解脱之路（涅槃------佛教的最终目标）：**

《法句经》概述了解脱的道路，主要通过伦理行为（戒）、心智发展（定）和智慧（慧）。它强调培养诸如慈悲、非暴力、诚实和无私等美德的重要性。

- **涅槃的目标：** 虽然并非总是明确提及，《法句经》中暗示的最终目标是涅槃，即痛苦的止息和完美平静的获得。

- **八正道（佛陀为结束痛苦开出的处方）：** 《法句经》隐含地引导读者走向八正道，这是乔达摩·佛陀为结束痛苦和获得涅槃而提供的实践指南。这条道路包括正见、正思惟、正语、正业、正命、正精进、正念和正定。

- **克服渴爱和执着（乔达摩·佛陀教导的我们非自愿轮回的原因）：** 《法句经》的教义与乔达摩·佛陀关于苦存在于世间的概念一致，导致所有世俗存在的出生、衰老和痛苦。我们在这个世界中永恒的出生、衰老和死亡循环的根本原因是我们的执着和对世俗事物的渴求。通过超越这些欲望，一个人可以从这个循环中获得解脱，并达到涅槃界，一个永恒的幸福和摆脱痛苦的状态。涅槃界代表了乔达摩·佛陀教义的最终目标，提供了一个永久的庇护所，摆脱了衰老和死亡的重负。

**5. 伦理行为（戒------佛教实践的基石）：**

- **道德的重要性（戒）：** 《法句经》强调伦理行为（戒）的重要性，这与乔达摩·佛陀关于道德戒律是精神进步的基础的教导一致。这包括诸如非暴力、诚实和自制等原则。"诸恶莫作，诸善奉行，自净其意，是诸佛教。"（第 183 颂）

- **慈悲和慈爱（乔达摩·佛陀教义中的基本品质）：** 正如乔达摩·佛陀所倡导的那样，《法句经》强调培养对一切众生的慈悲（Karuna）和慈爱（Metta），认为这是精神成长的必要条件。

**6. 智慧和自我意识（慧------佛教道路的顶峰）：**

- **智慧的重要性（慧）：** 《法句经》强调发展智慧（慧）和对现实真正本质的洞察力，这与乔达摩·佛陀强调智慧是解脱的关键的教导相呼应。

- **智者和愚者的本质：** 许多偈颂对比了智者（Paṇḍita）和愚者（Bāla）的特征。智者是那些正念、有纪律并努力寻求解脱的人，而愚者是那些执着于世俗享乐并从事不善行为的人。

- **自我意识和内省（佛教中自我转化的重要工具）：** 正如《法句经》所鼓励的那样，审视自己的思想和行为反映了乔达摩·佛陀关于自我意识和内省对于自我完善和精神进步的重要性的教导。

**为何它是永恒的宝藏：**

- **普遍性：** 《法句经》关于伦理、正念和现实本质的教义是普遍的，适用于所有背景和时代的人们。

- **简洁性和易记性：** 这些偈颂简短、富有诗意且易于记忆，使其成为反思和沉思的理想选择。

- **实践指导：** 该文本为过上更合乎道德、正念和有意义的生活提供了实践指导。

- **直接获得佛陀的智慧：** 作为一部归于佛陀的著作，《法句经》被认为是其原始和真实智慧的直接传承，提供了对存在本质和解脱之路的深刻见解。

本质上，《法句经》是乔达摩·佛陀原始教义的简洁而实用的指南，提供了一条通往道德、有意义的生活、培养精神纪律并最终实现从痛苦中解脱的道路。它强调个人责任、心的力量以及智慧和慈悲的重要性，所有这些都是乔达摩·佛陀教义的核心。

### **Summary of Yamaka Vagga**

This chapter primarily explores the relationship between intention (will), actions, and their consequences, as well as how to attain liberation through right conduct and thought. The core ideas can be summarized as follows:

1.  **Mind is the Forerunner:** All actions (of body, speech, and mind) are preceded by intention. Intention is the ruler; everything is produced by intention. Impure intention leads to suffering, like the wheel follows the hoof of the ox; pure intention brings happiness, like a shadow that never leaves. This emphasizes the importance of controlling and purifying the mind, which is the foundation of all good deeds.

2.  **Cessation of Hatred:** Hatred is never appeased by hatred; only by non-hatred is hatred appeased. This is an eternal law. Holding onto past hurts only intensifies suffering; letting go of resentment brings inner peace. Recognizing the inevitability of death also helps to quell disputes.

3.  **Diligence and Negligence:** Those who indulge in worldly pleasures, do not restrain their senses, are immoderate in eating, lazy, and weak in strength are easily defeated by \"Mara\" (symbolizing temptations and obstacles), like a weak tree blown down by the wind. Conversely, those who contemplate the impermanent nature of things, restrain their senses, are moderate in eating, have firm faith, and strive diligently are not easily defeated by \"Mara,\" like a solid rock. This emphasizes the importance of diligent practice and self-discipline.

4.  **The Meaning of the Robe:** One who wears the orange robe but has not purified inner defilements and does not possess the Dhamma of truth is not worthy of the robe. Only one who has purged all defilements, is well-established in morality (sila), and possesses the Dhamma of truth is truly worthy of the robe. This illustrates that inner practice is more important than outward forms.

5.  **Grasping the Essential:** Regarding the non-essential as essential and the essential as non-essential is a wrong direction of effort and will not lead to the important goal. Conversely, regarding the essential as essential and the non-essential as non-essential is the right direction and will lead to the important goal. This emphasizes the need for correct judgment and focus.

6.  **Karmic Consequences:** Those who do evil grieve in this world and the next; those who do good rejoice in this world and the next. This emphasizes the law of cause and effect, encouraging people to do good and avoid evil.

7.  **Consistency of Words and Deeds:** One who talks and plans a lot but does not act accordingly is negligent, like a cowherd counting the cattle of others, not a true practitioner. On the other hand, even if one speaks and plans little, but practices according to the Dhamma, abandoning craving, hatred, and delusion, striving for perfect liberation of the mind, and not clinging to this world or the next, that one is a true practitioner. This emphasizes the importance of practice; knowing and acting in accordance with the Dhamma is true practice.

In summary, the Yamaka Vagga uses comparisons and metaphors to explain the relationships between intention, action, cause and effect, and practice, urging people to purify their minds, cease hatred, strive diligently, discern right from wrong, and be consistent in word and deed, so as to ultimately attain liberation.**\**

The zealous bhikkhu, realizing the danger of zealless, unable to regress, he is just in the vicinity of the nibbāna. 乐热忱比丘，或者惧放逸，已邻近涅槃，必不会退步。

### **Summary of Appamāda Vagga**

In Theravada Buddhism, appamāda is widely recognized as the fundamental pillar of all spiritual progress. It holds a central role in the practice of meditation, serving as the bedrock for all virtuous mental states. The Buddha himself emphasized the importance of appamāda in his parting words to his followers, as: \"Transient are all formations. Strive zealously!\" (appamādena sampādetha). This concept stresses the necessity of unwavering effort and energetic dedication in one\'s spiritual pursuit.

It is crucial to acknowledge that the meaning of appamāda evolves over time, beginning as a powerful emotional drive (equivalent to Chanda in the Four Iddhipada) and gradually encompassing a broader scope that includes mindfulness and wisdom. This evolution underscores the multifaceted nature of appamāda, highlighting its transformative impact on the individual\'s spiritual journey.

The etymology of *appamāda* can be analyzed as follows: *ma* signifies death, *pa* indicates \"going to,\" and *pama* thus means \"going to die.\" Therefore, *appama* signifies \"not to die.\"

To illustrate *appamāda*, consider someone falling into a river and facing the imminent threat of drowning (pama -- \"going to die\"). The natural instinct for survival ignites a powerful desire to live (appama -- \"not to die\"). This manifests as a determined struggle to stay afloat and reach the shore. This urgent striving to preserve life is the essence of *appamāda*: not merely a passive wish to avoid death, but an active, unwavering commitment to life that translates into zeal, diligence, heedfulness, and earnest effort.

Appamāda Vagga emphasizes the crucial role of zeal, diligence, earnestness, and mindfulness (appamāda) in spiritual progress and the attainment of liberation (Nibbana). It contrasts this with heedlessness, negligence, and apathy (pamāda), which are likened to a path leading to death.

Zeal (appamāda) is the path to the Deathless (amata), while zeallessness (pamāda) is the path to death (maccu). The zealous do not truly die, while the zealless are as if already dead. This central theme underscores the urgency of consistent effort and mindful living.

- **Zeal as the Foundation of Progress (Verses 1-3):** Zeal is the basis for achieving immortality, while zeallessness leads to a state akin to death. The wise understand this distinction and rejoice in zeal, finding delight in the realm of the Noble Ones (ariyānaṃ gocare). Through constant meditation and strong effort, the wise attain Nibbana, the supreme peace.

- **Benefits of Zeal (Verse 4):** Diligence in mindfulness, purity in action, careful consideration, self-restraint, and righteous living according to the Dhamma all contribute to the steady growth of zeal and spiritual progress.

- **Zeal as a Refuge (Verse 5):** The wise, through their zeal and controlled thoughts, create an \"island\" of refuge for themselves, which cannot be overwhelmed by the \"flood\" of worldly distractions and suffering.

- **The Contrast Between Wise and Foolish (Verse 6):** Foolish and ignorant people indulge in zeallessness, while the wise guard zeal as their most precious treasure.

- **Avoiding Sensual Pleasures (Verse 7):** One should not indulge in zeallessness or delight in sensual pleasures. The zealous meditator, through their practice, attains abundant bliss.

- **Wisdom Gained Through Zeal (Verse 8):** By overcoming zeallessness with zeal, the wise ascend to the \"palace of wisdom\" and, free from sorrow, observe the sorrowful, ignorant masses below, like a mountaineer viewing those on the plains.

- **Zeal Among the Zealless (Verse 9):** The wise person is zealous among the zealless and awake among the sleeping, progressing swiftly like a strong horse leaving a weak one behind.

- **Exemplars of Zeal (Verse 10):** Maghava (Indra), through his zeal, became the chief of the devas (gods). Zeal is always praised, while zeallessness is always condemned.

- **The Zealous Monk (Verses 11-12):** The bhikkhu (monk) who delights in zeal and sees the danger in zeallessness advances like a fire, burning away all fetters, both small and large. Such a bhikkhu is close to Nibbana and incapable of falling back.

**In essence, the Appamāda Vagga stresses the following:**

- Consistent effort and mindfulness are essential for spiritual progress and the attainment of Nibbana.

- Zeallessness and indulgence in sensual pleasures lead to suffering and stagnation.

- The wise cultivate zeal as their greatest treasure, achieving wisdom, peace, and liberation.

This chapter uses powerful metaphors and examples to illustrate the importance of maintaining a vigilant and earnest approach to spiritual practice. It emphasizes that liberation is not achieved through chance or external forces, but through consistent self-discipline, mindfulness, and the unwavering pursuit of the Dhamma.

**1(33). Phandanaṃ capalaṃ cittaṃ, dūrakkhaṃ dunnivārayaṃ;\
Ujuṃ karoti medhāvī, usukāro'va tejanaṃ.**

The wavering, unsteady citta (mind), hard to guard, difficult to restrain --- the wise person straightens it like an arrow-maker straightens an arrow. 变易不定心，难守难克制。智者调直之，如匠搦箭直。

**2(34). Vārijo'va thale khitto, okamokata ubbhato;\
Pariphandatimidaṃ cittaṃ, māradheyyaṃ pahātave.**

Like a fish that is drawn from its watery abode and thrown upon land, this citta (mind) trembles in order to leave Mara\'s realm. 如鱼离水栖，投于陆地上，此心亦颤动，为脱死神界。

**3(35). Dunniggahassa lahuno, yattha kāmanipātino;\
Cittassa damatho sādhu, cittaṃ dantaṃ sukhāvahaṃ.**

The mind is difficult to restrain, quick, it falls upon sensual pleasures. Good is the taming of the mind. A restrained mind brings happiness. 此心难克制，轻巧随欲转。善哉心调伏，心调得安乐。

**4(36). Sududdasaṃ sunipuṇaṃ, yattha kāmanipātinaṃ;\
Cittaṃ rakkhetha medhāvī, cittaṃ guttaṃ sukhāvahaṃ.**

The mind is very difficult to see, very subtle, it falls upon sensual pleasures. The wise guard the mind; a guarded mind brings happiness. 极微妙难见，此心随欲转。智者防护心，心护得安乐。

**5(37). Dūraṅgamaṃ ekacaraṃ, asarīraṃ guhāsayaṃ;\
Ye cittaṃ saṃyamessanti, mokkhanti mārabandhanā.**

Wandering alone, dwelling in a cave, no more (rāga, dosa, and moha) emanating from the body; a well restrained mind, is liberated from the bond of Māra. 远行独窟居，脱离身体源。心已被调伏，解脱死神缚。

**\**

**6(38). Anavaṭṭhitacittassa, saddhammaṃ avijānato;\
Pariplavapasādassa, paññā na paripūrati.**

He whose mind is unsteady, he who does not know the true dhamma (Buddha's teachings), he whose faith wavers --- the wisdom of such one will not become perfect. 心若不安定，又不知正法，信心摇摆者，智慧不成就。

**7(39). Anavassutacittassa, ananvāhatacetaso;\
Puñña pāpapahīṇassa, natthi jāgarato bhayaṃ.**

He whose mind is free from desires, who is free from voilence, who is virtuous, who has abandoned wrong doing--- for such a vigilant one there is no fear. 离欲离嗔行，离恶具善德，警戒者无畏。

**8(40). Kumbhūpamaṃ kāyamimaṃ viditvā, nagarūpamaṃ cittamidaṃ ṭhapetvā; Yodhetha māraṃ paññāvudhena, jitañca rakkhe anivesano siyā.**

Having understood this body to be like a jar; having accepted this mind like a fortress, attack Māra with the weapon of wisdom. You should protect the victory with no attachment. 知身如陶器，知心似城堡，慧剑击魔罗，守胜无染着。

**9(41). Aciraṃ vat'ayaṃ kāyo, paṭhaviṃ adhisessati;\
Chuddho apetaviññāṇo, niratthaṃ'va kaḷiṅgaraṃ.**

Not long, this body will lie upon the ground, discarded, without consciousness, worthless, like a piece of worthless wood. 此身实不久，当睡于地下，被弃无意识，无用如木块。

**10(42). Diso disaṃ yaṃ taṃ kayirā, verī vā pana verinaṃ;\
Micchā panihitaṃ cittaṃ, pāpiyo naṃ tato kare.**

Whatever (harm) a foe may do to a foe, or a hater to a hater, a wrongly directed mind can do itself far greater damage. 仇敌害仇敌，怨家对怨家，若心向邪行，恶业最为大。

**11(43). Na taṃ mātā pitā kayirā, aññe vā pi ca ñātakā;\
Sammā panihitaṃ cittaṃ, seyyaso naṃ tato kare.**

What (favor) neither mother, nor father, nor any other relative can do, a properly directed mind can do even far better than that. 非父母之善，非眷属能比，若心向正行，得更好善业。

**\**

### **Summary of Citta Vagga**

Citta Vagga focuses on the nature of the mind (citta), its challenges, and the importance of its control and cultivation for spiritual progress. The mind is fickle, unsteady, difficult to guard and restrain, but through wisdom and diligent effort, it can be tamed and directed towards liberation. An uncontrolled mind is the source of great harm, while a well-directed mind is the source of great good. It stresses the central role of the mind in both suffering and liberation. By understanding the mind\'s nature, diligently guarding and restraining it, and directing it towards wisdom and virtue, one can escape reincarnation and attain true happiness and freedom.

- **The Unruly Nature of the Mind (Verses 1-2):** The mind is described as wavering and unsteady, difficult to guard and restrain. A wise person straightens it, just as an arrow-maker straightens an arrow. Like a fish thrown out of water onto dry land, the mind trembles and struggles to escape Mara\'s realm (the realm of passions and suffering).

- **The Benefit of Taming the Mind (Verses 3-4):** The mind is quick and prone to falling into sensual pleasures. Therefore, taming the mind is good, and a tamed mind brings happiness. The mind is also subtle and difficult to perceive; therefore, the wise person should guard it diligently, as a guarded mind brings happiness.

- **The Wandering Nature of the Mind and Liberation (Verse 5):** The mind wanders far, travels alone, and dwells in a \"cave\" (referring to the body). By restraining the mind, one is liberated from the bonds of Mara. This verse emphasizes the importance of internal control over external circumstances.

- **The Importance of a Stable Mind and True Dhamma (Verse 6):** An unsteady mind, lacking knowledge of the true Dhamma (the Buddha\'s teachings), and wavering faith will not achieve perfect wisdom. This highlights the connection between mental stability, understanding of the Dhamma, and the development of wisdom.

- **Freedom from Fear Through Mental Purity (Verse 7):** One whose mind is free from desires and violence (or ill-will), who is virtuous and has abandoned wrongdoing, has no fear. This emphasizes the connection between mental purity and fearlessness.

- **The Body as a Jar, the Mind as a Fortress (Verse 8):** Understanding the body to be like a fragile jar and establishing the mind like a fortified city, one should fight Mara (temptation and defilements) with the weapon of wisdom. After achieving victory, one should guard it without attachment. This emphasizes using wisdom to overcome defilements and maintaining non-attachment even after achieving spiritual progress.

- **The Impermanence of the Body (Verse 9):** This body will soon lie on the earth, discarded, without consciousness, and as useless as a piece of wood. This reflection on impermanence encourages detachment from the physical form.

- **The Power of a Misdirected Mind (Verse 10):** Whatever harm a foe may do to a foe, or an enemy to an enemy, a wrongly directed mind can do far greater harm to oneself. This emphasizes the destructive power of an uncontrolled mind.

- **The Power of a Well-Directed Mind (Verse 11):** What neither mother, nor father, nor any other relative can do, a properly directed mind can do even better for oneself. This emphasizes the immense potential of a well-controlled mind for good.**\**

**1(44). Ko imaṃ paṭhaviṃ vicessati, yamalokañca imaṃ sadevakaṃ;\
Ko dhammapadaṃ sudesitaṃ, kusalo puppham'iva pacessati.**

Who investigates the traveler, who wanders in this death-ruled-world with all the devas? Who studies this well taught Dhammapada, understand it's as wholesome as flowers? 谁探察这旅者，迷失在诸天的死神界；谁探究此善说之法句，了知其如花一样益善。

**2(45). Sekho paṭhaviṃ vicessati, yamalokañca imaṃ sadevakaṃ;\
Sekho dhammapadaṃ sudesitaṃ, kusalo puppham'iva pacessati.**

A noble learner for purification investigates the traveler, who wanders in this death-ruled-world with all the devas. A noble learner for purification studies the well taught Dhammapada, understand it's as wholesome as flowers. 圣学者探察这旅者，迷失在诸天的死神界；圣学者探究此善说之法句，了知其如花一样益善。

**3(46). Pheṇūpamaṃ kāyamimaṃ viditvā, marīcidhammaṃ abhisambudhāno; Chetvāna mārassa papupphakāni, adassanaṃ maccurājassa gacche.**

Having understood that this body is illusional, having realized its mirage-like nature, one should cutoff the flowers decorated for Māra, and leave beyond the sight of the king of death. 知此身如幻，觉悟是幻法，断死神之花，越死王之所见。

**4(47). Pupphāni h'eva pacinantaṃ, byāsattamanasaṃ naraṃ;\
Suttaṃ gāmaṃ mahogho'va, maccu ādāya gacchati.**

Humans who gather such flowers, with attached minds, the death will carry them away, like a great flood (carries away) a sleeping village. 人采集此花，其心有贪着，死神捉将去，如洪流于睡村。

**5(48). Pupphāni h'eva pacinantaṃ, byāsattamanasaṃ naraṃ;\
Atittaññeva kāmesu, antako kurute vasaṃ.**

Humans who gather such flowers, with attached minds, unsatisfied in sensual desires, are under the control of the Ender (an epithet for Māra). 人采集此花，其心有贪着; 贪欲无厌足，为终结者伏。

**\**

**6(49). Yathā'pi bhamaro pupphaṃ, vaṇṇagandham aheṭhayaṃ;\
Paḷeti rasam'ādāya, evaṃ gāme munī care.**

As a bee without harming the flower, its colour or scent, flies away, collecting only the honey, so should a sage walk through the village. 智者入村落，譬如蜂采花；不坏色与香，但取其蜜去。

**7(50). Na paresaṃ vilomāni, na paresaṃ katākataṃ;\
Attano'va avekkheyya, katāni akatāni ca.**

One should not look at others\' wrong deeds, what the others have done or not; but examine what has and has not done by oneself. 不观他人过,他人作不作,但观自身行,已作与未作.

**8(51). Yathā'pi ruciraṃ pupphaṃ, vaṇṇavantaṃ agandhakaṃ;\
Evaṃ subhāsitā vācā, aphalā hoti akubbato.**

Like a beautiful flower without scent, a well said speech of somebody, who does not act accordingly, is fruitless. it is well said that one who does not practise is fruitless. 犹如鲜妙花，色美而无香，如是说善语，彼不行无果。

**9(52). Yathā'pi ruciraṃ pupphaṃ, vaṇṇavantaṃ sugandhakaṃ;\
Evaṃ subhāsitā vācā, saphalā hoti kubbato.**

Like a beautiful and fragrant flower, a well said speech of somebody, who does act accordingly, is fruitful. 犹如鲜妙花，色美而芳香，如是说善语，彼实行有果。

**10(53). Yathā'pi puppharāsimhā, kayirā mālāguṇe bahū;\
Evaṃ jātena maccena, kattabbaṃ kusalaṃ bahuṃ.**

Just as from the heap of flowers one can do a lot of garlands, so a born mortal should do a lot of meritorious deeds. 如从诸花堆，得造众花鬘，如是生为人，当做诸善事。

**11(54). Na pupphagandho paṭivātam eti, na candanaṃ tagaramallikā;\
Satañca gandho paṭivātam eti, sabbā disā sappuriso pavāyati.**

The scent of flowers, sandal, tagara or jasmine does not go against the wind; but the fragrance of the righteous man blows against the wind and pervades in every direction. 花香不逆风， 德香逆风薰，遍闻于诸方。

**\**

**12(55). Candanaṃ tagaraṃ vā'pi, uppalaṃ atha vassikī;\
Etesaṃ gandhajātānaṃ, sīlagandho anuttaro.**

Sandalwood, tagara, lotus, jasmine: above all these kinds of fragrance, the scent of sīla is the best. 檀香多伽罗，茉莉与青莲，如是诸香中，戒香为最上。

**13(56). Appamatto ayaṃ gandho, yvāyaṃ tagaracandanaṃ;\
Yo ca sīlavataṃ gandho, vāti devesu uttamo.**

Of little account is the fragrance of tagara or sandal; the scent of sīla blows even among the devas, it is the best. 檀香多伽罗，此等香甚微。持戒者最上，香薰诸天间。

**14(57). Tesaṃ sampanna sīlānaṃ, Appamāda vihārinaṃ;\
Sammadaññā vimuttānaṃ, māro maggaṃ na vindati.**

Completely endowed with sīla, dwell in zeal, perfectly understand liberation, then māra will not find your way. 成就诸戒行，住于热忱中，正知解脱者，死神不能达。

**15(58). Yathā saṅkāradhānasmiṃ, ujjhitasmiṃ mahāpathe.\
Padumaṃ tattha jāyetha, sucigandhaṃ manoramaṃ.**

Just as at the rubbish heap, thrown out by the big road, can grow a lotus, of clean smell and mind pleasing. 犹如垃圾堆，弃着于大道，莲花生其中，洁净而悦意。

**16(59). Evaṃ saṅkārabhūtesu, andhabhūte puthujjane.\
Atirocati paññāya, sammā-sambuddha-sāvako.**

Thus, among the dust-like beings, amongst ignorant ordinary people, there are those magnificently shine with wisdom, they are the followers of the Fully Enlightened One. 如是尘埃众，盲昧凡夫中，正觉者弟子，智慧光广照。

### **Summary of Puppha Vagga**

Puppha Vagga uses the metaphor of flowers to illustrate various aspects of the Dhamma, particularly focusing on impermanence, attachment, the importance of practice, and the supreme value of virtue. Just as flowers are beautiful but transient, so too are worldly pleasures. True value lies in cultivating virtue (sila) and wisdom, which, unlike fleeting scents, spread far and wide, even against the wind. It encourages self-reflection, detachment from worldly pleasures, and the cultivation of wisdom and good conduct.

- **The Search for Truth (Verses 44-45):** These verses pose the question of who can truly understand the nature of existence and the well-taught Dhammapada. The answer is the \"sekha\" (learner, one under training), who investigates the world and its inhabitants, understanding the Dhamma as wholesome and beneficial as flowers.

- **The Illusory Nature of the Body and Escape from Mara (Verse 46):** Recognizing the body\'s illusory, mirage-like nature is crucial. By cutting off \"Mara\'s flowers\" (attachments and defilements), one transcends the realm of death.

- **The Danger of Attachment (Verses 47-48):** Those attached to worldly pleasures, symbolized by \"gathering flowers,\" are carried away by death like a flood sweeping away a sleeping village. Unsatisfied desires leave one under Mara\'s control. These verses warn against the dangers of clinging to transient things.

- **The Sage\'s Conduct (Verse 49):** A sage, like a bee collecting honey without harming the flower\'s color or scent, moves through the world taking what is essential (spiritual nourishment) without causing harm or becoming attached. This emphasizes non-harming and detachment.

- **Focus on Self-Reflection (Verse 50):** One should not focus on the faults of others but diligently examine one\'s own actions, both done and undone. This emphasizes self-awareness and personal responsibility.

- **The Importance of Practice (Verses 51-52):** Just as a beautiful but scentless flower is of little value, well-spoken words are fruitless without corresponding action. Conversely, beautiful and fragrant flowers represent words accompanied by practice, which bear fruit. This stresses the necessity of putting the Dhamma into action.

- **Accumulating Merit (Verse 53):** Just as many garlands can be made from a heap of flowers, so too should mortals accumulate many meritorious deeds during their lifetime. This encourages the performance of good actions.

- **The Fragrance of Virtue (Verses 54-57):** The scent of flowers like sandalwood, tagara, and jasmine does not travel against the wind, but the \"fragrance\" of virtuous conduct (sila) travels in all directions, even reaching the devas (gods). This highlights the superior and far-reaching influence of virtue compared to worldly things. The fragrance of virtue is the most excellent among all fragrances. Those who are endowed with virtue, dwell in heedfulness, and are liberated by perfect knowledge are beyond Mara\'s reach.

- **The Lotus from the Rubbish Heap (Verses 58-59):** Just as a fragrant and beautiful lotus can grow from a rubbish heap, so too can a wise disciple of the Buddha shine with wisdom amidst ordinary, ignorant people (the \"dust-like beings\"). This offers hope and illustrates that enlightenment is possible regardless of one\'s background.**\**

[]{#_Toc188087600 .anchor}**5. Bāla Vagga -- Fool 愚**

**1(60). Dīghā jāgarato ratti, dīghaṃ santassa yojanaṃ;\
Dīgho bālānaṃ saṃsāro, addhammaṃ avijānataṃ.**

Long is the night to the wakeful; a journey is long to the weary; long is samsāra to fools who do not know the Dhamma. 不眠者夜长，倦者旅途长，不明达正法──愚者轮回长。

**2(61). Carañce nādhigaccheyya, seyyaṃ sadisam attano;\
Ekacariyaṃ daḷhaṃ kayirā, natthi bāle sahāyatā.**

If a wanderer should not find a companion better or equal to oneself, then he should resolutely wander alone. There is no companionship with a fool. 不得胜我者为友，与我相等者亦无，宁可坚决独行，不与愚人作伴。

**3(62). Puttā m'atthi dhanam m'atthi, iti bālo vihaññati;\
Attā hi attano natthi, kuto puttā kuto dhanaṃ.**

"I have sons; I have wealth"; this is what the fool worries. He himself is not his own. Whence sons? Whence wealth? 「我有子与财」，愚人常为忧。他并非自有(他自己尚且不属于他自己)，何有子与财？

**4(63). Yo bālo maññati bālyaṃ, paṇḍito vā'pi tena so;\
Bālo ca paṇḍitamānī, sa ve "bālo"ti vuccati.**

The fool who knows he is a fool is wise in that at least; the fool who thinks that he is wise is called a fool. 愚者自知愚，彼即是智人。愚人自谓智，实称真愚夫。

**5(64). Yāvajīvam'pi ce bālo, paṇḍitaṃ payirupāsati;\
Na so dhammaṃ vijānāti, dabbi sūparasaṃ yathā.**

Though a fool associates with a wise man his whole life, he understands the Dhamma no more than a spoon knows the flavour of soup. 愚者虽终身，亲近于智者，彼不了知法， 如匙尝汤味。

**6(65). Muhuttam api ce viññu, paṇḍitaṃ payirupāsati;\
Khippaṃ dhammaṃ vijānāti, jivhā sūparasaṃ yathā.**

Though an intelligent person, associates with a wise man for only a moment, he quickly understands the Dhamma as the tongue knows the flavour of soup. 慧者须臾顷，亲近于智者，能速了解法，如舌尝汤味。

**7(66). Caranti bālā dummedhā, amitten'eva attanā;\
Karontā pāpakaṃ kammaṃ, yaṃ hoti kaṭukapphalaṃ.**

The stupid fools behave as if they themselves were their enemies, doing bad deeds, which have bitter fruit. 愚人不觉知，与自己为敌，造作诸恶业，来受更苦果。

**8(67). Na taṃ kammaṃ kataṃ sādhu, yaṃ katvā anutappati;\
Yassa assumukho rodaṃi, vpākaṃ paṭisevati.**

That deed is not well done, which having done it, one repents, one weeps with a tearful face, on reaping its results. 彼作不善业，作已生后悔，哭泣泪满面，为得其果报。

**9(68). Tañca kammaṃ kataṃ sādhu, yaṃ katvā nānutappati;\
Yassa patīto sumano, vipākaṃ paṭisevati.**

That deed is well done when, after having done it, one repents not, and with joy and pleasure, one reaps the fruit thereof. 若彼作善业，作已不追悔，欢喜而愉悦，为得其果报。

**10(69). Madhu vā maññati bālo, yāva pāpaṃ na paccati;\
Yadā ca paccatī pāpaṃ, bālo[16](javascript:void(0)) dukkhaṃ nigacchati.**

As sweet as honey is an evil deed, so thinks the fool so long as it ripens not; but when it ripens, then he comes to grief. 恶业未成熟，愚人思如蜜; 恶业成熟时，愚人必受苦。

**11(70). Māse māse kusaggena bālo, bhuñjetha bhojanaṃ;\
Na so saṅkhātadhammānaṃ, kalaṃ agghati soḷasiṃ.**

Month after month a fool may eat only the smallest possible amount (with the tip of a kusa grass); but he is not worth a sixteenth part of those who have comprehended the Dhamma. 愚者月复月，虽取甚少食; 彼所得功德，不及得法者，十六分之一。

**12(71). Na hi pāpaṃ kataṃ kammaṃ, sajju khīraṃ 'va muccati;\
Ḍahantaṃ bālam anveti, bhasmacchanno'va pāvako.**

An evil deed does not immediately bear fruit, just as milk does not curdle at once; Burning, it follows the fool like fire covered with ashes. 犹如牛乳酪，醍醐非速成。愚人造恶业，不即感恶果，业力随其后，如死灰覆火。

**\**

**13(72). Yāvadeva anatthāya, ñattaṃ bālassa jāyati;\
Hanti bālassa sukkaṃsaṃ, muddham assa vipātayaṃ.**

The knowledge of a fool leads only to his own ruin; it destroys his good fortune and shatters his wisdom. 愚夫求知识，反而趋灭亡，摧毁其福报，败坏其智慧。

**14(73). Asantaṃ bhāvanam iccheyya, purekkhārañca bhikkhusu;\
Āvāsesu ca issariyaṃ, pūjā parakulesu ca.**

Whoever want not peace in meditation, (would want) priority among bhikkhus, authority over monasteries (dwelling places), worship among families. 不求禅定安，僧中求為首；寺院中称雄，受他家敬奉。

**15(74). Mam eva kata maññantu, gihī pabbajitā ubho,\
Mam ev'ativasā assu, kiccākiccesu kismici,\
Iti bālassa saṅkappo, icchā māno ca vaḍḍhati.**

Let both householders and renunciates think (that) the work is done by me alone; let them be under my control in any and all matters; thus, is the fool\'s intention, and (his) desire and conceit grow. 僧与俗共知，此事由我作，事无论大小，皆由我作主，愚人作此想，贪与慢增长。

**16(75). Aññā hi lābhūpanisā, aññā nibbānagāminī,\
Evam etaṃ abhiññāya, bhikkhu Buddhassa sāvako,\
Sakkāraṃ nābhinandeyya, vivekam anubrūhaye.**

Different indeed is the basis of gain, different is (the path) leading to Nibbana. Having fully understood this, a bhikkhu, a disciple of the Buddha, should not rejoice in honor, (but) should cultivate seclusion. 一道引世利，一道向涅槃。佛弟子比丘，当如是了知，莫贪着世利，专注于远离。

**\**

### **Summary of Bāla Vagga**

Bāla Vagga contrasts the characteristics and consequences of foolish behavior with those of wisdom. Foolishness leads to prolonged suffering in the cycle of existence (samsara), while wisdom, gained through association with the wise and understanding the Dhamma, leads to liberation. It emphasizes the importance of seeking wisdom, associating with the wise, understanding the Dhamma, and acting with mindful consideration of consequences. True progress lies not in external displays or worldly gain but in inner transformation and the pursuit of liberation.

- **The Length of Samsara for Fools (Verse 60):** For those who don\'t understand the Dhamma, the cycle of rebirth (samsara) is long, just as the night is long for the sleepless and a journey is long for the weary. This sets the stage for the chapter\'s focus on the consequences of ignorance.

- **The Importance of Wise Companionship (Verse 61):** If one cannot find a companion who is better or equal to oneself in wisdom, it is better to walk alone. There is no benefit in associating with fools.

- **The Fool\'s Attachment to Possessions (Verse 62):** A fool worries about possessions and family, saying, \"I have sons; I have wealth.\" But since the fool doesn\'t even truly possess themselves (meaning they lack self-mastery and understanding of their true nature), how can they claim to possess anything else? This highlights the delusion of attachment.

- **The Fool Who Thinks Themselves Wise (Verses 63-66):** A fool who recognizes their own foolishness is at least wise to that extent. However, a fool who thinks they are wise is truly foolish. Even if a fool associates with a wise person for their entire life, they will not understand the Dhamma, just as a spoon cannot taste the soup. In contrast, even a brief association with a wise person can lead to quick understanding, like the tongue tasting the soup. It emphasizes the importance of humility and the value of wise companionship.

- **Fools as Their Own Enemies (Verse 67):** Foolish people act as their own enemies, performing evil deeds that lead to bitter consequences.

- **Regret and Joyful Consequences (Verses 68-69):** Actions that lead to regret, weeping, and suffering are not well done. Actions that are performed without regret and lead to joy and pleasure are well done. Fools consider evil deeds sweet as honey until the bitter consequences ripen. This underscores the importance of considering the long-term consequences of one\'s actions.

- **External Practices Without Understanding (Verses 70-71):** Even if a fool performs extreme austerities, they are not worth a fraction of those who understand the Dhamma. Evil deeds, though they may not bear fruit immediately, eventually follow the fool like a fire covered with ashes. This emphasizes the importance of inner understanding over mere outward practices.

- **The Destructive Nature of Foolish Knowledge (Verse 72):** The \"knowledge\" of a fool only leads to their own ruin, destroying their good fortune and shattering their wisdom. This highlights the danger of misapplied or misunderstood knowledge.

- **The Fool\'s Desires for Recognition and Control (Verses 73-75):** Fools desire recognition, leadership positions, and control over others. This desire for worldly gain and status is contrasted with the path to Nibbana, which requires detachment and seclusion. A true disciple of the Buddha understands the difference between worldly gain and Nibbana and cultivates seclusion rather than seeking honor.**\**

[]{#_Toc188087602 .anchor}**6. Paṇḍita Vagga - Wise 智**

**1(76). Nidhīnaṃ 'va pavattāraṃ, yaṃ passe vajjadassinaṃ;\
Niggayhavādiṃ medhāviṃ, tādisaṃ paṇḍitaṃ bhaje;\
Tādisaṃ bhajamānassa, seyyo hoti na pāpiyo.**

Should one meet a wise man, who, like a revealer of treasure, points out faults; let one associate with such a wise person; it will be better, not worse, for him who associates with such a one. 若见彼智者，如指示宝藏，能指示过失，当亲近如是，增益无损坏。

**2(77). Ovadeyyānusāseyya, asabbhā ca nivāraye;\
Sataṃ hi so piyo hoti, asataṃ hoti appiyo.**

He should advise and instruct (others) to restrain from bad conduct; truly he is dear to good people, and he is disliked by bad people. 训诫与教示，并制止恶行。彼为善人爱，但为恶人憎。

**3(78). Na bhaje pāpake mitte, na bhaje purisādhame;\
Bhajetha mitte kalyāṇe, bhajetha purisuttame.**

One should not associate with bad friends, one should not associate with base persons (morally low); one should associate with good friends, one should associate with superior persons. 莫与恶友交，莫友下劣者。应与善友交，应亲近贤士。

**4(79). Dhammapīti sukhaṃ seti, vippasannena cetasā;\
Ariyappavedite dhamme, sadā ramati paṇḍito.**

One who delights in the Dhamma sleeps happily, with mind pacified; in the Dhamma proclaimed by the Noble Ones, the wise person always delights. 乐法者安眠，于心清静故。智者常喜悦，圣者所说法。

**5(80). Udakañhi nayanti nettikā, usukārā namayanti tejanaṃ;\
Dāruṃ namayanti tacchakā, attānaṃ damayanti paṇḍitā.**

Irrigators lead the waters; arrow makers straighten arrows; carpenters shape the wood; the wise tame themselves. 灌溉者引水，箭匠之矫箭，木匠之绳木，智者自调御。

**6(81). Selo yathā ekaghano, vātena na samīrati;\
Evaṃ nindāpasaṃsāsu, na samiñjanti paṇḍitā.**

As a solid rock is not shaken by the wind, even so the wise are not moved by praise or blame. 犹如巨岩，不为风摇，毁谤赞誉，智者不动。

**7(82). Yathā'pi rahado gambhīro, vippasanno anāvilo;\
Evaṃ dhammāni sutvāna, vippasīdanti paṇḍitā.**

Just as a deep lake is clear and still, so on hearing the Dhamma, the wise become serene. 譬如深池，清澈无浊；如是智者，闻法清净。

**8(83). Sabbattha ve sappurisā cajanti, na kāmakāmā lapayanti santo;\
Sukhena phuṭṭhā athavā dukhena, na uccāvacaṃ paṇḍitā dassayanti.**

Everywhere indeed good people give up (attachment), the good ones do not speak driven by desires; touched by happiness or by suffering, wise people do not show fluctuations. 善人离诸欲，不论诸欲事。苦乐所不动，智者无喜忧。

**9(84). Na attahetu na parassa hetu, na puttamicche na dhanaṃ na raṭṭhaṃ; Na iccheyya adhammena samiddhim attano, sa sīlavā paññavā dhammiko siyā.**

Neither for his own sake nor for the sake of others should he desire a son, wealth, or a kingdom; he should not desire success for himself by unrighteous means; such a one is virtuous, wise, and righteous. 不因自与他，不希求子嗣、财富或国土；不以非法求成就，彼为持戒、具慧正法人。

**10(85). Appakā te manussesu, ye janā pāragāmino;\
Athāyaṃ itarā pajā, tīramevānudhāvati.**

Few are those among people, who have gone to the other shore; the rest of mankind only run about on the bank. 于此人群中，达彼岸者少。其余诸人等，徘徊于此岸。

**11(86). Ye ca kho sammadakkhāte, dhamme dhammānuvattino;\
Te janā pāramessanti, maccudheyyaṃ suduttaraṃ.**

But those who practice according to the well-expounded Dhamma will cross over this realm of Death, so difficult to overcome. 依循善说法，彼能达彼岸，度难度死界。

**12(87). Kaṇhaṃ dhammaṃ vippahāya, sukkaṃ bhāvetha paṇḍito;\
Okā anokaṃ āgamma, viveke yattha dūramaṃ.**

A wise person should abandon dark qualities and cultivate bright ones, going from home to homelessness, to solitude where joy is hard to find. 应舍弃(不善之)黑法，智者修(良善之)白法，由在家而出家，前往难觅欢乐之寂静处。

**\**

**13(88). Tatrābhiratim iccheyya, hitvā kāme akiñcano;\
Pariyodapeyya attānaṃ, cittaklesehi paṇḍito.**

There (In that solitude), having given up sensual desires and being free from possessions, let him find delight; the wise should cleanse themselves of mental impurities. 于寂静处，寻求法乐，舍弃欲乐，一无所有，智者清净，除心垢秽。

**14(89). Yesaṃ sambodhiyaṅgesu, sammā cittaṃ subhāvitaṃ;\
Ādānapaṭinissagge, anupādāya ye ratā,\
Khīṇāsavā jutīmanto, te loke parinibbutā.**

Those whose citta minds are well developed in the factors of enlightenment, who, relinquishing all grasping, delight in non-clinging, whose cankers are destroyed, radiant, they are fully liberated in this world. 于诸觉支，心善修习，舍弃执取，而生欢喜，漏尽光耀，现证涅槃。

### **Summary of Paṇḍita Vagga**

Paṇḍita Vagga focuses on the qualities and conduct of wise individuals, contrasting them with the foolish. It emphasizes the importance of associating with the wise, cultivating virtue, and striving for liberation. True wisdom lies in recognizing and abandoning negative qualities, cultivating positive ones, and striving for liberation through self-discipline and understanding of the Dhamma. Association with wise companions is crucial on this path.

- **Associating with the Wise (Verses 76-77):** One should associate with wise people who point out faults, like someone revealing hidden treasure. Such association leads to improvement, not decline. Wise individuals advise, instruct, and restrain others from wrongdoing, earning the love of the good and the dislike of the wicked.

- **Choosing Companions (Verse 78):** One should avoid associating with bad friends and morally base individuals, instead choosing good friends and superior persons. This emphasizes the influence of companionship on one\'s character and development.

- **The Wise Delight in the Dhamma (Verse 79):** Those who delight in the Dhamma find peace and happiness. Wise people constantly find joy in the Dhamma proclaimed by the Noble Ones (Ariyas).

- **Self-Taming as the Mark of the Wise (Verse 80):** Just as irrigators direct water, arrow-makers straighten arrows, and carpenters shape wood, wise people tame themselves. This highlights self-discipline and self-improvement as key characteristics of wisdom.

- **Imperturbability in the Face of Praise and Blame (Verse 81):** Like a solid rock unshaken by the wind, wise people remain unmoved by praise or blame. This emphasizes mental stability and equanimity.

- **Serenity Through the Dhamma (Verse 82):** Just as a deep lake is clear and undisturbed, wise people, upon hearing the Dhamma, become serene. This highlights the calming and purifying effect of the Dhamma.

- **Detachment and Equanimity (Verse 83):** Good people give up attachments everywhere. They don\'t speak driven by desires. Whether touched by happiness or suffering, wise people show no fluctuations in their demeanor. This emphasizes detachment and equanimity in all circumstances.

- **Righteousness and Wisdom (Verse 84):** One should not seek worldly possessions (sons, wealth, kingdom) or success through unrighteous means, neither for oneself nor for others. A truly wise person is virtuous, wise, and righteous. This emphasizes ethical conduct as an integral part of wisdom.

- **The Few Who Reach the Other Shore (Verses 85-86):** Few among humans reach the \"other shore\" (liberation). Most remain on the \"bank\" (caught in samsara). Only those who practice the well-expounded Dhamma will cross the difficult-to-traverse realm of death. This emphasizes the rarity of true liberation and the importance of diligent practice.

- **Cultivating Bright Qualities in Solitude (Verses 87-88):** Wise people abandon \"dark\" (unwholesome) qualities and cultivate \"bright\" (wholesome) ones, going from the household life to homelessness (renunciation) and seeking solitude, where joy is hard to find (meaning worldly pleasures are relinquished). There, they find delight in the Dhamma, free from possessions and cleansing themselves of mental impurities. This emphasizes the importance of renunciation and solitude for serious spiritual practice.

- **The Fully Liberated (Verse 89):** Those whose minds are well-developed in the factors of enlightenment, who delight in non-clinging by relinquishing all grasping, whose defilements (asavas) are destroyed, are radiant and fully liberated in this world. This describes the ultimate goal of the Buddhist path and the characteristics of those who have attained it.

In essence, the Paṇḍita Vagga describes the qualities of a wise person: they associate with the wise, practice self-discipline, remain unmoved by praise or blame, find joy in the Dhamma, abandon attachments, and strive for liberation. The chapter emphasizes the importance of inner transformation and the cultivation of wisdom and virtue as the path to true happiness and freedom.

[]{#_Toc188087604 .anchor}**7. Arahanta Vagga - Arahant 阿罗汉**

**1(90). Gataddhino visokassa, vippamuttassa sabbadhi;\
Sabbaganthappahīṇassa, pariḷāho na vijjati.**

For him who has completed the journey, who is sorrowless and wholly liberated, who has broken all bonds, there is no more torment. 行尽道者，离忧无恼，一切解脱，断诸系缚，无复热恼。

**2(91). Uyyuñjanti satīmanto, na nikete ramanti te;\
Haṃsā'va pallalaṃ hitvā, okam okaṃ jahanti te.**

Those who have mindfulness exert themselves; they do not delight in any abode. Like swans that leave a lake, they abandon one dwelling place after another. 具念者勤勉，不乐于住处；如鹅离池，舍弃一处又一处。

**3(92). Yesaṃ sannicayo natthi, ye pariññātabhojanā;\
Suññato animitto ca, vimokkho yassa gocaro;\
Ākāse'va sakuntānaṃ, gati tesaṃ durannayā.**

Those who do not hoard, who have understood food, whose domain of understanding (for this world) is emptiness and signlessness---their path, like that of birds in the air, cannot be traced. 彼等无积聚,已知食之义;空无相解脱,是彼所行境;如鸟飞于空,踪迹不可得。

**4(93). Yassāsavā parikkhīṇā, āhāre ca anissito;\
Suññato animitto ca, vimokkho yassa gocaro;\
Ākāse'va sakuntānaṃ, padaṃ tassa durannayaṃ.**

He whose taints are destroyed, who is not attached to food, whose domain of understanding (for this world) is emptiness and signlessness---their path, like that of birds in the air, cannot be traced. 彼之诸漏尽,亦不依于食;空无相解脱,是彼所行境;如鸟飞于空,踪迹不可得.

**5(94). Yass'indriyāni samathaṅgatāni, assā yathā sārathinā sudantā;\
Pahīnamānassa anāsavassa, devā'pi tassa pihayanti tādino.**

He whose senses are calmed, like horses well-tamed by a charioteer, who has abandoned pride and is free from defilements---even the devas admire such a one. 彼诸根寂静，如御者调马，舍我慢无漏，为天人所慕。

**\**

**6(95). Paṭhavi samo no virujjhati, indakhilupamo tādi subbato;\
Rahado'va apetakaddamo, saṃsārā na bhavanti tādino.**

He is like the earth, he is not provoked; he is like Indra\'s pillar, steadfast, virtuous; he is like a lake free from mud---for such a one samsāra does not arise. 彼如地不瞋，彼如柱坚固，彼品德高尚,如湖无污泥，是人无轮回。

**7(96). Santaṃ tassa manaṃ hoti, santā vācā ca kamma ca;\
Sammadaññā vimuttassa, upasantassa tādino.**

Calm is his mind, calm is his speech and action, who is liberated through perfect knowledge, perfectly peaceful and stable. 彼人心寂静，语与业寂静，正智解脱者，如是得安稳。

**8(97). Assaddho akataññū ca, sandhicchedo ca yo naro;\
Hatāvakāso vantāso, sa ve uttamaporiso.**

He who is without faith (i.e. direct knowledge and understanding) and ungrateful (Akataññū, not acknowledging benefits received), breaker of bonds, who has made an end of rebirth and has discarded craving---he, indeed, is the noblest of persons. 无信不感恩，断结缚再生，彼弃舍贪欲，实为最上人。

**9(98). Gāme vā yadi vāraññe, ninne vā yadi vā thale;\
Yatthārahanto viharanti, taṃ bhūmirāmaṇeyyakaṃ.**

Whether in village or in forest, in valley or on hill, wherever Arahants dwell, that place is delightful. 于村落林间，平地或丘陵，阿罗汉住处，彼地即乐处。

**10(99). Ramaṇīyāni araññāni, yattha na ramatī jano;\
Vītarāgā ramissanti, na te kāmagavesino.**

Delightful are the forests, where people find no joy; those without rāga will delight there, for they seek no sensual pleasures. 林野甚可乐, 凡夫不乐居; 离欲者乐住, 不求诸欲乐.

### **Summary of Arahanta Vagga**

Arahanta Vagga describes the characteristics of an Arahant, a fully enlightened being who has eradicated all defilements and attained liberation (Nibbana). It emphasizes the Arahant\'s freedom from suffering, attachment, and the cycle of rebirth. The Arahant is a perfected being, free from all suffering, attachment, and the cycle of rebirth. Their qualities include mindfulness, detachment, equanimity, and deep wisdom. Arahant as a being has transcended all forms of suffering and attachment through the eradication of defilements. Their qualities include mindfulness, detachment, equanimity, calmness, wisdom, and freedom. They are a source of inspiration and blessing to the world, and their presence makes any place delightful. The chapter emphasizes the ultimate goal of the Buddhist path: complete liberation from the cycle of rebirth and the attainment of perfect peace.

- **Freedom from Torment (Verse 90):** The Arahant, having completed the spiritual journey, is free from sorrow, fully liberated, and has broken all bonds. They experience no more torment or suffering.

- **Detachment from Abodes (Verse 91):** Mindful Arahants do not cling to any dwelling place. Like swans leaving a lake, they move on, unattached to any particular location. This emphasizes their detachment from worldly possessions and places.

- **Untraceable Path (Verses 92-93):** Arahants do not accumulate possessions, understand the true nature of food (i.e., they eat only to sustain the body for practice, not for pleasure), and their domain of understanding is emptiness (suññata) and signlessness (animitta). Their path, like that of birds in the sky, is untraceable, signifying their complete freedom and transcendence of worldly limitations.

- **Calmed Senses and Absence of Pride (Verse 94):** The Arahant\'s senses are calmed, like well-trained horses controlled by a charioteer. They have abandoned pride (mana) and are free from all defilements (asavas). Even the devas admire such a being.

- **Equanimity and Steadfastness (Verse 95):** The Arahant is unshaken like the earth, steadfast like Indra\'s pillar, and pure like a mud-free lake. For them, the cycle of rebirth (samsara) has ceased. This emphasizes their unwavering equanimity and stability.

- **Calmness in Thought, Speech, and Action (Verse 96):** The Arahant\'s mind, speech, and actions are calm and peaceful. They are liberated through perfect knowledge and are perfectly tranquil and stable.

- **Qualities of the Utmost Person (Verse 97):** The Arahant is without blind faith (assaddha), ungrateful (akataññū - in the sense of not clinging to past experiences or benefits), has broken all bonds (of attachment), has ended rebirth, and has discarded craving (vantaso). Such a one is the noblest of persons.

- **The Delightful Presence of Arahants (Verse 98):** Wherever Arahants dwell, whether in a village, forest, valley, or on a hill, that place becomes delightful due to their presence.

- **Joy in Solitude (Verse 99):** Forests, which ordinary people find uninviting, are delightful to Arahants, who are free from craving (raga) and do not seek sensual pleasures. This reinforces the Arahant\'s detachment from worldly attractions and their contentment in solitude.**\**

**1(100). Sahassam api ce vācā, anatthapadasaṃhitā;\
Ekaṃ atthapadaṃ seyyo, yaṃ sutvā upasammati.**

Ekaṃ gāthāpadaṃ seyyo, yaṃ sutvā upasammati.**

Better than a thousand verses comprising useless words, is a single beneficial line by hearing which one is pacified. 虽诵千句偈，若无义理者，不如一句偈，闻已得寂静。

Though one recites a hundred verses of meaningless words, better is a single true Dhamma verse by hearing which one is pacified. 彼诵百句偈，若无义理者，不如一法句，闻已得寂静。

### **Summary of Sahassa Vagga**

Sahassa Vagga emphasizes the superiority of meaningful words and actions over empty rhetoric and prolonged but unproductive existence. It highlights the importance of self-conquest, reverence for the virtuous, and the value of even a short life lived in accordance with the Dhamma.

Quality over quantity. A single meaningful word or action, especially those leading to self-improvement and understanding of the Dhamma, is far more valuable than thousands of meaningless ones or a long life lived without purpose.

- **Value of Meaningful Words (Verses 100-103):** A single beneficial word or line that brings peace and understanding is better than thousands of meaningless words or verses. Even reciting hundreds of meaningless verses is inferior to hearing a single phrase of the true Dhamma that leads to tranquility. This emphasizes the importance of substance over mere volume.

- **Superiority of Self-Conquest (Verses 104-106):** Conquering oneself is far greater than conquering thousands in battle. The self-tamed individual, always acting with restraint, is the true victor. Not even gods, celestial musicians, Mara, or Brahma can undo the victory of self-conquest. This highlights the internal nature of true victory.

- **Reverence for the Virtuous (Verses 107-109):** Even briefly venerating a person who has cultivated their mind is more meritorious than performing costly sacrifices for a hundred years. Showing respect and honor to elders and virtuous individuals brings four blessings: long life, beauty, happiness, and strength. This emphasizes the importance of respecting and learning from those who have walked the path.

- **The Value of a Virtuous Life, Even a Short One (Verses 110-115):** A single day lived with morality (sila) and meditation is better than a hundred years lived immorally and without composure. Similarly, a day lived with wisdom and meditation is better than a hundred years lived unwisely and without composure. Even a day spent making intense effort, seeing the arising and passing away of phenomena (udayabbaya), or seeing the path to the Deathless (Nibbana) or the Highest Dhamma, is more valuable than a hundred years lived without these insights. These verses emphasize the importance of living a meaningful life, even if it is short, rather than a long but unproductive one.

In summary, the Sahassa Vagga stresses that true value lies not in quantity but in quality. A single meaningful word, a single act of self-discipline, a moment of reverence for the virtuous, or even a single day lived in accordance with the Dhamma is far more valuable than empty words, external conquests, costly rituals, or a long life devoid of purpose and understanding. The chapter encourages inner transformation, reverence for wisdom, and the pursuit of liberation as the most worthwhile goals.

[]{#_Toc188087608 .anchor}**9. Pāpa Vagga -- Evil 恶**

**1(116). Abhittharetha kalyāṇe, pāpā cittaṃ nivāraye;\
Dandhañhi karoto puññaṃ, pāpasmiṃ ramatī mano.**

Be quick in doing good; restrain your mind from evil. For the mind of one who is slow in doing good finds pleasure in evil. 应赶快行善，制止心念恶。 如行善拖延，心会乐于恶。

**2(117). Pāpañce puriso kayirā, na naṃ kayirā punappunaṃ;\
Na tamhi chandaṃ kayirātha, dukkho pāpassa uccayo.**

If a person commits a wrong action, let him not repeat it; let him not delight in it; pain is the outcome of evil. 若人作恶业，莫再重复作; 莫喜于作恶，积恶则受苦。

**3(118). Puññañce puriso kayirā, kayirā naṃ punappunaṃ;\
Tamhi chandaṃ kayirātha, sukho puññassa uccayo.**

If a person performs merit, let him do it repeatedly; he should delight in it: for the accumulation of merit is blissful. 若人作善业，应当重复作; 当喜于作善，积善则受乐。

**4(119). Pāpo pi passati bhadraṃ, yāva pāpaṃ na paccati;\
Yadā ca paccati pāpaṃ, atha pāpo pāpāni passati.**

Even an evil-doer sees good as long as evil doesn't ripen; but when it bears fruit, then he sees the evil result. 恶人亦见福，乃至恶未熟；及至恶成熟，尔时方见恶。

**5(120). Bhadro'pi passati pāpaṃ, yāva bhadraṃ na paccati;\
Yadā ca paccati bhadraṃ, atha bhadro bhadrāni passati.**

Even a good man may see misfortune as long as his good deed has not ripened; but when his good deed has ripened, then the good man sees good. 善人亦见恶，乃至善未熟；及至善成熟，尔时方见善。

**6(121). Māvamaññetha pāpassa, na maṃ taṃ āgamissati; Udabindunipātena, udakumbho'pi pūrati Bālo pūrati pāpassa, thokathokam pi ācinaṃ.**

Do not think lightly of evil, saying, \'It will not come to me.\' By the falling of drops of water, even a water jar is filled; likewise, the fool, gathering it little by little, is filled with evil. 莫轻于小恶，谓我不招报，须知滴水落，亦可盈满壶，愚人积小恶，终将恶盈身。

**7(122). Māvamaññetha puññassa, na maṃ taṃ āgamissati; Udabindunipātena, udakumbho'pi pūrati; Dhīro pūrati[46](javascript:void(0)) puññassa, thokathokam pi ācinaṃ.**

Do not think lightly of good, saying, \'It will not come to me.\' By the falling of drops of water, even a water jar is filled; likewise, the wise man, gathering it little by little, is filled with good. 莫轻于小善，谓我不招报，须知滴水落，亦可盈满壶，智者积小善,终将善盈身.

**8(123). Vāṇijo'va bhayaṃ maggaṃ, appasattho mahaddhano;\
Visaṃ jīvitukāmo'va, pāpāni parivajjaye.**

As a merchant with a small escort and great wealth avoids a dangerous road, as one desiring to live avoids poison, so should one avoid evil deeds. 商人避险道，寡侣而多财; 如爱生避毒，当避诸恶行。

**9(124). Pāṇimhi ce vaṇo n'āssa, hareyya pāṇinā visaṃ;\
Nābbaṇaṃ visam anveti, natthi pāpaṃ akubbato.**

If there is no wound on one's hand, one may carry poison. Poison does not harm one who has no wound. there is no evil for one who does not do evil. 若手无疮痍，可持手中毒；无疮毒不侵，不作恶无恶。

**10(125). Yo appaduṭṭhassa narassa dussati, suddhassa posassa anaṅganassa; Tam eva bālaṃ pacceti pāpaṃ, sukhumo rajo paṭivātaṃ 'va khitto.**

If one harms a harmless man, a pure man, one who is without blemish, that evil rebounds upon that very fool, like fine dust thrown against the wind. 若人害无辜，清净无垢者；其恶还自身，如尘逆风扬。

**11(126). Gabbham eke uppajjanti, nirayaṃ pāpakammino;\
Saggaṃ sugatino yanti, parinibbanti anāsavā.**

Some are born in a womb; evil-doers are reborn in hell; the virtuous go to heaven; the taintless pass into Nibbana. 有生于母胎，作恶堕地狱；行善升天堂，无漏入涅槃。

**12(127). Na antaḷikkhe na samuddamajjhe, na pabbatānaṃ vivaraṃ pavissa; Na vijjatī so jagatippadeso, yatthaṭṭhito mucceyya pāpakammā.**

Not in the sky, nor in the midst of the sea, nor by entering into mountain caves, nowhere in the world is there a spot where one may be freed from (the consequences of) evil deeds. 非于虚空中，非于大海中，非入山窟中，无处脱恶业。

**13(128). Na antaḷikkhe na samuddamajjhe, na pabbatānaṃ vivaraṃ pavissa; Na vijjatī so jagatippadeso, yatthaṭṭhito nappasahetha maccu.**

Not in the sky, nor in the midst of the sea, nor by entering into mountain caves, nowhere in the world is there a spot where one will not be overcome by death. 非于虚空中，非于大海中，非入山窟中，无处不遇死。

### **Summary of Pāpa Vagga**

Pāpa Vagga focuses on the nature of evil actions and their consequences, emphasizing the importance of actively avoiding evil and cultivating good. It stresses the inevitability of karmic results and the impossibility of escaping the consequences of one\'s actions.

Evil actions, even small ones, accumulate and lead to suffering. One should diligently avoid evil and cultivate good, understanding that actions have consequences that cannot be escaped.

- **Promptness in Doing Good (Verse 116):** One should be quick to do good and restrain the mind from evil. Delaying good deeds allows the mind to find pleasure in evil. This emphasizes the urgency of acting virtuously.

- **Avoiding Repetition of Evil (Verse 117):** If one commits an evil act, they should not repeat it or find delight in it, as the accumulation of evil leads to suffering.

- **Repeatedly Performing Good (Verse 118):** Conversely, one should repeatedly perform good deeds and find joy in doing so, as the accumulation of merit brings happiness.

- **The Ripening of Good and Evil (Verses 119-120):** An evil-doer may experience seeming good fortune until their evil actions ripen, at which point they will experience the painful consequences. Similarly, a good person may experience seeming misfortune until their good actions ripen, bringing about positive results. This emphasizes the law of cause and effect and the delayed nature of some karmic consequences.

- **The Accumulation of Evil and Good (Verses 121-122):** Do not underestimate small evils, thinking they will not have an effect. Just as a water jar is filled drop by drop, so too does a fool become filled with evil through the accumulation of small misdeeds. Likewise, wise people become filled with good through the accumulation of small acts of merit. This illustrates the cumulative nature of karma.

- **Avoiding Evil Like a Dangerous Path or Poison (Verse 123):** One should avoid evil as carefully as a merchant with few escorts and much wealth avoids a dangerous road, or as one who values life avoids poison. This emphasizes the need for vigilance in avoiding evil.

- **The Immunity of the Harmless (Verse 124):** If one has no \"wound\" (no inclination towards evil), one can handle \"poison\" (negative influences) without being harmed. This verse suggests that those who are truly pure in heart are not affected by external negativity.

- **The Rebound of Evil (Verse 125):** Harming a harmless, pure, and innocent person results in the evil rebounding upon the perpetrator, like fine dust thrown against the wind. This emphasizes the futility and self-destructive nature of harming the innocent.

- **Destinations After Death (Verse 126):** This verse summarizes the destinations of different types of individuals after death: some are reborn in a womb (as humans or other beings), evil-doers are reborn in hell, virtuous people go to heaven, and the taintless (Arahants) attain Nibbana.

- **No Escape from Consequences (Verses 127-128):** There is no place in the world---neither in the sky, the sea, nor mountain caves---where one can escape the consequences of their evil deeds or avoid death itself. This underscores the inevitability of karmic consequences and the universality of death.

In summary, the Pāpa Vagga warns against the dangers of evil actions, even seemingly small ones, and emphasizes the importance of diligently cultivating good. It teaches that actions have inevitable consequences and that there is no escape from the law of karma. The chapter encourages vigilance, self-restraint, and the constant pursuit of virtue.

[]{#_Toc188087610 .anchor}**10. Daṇḍa Vagga -- Stick 杖**

**1(129). Sabbe tasanti daṇḍassa, sabbe bhāyanti maccuno;\
Attānaṃ upamaṃ katvā, na haneyya na ghātaye.**

All tremble at the rod. All fear death. Comparing others with oneself, one should neither kill nor cause to kill. 一切惧刀杖，一切皆畏死，以己比他人，勿杀勿教杀。

**2(130). Sabbe tasanti daṇḍassa, sabbesaṃ jīvitaṃ piyaṃ;\
Attānaṃ upamaṃ katvā, na haneyya na ghātaye.**

All tremble at the rod. Life is dear to all. Comparing others with oneself, one should neither hit nor injure others. 一切惧刀杖，一切皆爱生，以己比他人，莫杀教他杀。

**3(131). Sukhakāmāni bhūtāni, yo daṇḍena vihiṃsati;\
Attano sukham esāno, pecca so na labhate sukhaṃ.**

He who, seeking his own happiness, harms with the rod beings who also desire happiness, finds no happiness hereafter. 众生皆求乐，以杖加害者；欲求己乐者，来世不得乐。

**4(132). Sukhakāmāni bhūtāni, yo daṇḍena na hiṃsati;\
Attano sukham esāno, pecca so labhate sukhaṃ.**

He who, seeking his own happiness, does not harm with the rod beings who also desire happiness, finds happiness hereafter. 众生皆求乐，不以杖加害；欲求己乐者，来世得安乐。

**5(133). M'āvoca pharusaṃ kañci, vuttā paṭivadeyyu taṃ;\
Dukkhā hi sārambhakathā, paṭidaṇḍā phuseyyu taṃ.**

Do not speak harshly to anyone; those thus spoken to will retort. Painful indeed is revengeful talk, and retaliation will reach you. 莫语粗恶言，语者还报汝；诤论实为苦，反击将及汝。

**6(134). Sace neresi attānaṃ, kaṃso upahato yathā;\
Esa patto'si nibbānaṃ, sārambho te na vijjati.**

If you control yourself as a cracked gong (that is silent), you have indeed reached Nibbana; contention is not found in you. 若能自调伏，如一破铜锣；是已证涅槃，诤论于汝无。

**7(135). Yathā daṇḍena gopālo, gāvo pājeti gocaraṃ;\
Evaṃ jarā ca maccū ca, āyuṃ pājenti pāṇinaṃ.**

As a cowherd drives cattle to pasture with a stick, so do old age and death drive the life of beings. 如牧人用杖，驱牛至牧场；如是老与死，驱逐众生命。

**8(136). Atha pāpāni kammāni, karaṃ bālo na bujjhati;\
Sehi kammehi dummedho, aggidaḍḍho'va tappati.**

The fool, while doing evil deeds, does not realize (their consequences); by his own actions the witless one is tormented, as if burnt by fire. 愚人作恶业，为时不知觉；愚者自作业，如火焚身苦。

**9(137). Yo daṇḍena adaṇḍesu, appaduṭṭhesu dussati;\
Dasannam aññataraṃ ṭhānaṃ, khippam eva nigacchati.**

He who inflicts violence on the harmless, on those who are innocent, quickly comes to one of ten (evil) states. 以杖加无辜，害彼无嗔者；迅速堕十处，其中必一处。

**10(138). Vedanaṃ pharusaṃ jāniṃ, sarīrassa ca bhedanaṃ;\
Garukaṃ vā'pi ābādhaṃ, cittakkhepañca pāpuṇe.**

He will experience sharp pain, bodily injury, severe disease, or even mental derangement. 当受剧烈痛，及身体毁伤；或罹重疾病，并陷于狂乱。

**11(139). Rājato vā upasaggaṃ, abbhakkhānañca dāruṇaṃ;\
Parikkhayañca ñātinaṃ, bhogānañca pabhaṅguraṃ.**

He may incur trouble from the king, terrible false accusations, loss of relatives, or destruction of wealth. 或为王迫害，或被诬重罪，或亲族衰亡，或财产破灭。

**12(140). Atha v'assa agārāni, aggi ḍahati pāvako;\
Kāyassa bhedā duppañño, nirayaṃ so'papajjati.**

His houses may be burned down by fire, and at the dissolution of the body, that unwise one goes to hell. 或彼舍宅舍，为火所焚烧；身坏愚顽者，死后堕地狱。

**13(141). Na naggacariyā na jaṭā na paṅkā, nānāsakā thaṇḍilasāyikā vā; Rajo jallaṃ ukkuṭikappadhānaṃ, sodhenti maccaṃ avitiṇṇakaṅkhaṃ.**

Not going naked, not matted hair, not mud, not fasting, nor lying on the bare ground, not dust and dirt, nor constantly squatting, can purify a mortal who has not overcome doubt. 非裸行结发，非涂泥绝食，非卧地自尘，非蹲踞能净人，若未度疑惑。

**14(142). Alaṅkato ce'pi samaṃ careyya, santo danto niyato brahmacārī;\
Sabbesu bhūtesu nidhāya daṇḍaṃ, so brāhmaṇo so samaṇo sa bhikkhu.**

Even though adorned (decorated with ornaments or finery), if he lives righteously, peaceful, controlled, restrained, leading a holy life, having laid aside violence towards all beings---he is a brāhmaṇa, he is a samaṇa, he is a bhikkhu. 纵使身庄严，若能行正道，寂静调伏制，梵行离诸害，彼即是梵人，彼即是沙门，彼即真比丘。

**15(143). Hirīnisedho puriso, koci lokasmiṃ vijjati;\
Yo niddaṃ apabodheti, asso bhadro kasām iva.**

Rare indeed is the man who is restrained by moral shame (conscience). Like a well-trained horse touched by the whip, he rouses himself (from sleep). 惭愧制伏人，世间实难得；能醒睡眠者，如良马鞭策。

**16(144). Asso yathā bhadro kasāniviṭṭho, Ātāpino saṃvegino bhavātha;\
Saddhāya sīlena ca vīriyena ca, samādhinā dhammavinicchayena ca;\**
**Sampannavijjācaraṇā patissatā, jahissatha dukkham idaṃ anappakaṃ.**

Just as a good horse touched by the whip (is responsive), be ye ardent and earnest. By faith (saddha), by morality (sīla), by effort (vīriya), by concentration (samādhi), by investigation of the Dhamma, endowed with knowledge and conduct (sampanna vijjā cara), mindful (sati), you will overcome this great dukkha. 如良马鞭策，汝等当奋勉，以信戒精进，以及三摩地，善分别正法，明行足及念，当灭此大苦。

**17(145). Udakañhi nayanti nettikā, usukārā namayanti tejanaṃ;\
Dāruṃ namayanti tacchakā, attānaṃ damayanti subbatā.**

Irrigators lead the water; arrow-makers shape the arrows; carpenters shape the wood; the disciplined ones (those who have taken good vows) tame themselves. 水工导水流，箭匠调箭直，木匠绳木材，修善者自御。

### **Summary of Daṇḍa Vagga**

Daṇḍa Vagga emphasizes non-violence, both physical and verbal, and the importance of self-control. It explores the consequences of harming others and the benefits of living a peaceful and virtuous life. Violence, in any form, leads to suffering, both for the perpetrator and the victim. True happiness comes from self-control, harmlessness, and living according to the Dhamma.

- **Universal Fear of Violence (Verses 129-130):** All beings fear punishment and death. By understanding this shared experience, one should neither kill nor harm others in any way. This establishes the fundamental principle of non-violence.

- **Consequences of Harming Others (Verses 131-132):** Those who seek their own happiness by harming others with violence will not find happiness in the future. Conversely, those who seek their own happiness by refraining from harming others will find happiness in the future. This highlights the karmic consequences of violence.

- **The Pain of Harsh Speech (Verse 133):** Avoid harsh speech, as it provokes retaliation and leads to painful disputes. This extends the concept of non-violence to verbal communication.

- **Self-Control as Nibbana (Verse 134):** Controlling oneself, like a silent, cracked gong, is equivalent to reaching Nibbana, where there is no contention or conflict. This links self-mastery to liberation.

- **Life Driven by Aging and Death (Verse 135):** Just as a cowherd drives cattle with a stick, old age and death drive the lives of all beings. This highlights the impermanent nature of life.

- **The Fool\'s Ignorance of Consequences (Verse 136):** Fools do not understand the consequences of their evil actions and suffer the torment of their own deeds, like being burned by fire.

- **Repercussions of Harming the Harmless (Verses 137-140):** Harming the harmless and innocent leads to ten possible negative consequences: sharp pain, bodily injury, severe illness, mental derangement, trouble with authorities, false accusations, loss of relatives, destruction of wealth, house fires, and ultimately, rebirth in hell. This emphasizes the severe karmic repercussions of violence against the innocent.

- **True Purification (Verse 141):** External practices like nakedness, matted hair, covering oneself with mud, fasting, lying on the bare ground, or constant squatting do not purify one who has not overcome doubt. True purification comes from inner transformation.

- **The Truly Holy Person (Verse 142):** Even if adorned with finery, one who lives righteously, peacefully, with self-control and restraint, practicing the holy life and abstaining from violence towards all beings, is a true Brahmana (holy person), Samana (ascetic), and Bhikkhu (monk). This emphasizes that true holiness is based on inner qualities and conduct, not outward appearances.

- **The Power of Moral Shame (Verse 143):** Rare is the person restrained by moral shame (conscience). Such a person, like a well-trained horse responding to the whip, is quick to correct their behavior.

- **The Path to Overcoming Suffering (Verse 144):** One should be ardent and earnest, developing faith (saddha), morality (sila), effort (viriya), concentration (samadhi), and the investigation of the Dhamma (dhammavinicchaya), along with knowledge (vijja), conduct (carana), and mindfulness (sati), to overcome suffering (dukkha). This verse summarizes the key elements of the Buddhist path.

- **Self-Taming as the Highest Discipline (Verse 145):** Just as irrigators direct water, arrow-makers straighten arrows, and carpenters shape wood, those who have taken good vows (subrata) tame themselves. This reinforces the central theme of self-mastery.

In summary, the Daṇḍa Vagga strongly advocates for non-violence in all its forms and emphasizes the importance of self-control, ethical conduct, and the pursuit of inner peace. It details the negative consequences of violence and the positive results of living a virtuous life according to the Dhamma.

[]{#_Toc188087612 .anchor}**11. Jarā Vagga - Old Age 老**

**1(146). Ko nu hāso kimānando, niccaṃ pajjalite sati;\
Andhakārena onaddhā, padīpaṃ na gavesatha.**

What is laughter, what is joy, when the world is constantly burning? Shrouded by darkness, would you not seek a lamp (of wisdom)? 有何可笑？ 有何可乐？常在燃烧中；被黑暗覆盖，却不求明灯。

**2(147). Passa cittakataṃ bimbaṃ, arukāyaṃ samussitaṃ;\
Āturaṃ bahusaṅkappaṃ, yassa natthi dhuvaṃ ṭhiti.**

Behold this form (body), made by the mind, prone to disease, filled with restless thoughts and emotions, of which there is no lasting stability. 看这心造之像，病弱之躯； 有诸多思绪，无恒常之住。

**3(148). Parijiṇṇam idaṃ rūpaṃ, roganīḷaṃ pabhaṅguraṃ;\
Bhijjati pūtisandeho, maraṇantañhi jīvitaṃ.**

This body is worn out, a nest of diseases, fragile; this foul-smelling body breaks apart, for indeed death is the end of life. 此身已衰朽，疾病之巢窟，腐败之身躯，终归于死亡。

**4(149). Yānimāni apatthān, alābūn'eva sārade;\
Kāpotakāni aṭṭhīni, tāni disvāna kā rati.**

Whatever these (remains) are, cast away like gourds in autumn; dove-colored bones---having seen those, what delight (is there)? 此被弃之物,如秋季之瓜,鸽色之骨骸,见此有何乐？

**5(150). Aṭṭhīnaṃ nagaraṃ kataṃ, maṃsalohitalepanaṃ;\
Yattha jarā ca maccū ca, māno makkho ca ohito.**

A city is built of bones, plastered with flesh and blood; where old age and death, conceit and contempt are deposited. 以骨为城郭，血肉敷其上； 老死与骄慢，尽皆藏其中。

**6(151). Jīranti ve rājarathā sucittā, atho sarīram pi jaraṃ upeti;\
Satañca dhammo na jaraṃ upeti, santo have sabbhi pavedayanti.**

Royal chariots, though beautifully adorned, indeed wear out; So too the body undergoes aging. But the Dhamma of the virtuous does not undergo aging; the virtuous ones indeed teach it among themselves (passed on by the virtuous ones). 华丽的王辇会损坏，身体也会衰老；然而善者的法不会衰老，善者们确实在彼此间传扬。

**7(152). Appassutāyaṃ puriso, balibaddo'va jīrati;\
Maṃsāni tassa vaḍḍhanti, paññā tassa na vaḍḍhati.**

A person who is unlearned grows old like an ox; his flesh increases, but his wisdom does not increase. 不学之人如牛老；其肉增长慧不增。

**8(153). Anekajāti saṃsāraṃ, sandhāvissaṃ anibbisaṃ;\
Gahakāraṃ gavesanto: dukkhā jāti punappunaṃ.**

Through many births in saṃsāra I wandered, not finding the house-builder. Painful is birth again and again. 轮回无数次，漂泊无止境；不见造屋者，轮回皆是苦。

**9(154). Gahakāraka diṭṭho'si, puna gehaṃ na kāhasi;\
Sabbā te phāsukā bhaggā, gahakūṭaṃ visaṅkhataṃ;\
Visaṅkhāragataṃ cittaṃ, taṇhānaṃ khayam ajjhagā.**

O house-builder, you are seen! You will not build this house again. All your rafters are broken, your roof-ridge is destroyed. The mind (citta), having reached the Unconditioned, has attained the destruction of craving. 造屋者，我已见你！你将无法再造屋；你所有的椽子都已折断，屋脊已摧毁；心已经离行，已止息渴爱。

**10(155). Acaritvā brahmacariyaṃ, aladdhā yobbane dhanaṃ;\
Jiṇṇakoñcā'va jhāyanti, khīṇamacche'va pallale.**

Not having practiced brahmacariya, not having acquired wealth in their youth, they waste away like old herons in a fishless pond. 少不修梵行，少时未积财，如老鹭枯守，鱼尽之池塘。

**11(156). Acaritvā brahmacariyaṃ, aladdhā yobbane dhanaṃ;\
Senti cāpātikhīnā'va, purāṇāni anutthunaṃ.**

Not having practiced brahmacariya, not having acquired wealth in their youth, they lie down like worn-out bows, lamenting over past things. 少不修梵行，少时未积财，如废弃弓箭,躺卧叹往昔。

### **Summary of Jarā Vagga**

Jarā Vagga reflects on the inevitability of aging, decay, and death, urging reflection on the impermanent nature of existence and the importance of seeking liberation. The body is inherently subject to decay and death. True and lasting happiness cannot be found in clinging to the physical form. Instead, one should seek the \"lamp of wisdom\" and strive for liberation from the cycle of rebirth. It uses the imagery of aging and decay to impress upon the reader the impermanent nature of the physical world and the importance of seeking true and lasting happiness through the practice of the Dhamma. It emphasizes the need to cultivate wisdom and abandon attachment to the body, urging listeners to make the most of their lives by pursuing spiritual liberation.

- **The Burning World and the Need for Wisdom (Verse 146):** In a world constantly consumed by suffering and impermanence, laughter and worldly joys are meaningless. The focus should be on seeking the \"lamp of wisdom\" to escape the darkness of ignorance.

- **The Impermanent and Diseased Body (Verses 147-148):** The body, created by the mind, is prone to disease, filled with restless thoughts, and lacks lasting stability. It is worn out, fragile, and ultimately breaks apart, as death is the inevitable end of life.

- **The Worthlessness of the Decayed Body (Verse 149):** The remains of the body are like gourds discarded in autumn, or like dove-colored bones. There is no lasting delight or value to be found in clinging to this decaying form.

- **The City of Bones (Verse 150):** The body is described as a \"city built of bones, plastered with flesh and blood,\" a place where old age, death, conceit, and contempt reside. This highlights the inherent imperfections and vulnerabilities of the physical form.

- **The Undying Dhamma (Verse 151):** While even beautifully adorned royal chariots and the body itself are subject to decay, the Dhamma of the virtuous does not age. It is passed down and preserved by the wise. This contrasts the impermanence of the physical world with the timeless nature of the Dhamma.

- **The Unlearned Grow Old Like Oxen (Verse 152):** Those who lack learning and wisdom grow old like oxen, their physical bodies increasing in size but their wisdom remaining stagnant. This emphasizes the importance of cultivating wisdom alongside physical development.

- **The Search for the House-Builder (Verses 153-154):** For countless lifetimes, the mind (citta) has wandered through saṃsāra, unknowingly trapped in the cycle of birth and death. Searching for the house-builder---the force constructing each new body (geham)---it has suffered again and again. At last, the house-builder (gahakāraka), the mind itself, is seen and understood. With this realization, the conditioned cycle is broken---the framework of craving and attachment that sustained rebirth is dismantled. The foundation of existence is shattered. Purified and freed from all conditioning (visaṅkhāra), the mind no longer constructs another body. With craving uprooted, it enters the Unconditioned and attains ultimate liberation, never to reenter the world of birth and death.

- **The Consequences of Not Practicing in Youth (Verses 155-156):** Those who fail to practice the holy life (brahmacariya) and acquire \"wealth\" (spiritual merit) in their youth waste away in old age, like old herons in a fishless pond or like worn-out bows lamenting the past. This emphasizes the importance of utilizing one\'s youth for spiritual practice and accumulating merit.**\**

[]{#_Toc188087614 .anchor}**12. Atta Vagga - Self 我**

**1(157). Attānañce piyaṃ jaññā, rakkheyya naṃ surakkhitaṃ;\
Tiṇṇam aññataraṃ yāmaṃ, paṭijaggeyya paṇḍito.**

If one holds one's soul dear, one should protect it well. At least in one of the three ages of life, a wise man should be vigilant. 若珍惜自我，当善加守护；人生三阶段，至少其中一，智者应警觉。

**Comment: The Three Ages (Tiṇṇam Yāmaṃ)**

In ancient India, the human lifespan is traditionally divided into three distinct stages, each roughly thirty to thirty-five years. The text suggests that the **paṇḍito** (wise man) must be vigilant in these periods to protect the soul:

1.  **First Age (Youth):** Characterized by physical vigor and the accumulation of knowledge. Vigilance here prevents the soul from being led astray by sensory distractions before it is firmly established in **Sīla**.

2.  **Second Age (Adulthood):** The period of family, professional responsibility, and \"worldly\" engagement. Vigilance is required here to ensure that the pursuit of wealth or status does not cause one to neglect **Brahmacariya**.

3.  **Third Age (Old Age):** The final threshold before the dissolution of the body. Vigilance here is critical for the **Citta** to remain **Sugato** (directed to wholesomeness) as it prepares for the transition.

**The Meaning of \"At Least\" (Aññataraṃ)**

The term **aññataraṃ** (one of the three) implies a hierarchy of effort based on a person\'s past actions and current circumstances:

- **The Ideal:** Ideally, a person is vigilant throughout all three ages.

- **The Minimum Requirement:** If one has lived a life of \$rāga\$, \$dosa\$, and \$moha\$ during youth and adulthood, it is not \"too late.\" The text emphasizes that one must wake up and be vigilant **at least** in the final stage.

- **The Warning:** It serves as a pragmatic warning. If a person neglects the soul in all three stages, there is no \"gateway\" to liberation. They remain bound to the involuntary cycles of death and \$dukkha\$ because they failed to guard the soul when they had the capacity.

The \"protection\" (\$rakkha\$) mentioned is the active guarding of the soul from falling into unwholesome states. This vigilance (\$paṭijaggeyya\$) ensures that the mind does not cling to what is worldly (\$loka\$) and instead prepares for transcendence.

**2(158). Attānam eva paṭhamaṃ, patīrūpe nivesaye;\
Ath'aññam anusāseyya, na kilisseyya paṇḍito.**

Let one first establish oneself in a proper state, and then one may instruct others. Such a wise man will not be defiled. 应先将自己，安立于正道； 而后方教他，智者不染污。

**3(159). Attānaṃ ce tathā kayirā, yathaññamanusāsati;\
Sudanto vata dametha, attā hi kira duddamo.**

If one should act towards oneself in the same way that one instructs others, then, being well-tamed, one would truly tame (others), for oneself is indeed difficult to tame, it is said. 若自己行事，如其教人般，善调伏自己，方能调他人，自己实难调。

**4(160). Attā hi attano nātho, ko hi nātho paro siyā;\
Attanā'va sudantena, nāthaṃ labhati dullabhaṃ.**

The self is indeed one\'s own master/refuge; who else could be one\'s refuge? Through self alone, when well-tamed, one obtains a refuge that is difficult to obtain. 我是自己的庇护，谁又是他人的庇护？ 唯有自我善调伏，方得难得之庇护。

**5(161). Attanā hi kataṃ pāpaṃ, attajaṃ attasambhavaṃ;\
Abhimatthati dummedhaṃ, vajiraṃ v'asmamayaṃ maṇiṃ.**

Indeed, evil done by oneself, born of oneself, originated from oneself, crushes the foolish one, just as a diamond crushes a stone gem. 恶业实由自己造，自己所生自己起；将摧毁愚者，如钻石碎珠。

**6(162). Yassa accantadussīlyaṃ, māluvā sālam iv'otataṃ;\
Karoti so tathattānaṃ, yathā naṃ icchatī diso.**

Whose misconduct is extreme, like a creeper entwining a Sal tree, that person does to himself just what his enemy would wish. 若人极行恶，如蔓草缠树；其人自作是，正中仇敌怀。

**7(163). Sukarāni asādhūni, attano ahitāni ca;\
Yaṃ 've hitañca sādhuñca, taṃ ve paramadukkaraṃ.**

Easy to do are bad deeds, and things harmful to oneself; but truly, that which is beneficial and good is extremely difficult to do. 恶事易作，且不利己；善行益事，实极难为。

**8(164). Yo sāsanaṃ arahataṃ, ariyānaṃ dhammajīvinaṃ;\
Paṭikkosati dummedho, diṭṭhiṃ nissāya pāpikaṃ;\
Phalāni kaṭṭhakass'eva, attaghātāya phallati.**

Whoever rejects the teaching of the Arahats, of the noble ones who live by the Dhamma, relying on an evil view, that foolish one---just like the fruits of the poisonous tree---it results in self-harm and ruin. 若人舍弃阿罗汉，及诸圣者正法教；愚者依止邪恶见，其果犹如毒树果，反害自身趋灭亡。

**9(165). Attanā hi kataṃ pāpaṃ, attanā saṃkilissati;\
Attanā akataṃ pāpaṃ, attanā'va visujjhati;\
Suddhi asuddhi paccattaṃ, n'āñño aññaṃ visodhaye.**

By oneself is evil done; by oneself is one defiled. By oneself is evil undone; by oneself is one purified. Purity and impurity depend on oneself. No one purifies another. 恶由自己作，亦染污自己；不作恶亦然，自己得清净；净与不净唯自己，无人能清净他人。

**10(166). Attadatthaṃ paratthena, bahunā'pi na hāpaye;\
Attadattham abhiññāya, sadatthapasuto siyā.**

One should not neglect one\'s own welfare for the sake of others, however important is that. Having understood one\'s own welfare, one should be devoted to one\'s own (true) purpose. 不应为他人，纵多舍己利； 既已明己利，当致力己义。

### **Summary of Atta Vagga**

Atta Vagga emphasizes the importance of self-mastery, self-reliance, and personal responsibility for one\'s own purification and liberation. It stresses that one\'s actions, whether good or bad, have direct consequences for oneself. The self is one\'s own master and refuge. By diligently guarding and training oneself, one paves the path to liberation. Conversely, neglecting self-discipline leads to self-harm and suffering.

- **Protecting Oneself (Verse 157):** If one values oneself, one should diligently protect oneself, maintaining vigilance throughout life. This emphasizes self-care and mindfulness.

- **Establishing Oneself First (Verse 158):** Before instructing others, one should first establish oneself in a proper state (of virtue and wisdom). This ensures that one\'s teachings are authentic and effective, and prevents hypocrisy.

- **Practicing What One Preaches (Verse 159):** One should act towards oneself in the same way one advises others. This reinforces the importance of integrity and consistency between one\'s words and actions. It also highlights the difficulty of self-mastery.

- **Self as One\'s Own Refuge (Verse 160):** The self is one\'s own refuge; no one else can ultimately provide true refuge. Through self-discipline and self-taming, one attains a rare and precious refuge (liberation). This emphasizes self-reliance on the path to enlightenment.

- **Self-Caused Harm (Verse 161):** Evil actions, originating from oneself, crush the foolish person like a diamond crushing a stone. This highlights the self-destructive nature of evil deeds.

- **Extreme Misconduct and Self-Destruction (Verse 162):** Extreme misconduct, like a creeper entangling a sal tree, brings about the very harm that one\'s enemies would wish upon them. This emphasizes that one\'s own actions are the greatest source of harm.

- **The Difficulty of Good Deeds (Verse 163):** Bad deeds, which harm oneself, are easy to do, while good and beneficial actions are extremely difficult. This acknowledges the challenge of cultivating virtue.

- **Rejecting the Teachings of the Wise (Verse 164):** Rejecting the teachings of the Arahants (enlightened beings) and the Noble Ones who live according to the Dhamma, due to wrong views, leads to self-harm and ruin, like consuming the fruit of a poisonous tree. This emphasizes the importance of respecting and following wise guidance.

- **Self-Purification (Verse 165):** One is defiled by one\'s own evil actions and purified by one\'s own good actions. Purity and impurity are solely dependent on oneself; no one can purify another. This reinforces personal responsibility for one\'s spiritual development.

- **Prioritizing One\'s Own Welfare (Verse 166):** One should not neglect one\'s own spiritual welfare for the sake of others, no matter how important they may seem. Having understood one\'s own true welfare (liberation), one should dedicate oneself to achieving it. This emphasizes the importance of prioritizing one\'s own spiritual path while still acting with compassion and kindness.

In summary, the Atta Vagga emphasizes the crucial role of self-mastery and self-reliance in the Buddhist path. It teaches that one\'s actions have direct consequences for oneself and that true refuge is found through self-discipline and the cultivation of wisdom and virtue. The chapter encourages personal responsibility for one\'s own purification and liberation.

[]{#_Toc188087616 .anchor}**13. Loka Vagga - World 世界**

**1(167). Hīnaṃ dhammaṃ na seveyya, pamādena na saṃvase;\
Micchādiṭṭhiṃ na seveyya, na siyā lokavaḍḍhano.**

One should not associate with inferior teachings; one should not connect with zealless. One should not embrace wrong views; one should not be one who increases (attachment to) the world. 不应亲近劣法，不与冷淡为伍；不应信受邪见，不应增世间欲。

**2(168). Uttiṭṭhe nappamajjeyya, dhammaṃ sucaritaṃ care;\
Dhammacārī sukhaṃ seti, asmiṃ loke paramhi ca.**

One should strive and not be negligent; one should practice the Dhamma well. The one who lives according to the Dhamma lives happily in this world and in the next. 奋起勿放逸，应善行正法； 奉法者安乐，此世及来世。

**3(169). Dhammaṃ care sucaritaṃ, na naṃ duccaritaṃ care;\
Dhammacārī sukhaṃ seti, asmiṃ loke paramhi ca.**

One should practice the Dhamma well, one should not practice it badly. The one who lives according to the Dhamma lives happily in this world and in the next. 应善行正法，不应行恶法；奉法者安乐，此世及来世。

**4(170). Yathā pubbuḷakaṃ passe, yathā passe marīcikaṃ;\
Evaṃ lokaṃ avekkhantaṃ, maccurājā na passati.**

Just as one should see a bubble, just as one should see a mirage, so one who observes the world thus---the king of death does not see him. 应看如水泡，应看如幻影；如是看世界，死王不得见。

**5(171). Etha passath'imaṃ lokaṃ, cittaṃ rājarathūpamaṃ;\
Yattha bālā visīdanti, natthi saṅgo vijānataṃ.**

Come, see this world, the mind which is like a royal chariot, where fools become attached; there is no attachment for those who know. 来看此世间，其心如王辇；愚者溺其中，智者无执着。

**6(172). Yo ca pubbe pamajjitvā, pacchā so nappamajjati;\
So imaṃ lokaṃ pabhāseti, abbhā mutto'va candimā.**

Whoever, having previously been negligent, afterwards is not negligent, he illuminates this world, just as the moon is freed from the clouds. 若人曾放逸,后不再放逸,彼照耀世间,如云开月明。

**7(173). Yassa pāpaṃ kataṃ kammaṃ, kusalena pidhīyati;\
So imaṃ lokaṃ pabhāseti, abbhā mutto'va candimā.**

Whose bad deed that has been done is covered by good deeds, he illuminates this world, just as the moon is freed from the clouds. 若昔造恶业,后善行遮蔽;彼照耀世间,如云开月明。

**8(174). Andhabhūto ayaṃ loko, tanuk'ettha vipassati;\
Sakuṇo jālamutto'va, appo saggāya gacchati.**

This world is blinded (ignorance); few here see clearly. Like a bird released from a net, few go to heavenly realm (attain liberation). 此世盲昧，鲜少明见；如鸟脱网，生天者稀。

**9(175). Haṃsādiccapathe yanti, ākāse yanti iddhiyā;\
Nīyanti dhīrā lokamhā, jetvā māraṃ savāhiniṃ.**

Swans go on the path of the sun; they go in the sky by means of psychic power. The wise are led away from the world, having conquered Māra with his forces. 鸿鹄翱翔日光道，亦以神通游虚空；智者离世界而去，战胜魔王及其军。

**10(176). Ekaṃ dhammaṃ atītassa, musāvādissa jantuno;\
Vitiṇṇaparalokassa, natthi pāpaṃ akāriyaṃ.**

A liar who has transgressed the one truth (next life), and who has rejected the other world, there is no evil they cannot do. 若人违犯一真谛，妄语虚诳舍来世，无恶彼所不能作。

**11(177). Na ve kadariyā devalokaṃ vajanti, bālā have nappasaṃsanti dānaṃ; Dhīro ca dānaṃ anumodamāno, ten'eva so hoti sukhī parattha.**

Misers do not go to the realms of the devas; fools indeed do not praise generosity. But the wise one, rejoicing in giving, and thus becomes happy in the next world. 悭吝者不升天界，愚人亦不赞布施；智者随喜行布施，来世因此得安乐。

**12(178). Pathabyā ekarajjena, saggassa gamanena vā;\
Sabbalokādhipaccena, sotāpattiphalaṃ varaṃ.**

By (ruling) the whole earth as sole king, or by going to heaven, or by sovereignty over the entire world, the fruit of sotāpanna (stream-entry) is better. 一统大地为王，或者往生天界，或统治诸世间，皆不及入流果。

### **Summary of Loka Vagga**

Loka Vagga reflects on the nature of the world, emphasizing its impermanence, the dangers of attachment, and the importance of living according to the Dhamma to attain liberation. The world is characterized by impermanence and suffering. True happiness lies not in worldly pursuits but in practicing the Dhamma, overcoming attachment, and attaining liberation. It reflects on the impermanent and unsatisfactory nature of the world. It emphasizes the importance of right understanding, righteous conduct, and the value of spiritual attainment over worldly achievements.

- **Avoiding Inferior Teachings and Wrong Views (Verse 167):** One should avoid associating with inferior teachings, negligence, and wrong views. One should not increase attachment to the world. This emphasizes the importance of right understanding and avoiding paths that lead to further entanglement in samsara.

- **Striving and Practicing the Dhamma (Verses 168-169):** One should strive diligently and practice the Dhamma well. Those who live according to the Dhamma experience happiness in this world and the next. This highlights the importance of effort and righteous conduct.

- **Seeing the World as Impermanent (Verse 170):** One should view the world as impermanent, like a bubble or a mirage. By seeing the world in this way, one is not ensnared by its illusions and thus escapes the notice of the \"king of death\" (Mara).

- **Attachment and Wisdom (Verse 171):** This world, with its mind likened to a royal chariot (a symbol of worldly power and allure), is a place where fools become attached. Those who possess wisdom, however, are free from such attachments. This emphasizes the contrast between attachment and wisdom.

- **Overcoming Negligence (Verses 172-173):** One who overcomes past negligence and practices diligently illuminates the world, just as the moon emerges from behind the clouds. Similarly, one who covers past misdeeds with good deeds also illuminates the world. These verses highlight the possibility of redemption and the power of positive actions.

- **The Rarity of True Vision (Verse 174):** This world is blinded by ignorance. Few see clearly (with wisdom). Like a bird escaping a net, few attain liberation (go to heavenly realms, here used as a metaphor for liberation). This emphasizes the difficulty of attaining true insight.

- **The Path of the Wise (Verse 175):** The wise, like swans flying on the path of the sun (a metaphor for spiritual progress) or using psychic powers to traverse the sky, are led away from the world, having conquered Mara (the personification of temptation and death) and his forces (defilements). This describes the path and victory of those who attain liberation.

- **The Consequences of Transgressing Truth (Verse 176):** For a liar who has transgressed the one law (truthfulness) and rejected the next world (spiritual values), there is no evil they are incapable of committing. This emphasizes the importance of truthfulness and moral integrity.

- **The Value of Generosity (Verse 177):** Misers do not attain heavenly realms, and fools do not praise generosity. Wise individuals, however, rejoice in giving and thus find happiness in the next world. This highlights the importance of generosity as a virtue.

- **The Superiority of Stream-Entry (Verse 178):** Even ruling the entire earth, going to heaven, or having sovereignty over the whole world is not as valuable as attaining the fruit of stream-entry (sotāpatti), the first stage of enlightenment. This emphasizes the supreme value of spiritual attainment over worldly achievements.**\**

[]{#_Toc188087618 .anchor}**14. Buddha Vagga - Buddha 佛陀**

**1(179). Yassa jitaṃ n'āvajīyati, jitaṃ yassa no yāti koci loke;\
Taṃ Buddham anantagocaraṃ, apadaṃ kena padena nessatha.**

Whose victory (over defilements) is absolute and irreversible, whose victory no one in the world can overturn; that Buddha of limitless range (has transcended all limitations), is trackless, how will you trace him by which way (conventional)? 彼之胜已无复败，其胜世间无人及；如是无限境界佛，当以何道而追踪？

**2(180). Yassa jālinī visattikā, taṇhā natthi kuhiñci netave;\
Taṃ Buddham anantagocaraṃ, apadaṃ kena padena nessatha.**

For whom the clinging net of craving does not exist to lead (him) anywhere (in this world), that Buddha of limitless range, is trackless, how will you trace him by which way (conventional)? 彼已断除缠缚网，贪欲不复引彼往；如是无限境界佛，当以何道而追踪？

**3(181). Ye jhānapasutā dhīrā, nekkhammūpasame ratā;\
Devā'pi tesaṃ pihayanti, sambuddhānaṃ satīmataṃ.**

Those wise ones who are devoted to jhāna and delighted in the peace of renunciation, even the devas envy them, such mindful Fully Enlightened Buddhas. 彼等禅修精进者，乐于出离寂静乐；诸天亦皆羡慕彼，正觉具念诸贤圣。

**4(182). Kiccho manussapaṭilābho, kicchaṃ macchāna jīvitaṃ;\
Kicchaṃ saddhammasavanaṃ, kiccho buddhānaṃ uppādo.**

Difficult is becoming a manussa; difficult is the life of mortals; difficult is hearing the true Dhamma; difficult is the appearance of Buddhas. 生为人类艰难，凡人生活艰难；闻正法难，佛出现难。

**5(183). Sabbapāpassa akaraṇaṃ, kusalassa upasampadā;\
Sacittapariyodapanaṃ, etaṃ buddhāna sāsanaṃ.**

Not to do any evil, to cultivate good, to purify one's mind, this is the teaching of the Buddhas. 诸恶莫作，众善奉行，自净其意，是诸佛教。

**\**

**6(184). Khantī paramaṃ tapo titikkhā, nibbānaṃ paramaṃ vadanti buddhā; Na pabbajito parūpaghātī, samaṇo hoti paraṃ viheṭhayanto.**

Endurance is the highest tapas practice of self-control, Nibbana is the supreme, say the Buddhas. One who harms others is not a renunciate of worldly life; one who torments others is not a samana. 忍耐为最上之苦行，涅槃为最上，诸佛如是说；害人不名为出家，恼他不名为沙门。

**7(185). Anūpavādo anūpaghāto, pātimokkhe ca saṃvaro;\
Mattaññutā ca bhattasmiṃ, pantañca sayanāsanaṃ;\
Adhicitte ca āyogo, etaṃ buddhāna sāsanaṃ.**

Not insulting, not harming, restraint according to the Patimokkha, and moderation in food, and secluded dwelling, and dedication to higher mind citta --- this is the teaching of the Buddhas. 不毁谤，不伤害，严持戒律仪，饮食知节量，安住寂静处，勤修增上心，是诸佛教诲。

**8(186). Na kahāpaṇavassena, titti kāmesu vijjati;\
Appassādā dukhā kāmā, iti viññāya paṇḍito.**

Not by a shower of gold coins is satisfaction found in sensual pleasures. Of little enjoyment, painful are sensual pleasures---thus having understood, is the wise person. 纵降金钱雨，欲乐无厌足；欲乐少味苦，智者如是知。

**9(187). Api dibbesu kāmesu, ratiṃ so n' ādhigacchati;\
Taṇhakkhayarato hoti, sammāsambuddhasāvako.**

Even in divine/heavenly pleasures, he does not find delight; he delights in the destruction of craving; (he is) a disciple of the Fully Enlightened Buddha. 纵使天界诸妙乐，彼亦不生丝毫喜；唯乐于灭尽渴爱，乃真正等觉弟子。

**10(188). Bahuṃ ve saraṇaṃ yanti pabbatāni vanāni ca;\
Ārāmarukkhacetyāni, manussā bhayatajjitā.**

Many, indeed, go for refuge to mountains and forests and parks, trees (in parks), and shrines---people afflicted by fear. 畏惧所逼之人，多归依山林树木，园林树祠等处，寻求庇护。

**11(189). N'etaṃ kho saraṇaṃ khemaṃ, n'etaṃ saraṇam uttamaṃ;\
N'etaṃ saraṇam āgamma, sabbadukkhā pamuccati.**

This,indeed,is not a safe refuge; this is not an ultimate refuge. Having gone to this refuge, one is not liberated from all suffering. 彼非安稳归依处,彼非最上归依处;皈依彼等不脱苦.

**12(190). Yo ca buddhañca dhammañca, saṅghañca saraṇaṃ gato;\
Cattāri ariyasaccāni, sammappaññāya passati.**

Whoever has gone for refuge to the Buddha, the Dhamma, and the Sangha, sees the Four Noble Truths with right wisdom. 若人皈依佛法僧，以四圣谛见正慧。

**13(191). Dukkhaṃ dukkhasamuppādaṃ, dukkhassa ca atikkamaṃ;\
Ariyaṃ caṭṭhaṅgikaṃ maggaṃ, dukkhūpasamagāminaṃ.**

Dukkha (the truth of suffering, the inherent unsatisfactoriness of existence), the origin of Dukkha, and the overcoming of Dukkha, and the Noble Eightfold Path leading to the cessation of Dukkha. 苦、苦之因、及苦之灭，以及灭苦之八圣道。

**14(192). Etaṃ kho saraṇaṃ khemaṃ, etaṃ saraṇam uttamaṃ;\
Etaṃ saraṇam āgamma, sabbadukkhā pamuccati.**

This, indeed, is a safe refuge; this is an ultimate refuge. Having gone to this refuge, one is liberated from all Dukkha. 此乃安稳归依处，此乃最上归依处；皈依此处解脱苦。

**15(193). Dullabho purisājañño, na so sabbattha jāyati;\
Yattha so jāyati dhīro, taṃ kulaṃ sukhamedhati.**

Difficult to find is a noble person; he is not born everywhere. Where that wise one is born, that family prospers happily. 贤士难得，非处处有；智者所生之处，其家幸福繁荣。

**16(194). Sukho buddhānaṃ uppādo, sukhā saddhammadesanā;\
Sukhā saṅghassa sāmaggi, samaggānaṃ tapo sukho.**

Happy is the arising of Buddhas, happy is the teaching of the true Dhamma, happy is the harmony of the Sangha, happy is in harmony those practising tapa. 诸佛出世乐，正法宣说乐；僧团和合乐，和合苦修乐。

**17(195). Pūjārahe pūjayato, Buddhe yadi va sāvake;\
Papañcasamatikkante, tiṇṇasokapariddave.**

Of one who honors those worthy of honor, whether Buddhas or disciples, who have transcended mental proliferation(papañca) and crossed over sorrow and lamentation. 敬礼应敬者，是佛或弟子；已超戏论者，已度忧悲者。

**18(196). Te tādise pūjayato, nibbute akutobhaye;\
Na sakkā puññaṃ saṅkhātuṃ, im'ettam'iti kenaci.**

Of one who honors such liberated ones, free from fear from anywhere, it is not possible for anyone to calculate the merit, saying \'it is this much\'. 敬礼如是寂灭、无有怖畏者，其所获功德，无人能计数，谓"其量如是"。

### **Summary of Buddha Vagga**

Buddha Vagga focuses on the unique qualities of a Buddha, the rarity of their appearance, and the importance of taking refuge in the Buddha, Dhamma, and Sangha. It also emphasizes the core teachings of the Buddhas and the path to liberation. The Buddha is a unique and supremely enlightened being who has conquered all defilements. Taking refuge in the Buddha, Dhamma, and Sangha, and practicing the teachings, is the path to liberation from suffering.

- **The Buddha\'s Unsurpassed Victory (Verses 179-180):** The Buddha\'s victory over defilements is absolute and irreversible. No one can overturn it. The Buddha is \"trackless,\" meaning their enlightened state transcends all conventional ways of understanding and cannot be traced by ordinary means. The Buddha has cut the \"clinging net of craving,\" preventing any further rebirth.

- **The Wise and the Buddhas (Verse 181):** The wise who are devoted to jhana (meditative absorption) and delight in the peace of renunciation are admired even by the devas. This verse praises the qualities of those who follow the path to enlightenment, including the Buddhas themselves.

- **The Rarity of the Buddha\'s Appearance (Verse 182):** Becoming a human being, living as a mortal, hearing the true Dhamma, and the appearance of a Buddha are all rare and precious occurrences. This highlights the fortunate circumstances required for spiritual progress.

- **The Teaching of the Buddhas (Verse 183):** The core teaching of all Buddhas is: to abstain from all evil, to cultivate good, and to purify one\'s mind. This is the essence of the Buddhist path.

- **Endurance and Non-Violence (Verse 184):** Endurance (khanti) is the highest form of self-discipline. Nibbana is the supreme goal, as taught by the Buddhas. One who harms or torments others is not a true renunciate (pabbajito) or ascetic (samana). This emphasizes the importance of non-violence and patience.

- **Essential Practices (Verse 185):** Key practices of the Buddhas\' teaching include: not insulting or harming others, practicing restraint according to the Patimokkha (monastic code of conduct), moderation in eating, dwelling in seclusion, and dedicating oneself to higher mental development (adhicitta).

- **The Unsatisfactory Nature of Sensual Pleasures (Verses 186-187):** Sensual pleasures, even heavenly ones, can never bring true satisfaction. They are of little enjoyment and ultimately lead to suffering. A true disciple of the Buddha finds delight in the destruction of craving, not in sensual gratification.

- **Taking Refuge (Verses 188-192):** People often seek refuge in external things like mountains, forests, parks, trees, and shrines due to fear. However, these are not ultimate refuges. True refuge is found in the Buddha, the Dhamma (the teachings), and the Sangha (the community of practitioners). Taking this refuge leads to understanding the Four Noble Truths: suffering (dukkha), its origin, its cessation, and the path to its cessation (the Noble Eightfold Path). This is the safe and ultimate refuge that leads to liberation from all suffering.

- **The Rarity of Noble Individuals (Verse 193):** Noble individuals (purisājañño) are rare and not born everywhere. The family in which such a wise person is born experiences happiness and prosperity.

- **The Happiness of the Buddha\'s Appearance and Teachings (Verses 194-196):** The arising of Buddhas, the teaching of the true Dhamma, the harmony of the Sangha, and the harmonious practice of those striving for liberation are all sources of great happiness. Honoring those worthy of honor, whether Buddhas or their disciples who have transcended mental proliferation (papañca) and overcome sorrow and lamentation, generates immeasurable merit.

In summary, the Buddha Vagga highlights the unique qualities of the Buddha as a fully enlightened being and emphasizes the importance of taking refuge in the Triple Gem (Buddha, Dhamma, and Sangha). It also emphasizes the core teachings of the Buddhas, including abstaining from evil, cultivating good, purifying the mind, and the path to liberation from suffering. The chapter concludes by extolling the happiness associated with the arising of Buddhas, the teaching of the Dhamma, the harmony of the Sangha, and the immeasurable merit of honoring those worthy of honor.

**1(197). Susukhaṃ vata jīvāma, verinesu averino;\
Verinesu manussesu, viharāma averino.**

**2(198). Susukhaṃ vata jīvāma, āturesu anāturā;\
Āturesu manussesu, viharāma anāturā.**

How happily indeed we live, without affliction among the afflicted! Among afflicted people, we dwell without affliction. 我等实乐哉，于诸受苦者，住于无苦中，于诸受苦人。

**3(199). Susukhaṃ vata jīvāma, ussukesu anussukā;\
Ussukesu manussesu, viharāma anussukā.**

How happily indeed we live, without restlessness among those who are restless! Among restless people, we dwell without restlessness. 我等实乐哉，于忙碌者中， 住内心平静，于诸忙碌人。

**4(200). Susukhaṃ vata jīvāma, yesaṃ no natthi kiñcanaṃ;\
Pītibhakkhā bhavissāma, devā ābhassarā yathā.**

How happily indeed we live, we who have no possession and no attachment! We shall be sustained by joy, like the shining devas (Ābhassara). 我等实乐哉，舍弃了一切；以喜悦为食，犹如光音天。

**5(201). Jayaṃ veraṃ pasavati, dukkhaṃ seti parājito;\
Upasanto sukhaṃ seti, hitvā jayaparājayaṃ.**

Victory breeds enmity; the defeated one dwells in pain. The peaceful one dwells in happiness, having abandoned victory and defeat. 胜利带来怨恨，失败让人痛苦；内心平静之人，才能安乐生活，放下输赢执着。

**6(202). Natthi rāgasamo aggi, natthi dosasamo kali;\
Natthi khandhasamā dukkhā, natthi santiparaṃ sukhaṃ.**

There is no fire like rāga (craving, passion, lust, attachment), there is no conflict like dosa (hatred, aversion, ill will, anger); there are no dukkha like the aggregates (worldly existence), there is no happiness beyond peace. 无火如贪欲,无冲突如嗔；无苦如世蕴,无乐平静外.

**7(203). Jighacchā paramā rogā, saṅkhārā paramā dukkha;\
Etaṃ ñatvā yathābhūtaṃ, nibbānaṃ paramaṃ sukhaṃ.**

Hunger is the worst of diseases; saṅkhārā (worldly fabrications, formations, volitional activities in the world, conditioned things) is the greatest dukkha. Having known this as it truly is, Nibbāna is the supreme happiness. 饥饿为最重之病，行于世乃最大苦；如实知此已，涅槃最上乐。

**8(204). Ārogyaparamā lābhā, santuṭṭhi paramaṃ dhanaṃ;\

Health is the supreme gain, contentment is the supreme wealth; trust is the supreme relative (kinsmen), Nibbāna is the supreme happiness. 健康为最上之利，知足为最上之财； 信赖为最上之亲，涅槃为最上之乐。

**9(205). Pavivekarasaṃ pitvā, rasaṃ upasamassa ca;\
Niddaro hoti nippāpo, dhammapītirasaṃ pivaṃ.**

Having tasted the flavour of solitude and supreme tranquility, he becomes free from fear and evil, drinking the nectar of the joy of the Dhamma. 饮独居之味，及寂静之味；饮法喜之味，离怖且无恶。

**10(206). Sādhu dassanam ariyānaṃ, sannivāso sadā sukha;\

Good is the seeing of the noble ones; association with them is always happiness. By not seeing fools, one may always be happy indeed. 见圣者是善，在一起是乐；不见愚人故，恒常得安乐。

**11(207). Bālasaṅgatacārī hi, dīgham addhāna socati;\

Dhīro ca sukhasaṃvāso, ñātīnaṃ 'va samāgamo.**

Indeed, one who associates with fools grieves for a long time. Association with fools is painful, just like with an enemy, always. And association with the wise is pleasant, like a gathering of kinsmen. 与愚者一起，长时受忧戚；与愚者一起，如与敌甚苦；与智者一起，乐如亲族聚。

**12(208). Tasmā hi dhīrañca paññañca bahussutañca, dhorayhasīlaṃ vatavantam āriyaṃ; Taṃ tādisaṃ sappurisaṃ sumedhaṃ, bhajetha nakkhattapathaṃ 'va candimā.**

Therefore, one should associate with such a good, wise, learned, virtuous, vow-observing, noble person of good wisdom, like the moon follows the path of the stars. 是故当亲近，具慧博学士，持戒守誓贤；亲近智慧者，如月随星路。

### **Summary of Sukha Vagga**

Sukha Vagga explores the nature of true happiness, contrasting it with fleeting worldly pleasures. It emphasizes the importance of inner peace, contentment, and association with the wise as sources of lasting joy. True happiness is found not in external circumstances or sensual pleasures but in inner peace, contentment, and the company of the wise.

- **Happiness Among the Hostile (Verses 197-199):** True happiness is found in living without enmity, affliction, or restlessness, even among those who are hostile, afflicted, or restless. This emphasizes the importance of inner peace and equanimity regardless of external circumstances.

- **Happiness of Non-Attachment (Verse 200):** Those who have no possessions or attachments live happily, sustained by joy, like the shining devas (Ābhassara). This highlights the liberating quality of detachment.

- **Peace Beyond Victory and Defeat (Verse 201):** Victory breeds enmity, and defeat leads to suffering. True happiness is found in peace, which comes from abandoning the desire for victory and the fear of defeat. This emphasizes the futility of worldly competition.

- **The Nature of True Happiness (Verses 202-204):** There is no fire like craving (rāga), no conflict like hatred (dosa), no suffering like the five aggregates (khandhas), and no happiness beyond peace (santi). Hunger is the worst disease, and conditioned existence (saṅkhārā) is the greatest suffering. True happiness is found in Nibbana (liberation), which is also described as the supreme gain (health), the supreme wealth (contentment), and the supreme kinship (trust). These verses identify the root causes of suffering and point towards Nibbana as the ultimate happiness.

- **The Joy of Solitude and the Dhamma (Verse 205):** Tasting the flavor of solitude and tranquility, one becomes free from fear and evil, experiencing the joy of the Dhamma. This emphasizes the benefits of seclusion and spiritual practice.

- **The Value of Noble Companionship (Verses 206-208):** Seeing and associating with noble ones brings constant happiness. Avoiding fools leads to constant joy. Associating with fools brings long-lasting grief, like being with an enemy. Associating with the wise, however, is pleasant, like a gathering of relatives. Therefore, one should associate with wise, learned, virtuous, and noble individuals, as the moon follows the path of the stars. This emphasizes the profound influence of one\'s companions and the importance of seeking out the wise.

Sukha Vagga contrasts fleeting worldly pleasures with the lasting joy of inner peace and liberation. It emphasizes the importance of below qualities, one can experience true and lasting happiness, transcending the suffering inherent in worldly existence.

- **Inner Peace:** Cultivating equanimity and freedom from negative emotions like enmity, affliction, and restlessness.

- **Detachment:** Abandoning attachment to possessions and worldly desires.

- **Contentment:** Finding happiness in simplicity and inner resources.

- **Wisdom:** Understanding the true nature of existence and the path to liberation.

- **Noble Companionship:** Seeking out and associating with wise and virtuous individuals.**\**

### **Summary of Piya Vagga**

Piya Vagga explores the nature of attachment and its connection to suffering. It emphasizes that true liberation comes from detachment, not from seeking happiness through worldly affections. Attachment to loved ones and aversion to disliked ones are sources of suffering. True peace and freedom come from relinquishing these worldly attachments and living according to the Dhamma.

- **Regret of Improper Engagement (Verse 209):** One who engages in improper activities and neglects proper ones, abandoning what is truly beneficial for what is merely pleasing, will regret their choices when they see the progress of those who have dedicated themselves to the path. This highlights the importance of prioritizing spiritual practice over worldly distractions.

- **The Pain of Association (Verse 210):** Association with both loved and disliked ones brings suffering. Not seeing loved ones causes pain, and seeing disliked ones also causes pain. This emphasizes the inherent unsatisfactoriness of attachment and aversion.

- **The Bondage of Affection (Verse 211):** One should not form attachments to loved ones, as separation from them is painful. Those who have no love or hate are free from such bonds. This emphasizes detachment as the path to freedom.

- **Sorrow and Fear from Affection (Verses 212-218):** Sorrow and fear arise from affection (piya), love (pema), craving (rati), sensual desire (kama), and lust (tanha). Those who are free from these attachments experience no sorrow or fear. These verses repeatedly emphasize the direct link between attachment and suffering.

- **The Beloved of Many (Verse 219):** One who is endowed with virtue and insight, established in the Dhamma, a knower of truth, and performs their duties is held dear by many. This highlights the positive qualities that attract respect and affection.

- **The Upstream Flow (Verse 218):** One whose desire is directed towards Nibbana (the unmanifest), whose mind is pervaded by this aspiration, and whose mind is not bound by sensual pleasures is called \"one who flows upstream,\" going against the current of worldly desires.

- **The Welcome of Good Deeds (Verses 219-220):** Just as relatives, friends, and companions welcome a person who has returned safely from a long journey, so too do good deeds welcome one who has passed from this world to the next. This emphasizes the importance of accumulating merit through good actions.

This chapter encourages listeners to focus on the path to liberation by overcoming worldly attachment and cultivating inner transcending peace.

- **Detachment from the worldly as the Path to Freedom:** Attachment to loved ones and aversion to disliked ones are sources of suffering. Relinquishing these attachments leads to true peace.

- **The Importance of Spiritual Practice:** Prioritizing spiritual development over worldly pleasures is crucial for attaining liberation.

- **The Power of Good Deeds:** Performing good deeds brings positive consequences in the future.

- **The Value of Virtue and Wisdom:** Cultivating virtue and wisdom makes one worthy of respect and affection.**\**

**1(221). Kodhaṃ jahe vippajaheyya mānaṃ, saṃyojanaṃ sabbam atikkameyya; Taṃ nāmarūpasmiṃ asajjamānaṃ, akiñcanaṃ nānupatanti dukkhā.**

Overcome anger by non-anger, overcome evil by good; overcome miserliness by generosity, overcome the liar by truth. 以非怒胜怒，以善胜不善；以施胜悭吝，以诚胜妄语。

Speak the truth, do not get angry, give even a little when asked; by these three things, one goes to the presence of the devas. 应说真话，不起瞋恚，分施与乞者; 通过这三事，可达诸天前。

Those sages who are harmless (non-violent), who are constantly restrained in body; they go to the unchanging realm (the Deathless, Nibbana), where having gone, they do not grieve. 不伤害的贤者，总防护己身；已达不朽界，那里不伤悲。

For those who are ever vigilant, who train day and night, who are intent on Nibbana, their defilements come to an end. 恒常醒觉者，日夜勤修学，志向于涅槃，息灭诸烦恼。

One should guard against bodily misconduct, one should be restrained in body; Having abandoned bodily misconduct, one should practice good conduct with the body. 当守护身体的不正行为，当以身体约束自己；舍离身恶行，以身修善行。

**12(232). Vacīpakopaṃ rakkheyya, vācāya saṃvuto siyā;\
Vacīduccaritaṃ hitvā, vācāya sucaritaṃ care.**

One should guard against verbal misconduct, one should be restrained in speech; Having abandoned verbal misconduct, one should practice good conduct with speech. 当守护语言的不正行为，当以言语约束自己；舍离语恶行，以语修善行。

**13(233). Manopakopaṃ rakkheyya, manasā saṃvuto siyā;\
Manoduccaritaṃ hitvā, manasā sucaritaṃ care.**

One should guard against mental misconduct, one should be restrained in will (Mano); Having abandoned mental misconduct, one should practice good conduct with the will. 当守护意念的不正行为，当以意念约束自己；舍离意恶行，以意修善行。

**14(234). Kāyena saṃvutā dhīrā, atho vācāya saṃvutā;\
Manasā saṃvutā dhīrā, te ve suparisaṃvutā.**

The wise are restrained in body, and also restrained in speech; the wise are restrained in will (mano); they are truly well restrained. 智者身调伏，亦复语调伏，于意亦调伏，实完全调伏。

### **Summary of Kodha Vagga**

Kodha Vagga focuses on the destructive nature of anger and the importance of overcoming it through self-control, kindness, and truthfulness. It also addresses the inevitability of blame and praise and emphasizes the importance of restraining one\'s actions, speech, and thoughts. Anger is a destructive force that must be overcome through self-control, non-violence, and the cultivation of positive qualities. True virtue lies in restraining one\'s actions, speech, and thoughts, leading to liberation from suffering.

- **Abandoning Anger, Pride, and Fetters (Verse 221):** One should abandon anger, renounce pride, and transcend all fetters (attachments). By not clinging to the concept of \"self\" (name and form) and being free from possessions, suffering ceases. This establishes the foundation for overcoming anger: detachment and non-clinging.

- **Controlling Anger as a Charioteer (Verse 222):** One who controls rising anger, like a charioteer controlling a runaway chariot, is a true charioteer (master of themselves). Others who merely hold the reins are not. This emphasizes the importance of active self-control.

- **Overcoming Negative Qualities with Positive Ones (Verse 223):** Overcome anger with non-anger (metta, loving-kindness), evil with good, miserliness with generosity, and lies with truth. This provides practical methods for countering negative emotions and actions.

- **The Path to Heavenly Realms (Verse 224):** Speaking the truth, not getting angry, and giving even a little when asked are three qualities that lead to heavenly realms (a metaphor for positive rebirths and states of mind). This emphasizes the importance of ethical conduct.

- **The Harmless Sages (Verse 225):** Sages who are harmless and constantly restrained in body attain the unchanging realm (Nibbana), where there is no more grief. This highlights the connection between non-violence and liberation.

- **Vigilance and the End of Defilements (Verse 226):** For those who are constantly vigilant, training day and night, and focused on Nibbana, their defilements (asavas) come to an end. This emphasizes the importance of continuous effort and mindfulness.

- **The Inevitability of Blame (Verse 227-228):** It is an ancient truth that people are blamed regardless of their actions -- whether they are silent, speak much, or speak moderately. There is no one in the world who is entirely blamed or entirely praised. This encourages acceptance of criticism and not being overly concerned with the opinions of others.

- **The Praise of the Wise (Verses 229-230):** The wise, after careful observation, praise those who live a blameless life, are intelligent, and possess moral restraint. Such individuals are like pure gold and are praised even by the devas and Brahma. This emphasizes the importance of living a virtuous and wise life, which earns genuine respect.

- **Restraint of Body, Speech, and Mind (Verses 231-234):** One should guard against misconduct in body, speech, and mind. By abandoning negative actions, one should practice good conduct in these three areas. Those who are restrained in body, speech, and mind are truly well restrained. This summarizes the core practice of self-discipline.

In summary, the Kodha Vagga provides practical guidance on overcoming anger and cultivating inner peace. It emphasizes:

- **Self-Control:** Mastering one\'s anger and negative emotions.

- **Non-Violence:** Avoiding harm to oneself and others in thought, word, and deed.

- **Kindness and Truthfulness:** Countering negative qualities with positive ones.

- **Ethical Conduct:** Living a blameless and virtuous life.

- **Mindfulness and Vigilance:** Maintaining constant awareness and effort on the path to liberation.

By practicing these principles, one can overcome anger, attain inner peace, and progress towards liberation from suffering.

[]{#_Toc188087626 .anchor}**18. Mala Vagga -- Impurities 垢**

**1(235). Paṇḍupalāso 'va 'dāni 'si, yamapurisā'pi ca te upaṭṭhitā;\
Uyyogamukhe ca tiṭṭhasi, pātheyyam pi ca te na vijjati.**

You are now like a withered leaf, and the messengers of Yama (the god of death) are also standing near you; you stand at the mouth of departure, and you have no provisions for the journey. 汝今已似枯槁叶，死神使者近身边。 汝已伫立死门前，旅途汝亦无资粮。

**2(236). So karohi dīpam attano, khippaṃ vāyama paṇḍito bhava;\
Niddhantamalo anaṅgaṇo, dibbaṃ ariyabhūmim upehisi.**

Therefore, make an island unto yourself, strive quickly, become wise; Free from impurities, stainless, you will enter the heavenly realm of the Ariyas. 汝应以自为岛洲，迅速精勤生智慧。拂除尘垢无烦恼，得达圣者神圣境 。

**3(237). Upanītavayo va'dāni'si, sampayāto'si yamassa santike;\
Vāso te natthi antarā, pātheyyam pi ca te na vijjati.**

You are now advanced in age, you have approached the presence of Yama (the god of death); You have no dwelling place in between (life and the next), and you have no provisions for the journey. 汝今寿命行已终，汝已移步近死神。 道中既无停息处，旅途汝亦无资粮。

**4(238). So karohi dīpam attano, khippaṃ vāyama paṇḍito bhava\
Niddhantamalo anaṅgaṇo, na puna jātijaraṃ upehisi.**

Therefore, make an island unto yourself, strive quickly, become wise; Free from impurities, stainless, you will not again undergo birth and old age. 汝应以自为岛洲，迅速精勤生智慧。拂除尘垢无烦恼，不复重来生与老。

**5(239). Anupubbena medhāvī, thokathokaṃ khaṇe khaṇe;\
Kammāro rajatass'eva, niddhame malam attano.**

Gradually, the wise one, little by little, moment by moment, as a smith (removes dross) from silver, should remove the impurities of himself. 一点一点地，每时每刻地，智者除自垢，如铁匠锻银。

**6(240). Ayasā'va malaṃ samuṭṭhitaṃ, tatuṭṭhāya tam'eva khādati;\
Evaṃ atidhonacārinaṃ, sāni kammāni nayanti duggatiṃ.**

Just as rust arisen from iron eats away the very (iron); So (too), their own deeds lead those who are excessively corrupt in conduct to a bad destination. 如铁自生锈，生已自腐蚀，犯罪者亦尔，自业导恶趣。

**7(241). Asajjhāyamalā mantā, anuṭṭhānamalā gharā;\
Malaṃ vaṇṇassa kosajjaṃ, pamādo rakkhato malaṃ.**

Lack of recitation is the impurity of mantras, lack of upkeep is the impurity of houses; Laziness is the impurity of beauty, negligence is the impurity of a guard. 不诵经典垢，不勤房子垢，懒惰美丽垢，疏忽护卫垢。

**8(242). Mal'itthiyā duccaritaṃ, maccheraṃ dadato malaṃ;\
Malā ve pāpakā dhammā, asmiṃ loke paramhi ca.**

Misconduct is the impurity of a woman, stinginess is the impurity of a giver; Impurities, indeed, are evil things, in this world and in the next. 行为不端是女子的污垢，吝啬是布施者的污垢；此世及来世，恶法实为垢。

**9(243). Tato malā malataraṃ, avijjā paramaṃ malaṃ;\
Etaṃ malaṃ pahatvāna, nimmalā hotha bhikkhavo.**

Of those impurities, ignorance is the greatest impurity; Having abandoned this impurity, be stainless, O Bhikkhus! 此等诸垢中，无知为最大，汝当除此垢，成无垢比丘！

**10(244). Sujīvaṃ ahirīkena, kākasūrena dhaṃsinā;\
Pakkhandinā pagabbhena, saṃkiliṭṭhena jīvitaṃ.**

Easy is life for one who is shameless, a crow-hero (heroic crow, false bravery, arrogance and impudence), offensive; for one who is shameless, careless, and corrupt is life easy. 生活无惭愧，如乌鸦英雄，诋毁于他人，大胆自夸张，傲慢邪恶者，其人生活易。

**11(245). Hirīmatā ca dujjīvaṃ, niccaṃ sucigavesinā;\
Alīnen'āpagabbhena, suddhājīvena passatā.**

But difficult is life for one who is conscientious, constantly seeking purity; for one who is unattached, not reckless, and lives a pure life, observing. 生活于惭愧，常求于清净，不着欲谦逊，住清净生活，富于观察者，其人生活难 。

**12(246). Yo pāṇam atipāteti, musāvādañca bhāsati;\
Loke adinnaṃ ādiyati, paradārañca gacchati.**

He kills living beings, speaks falsely; He takes what is not given, and goes to another\'s wife.杀生说妄语，取人所不与，犯于别人妻。

**13(247). Surāmerayapānañca, yo naro anuyuñjati;\
Idh'evam eso lokasmiṃ, mūlaṃ khaṇati attano.**

He who consumes intoxicating drinks, Indeed, in this world, he digs his own grave. 饮陶醉饮料，行为如是者，即于此世界，为自己挖坟。

**14(248). Evaṃ bho purisa jānāhi, pāpadhammā asaññatā;\
Mā taṃ lobho adhammo ca, ciraṃ dukkhāya randhayuṃ.**

Thus, good man, know this: evil deeds are not unnoticed. Let not greed and evil deeds, for a long time, make you a vessel of suffering. 如是汝应知：恶行不会不被察觉, 莫让贪和恶, 长时自陷苦。

**15(249). Dadāti ve yathāsaddhaṃ, yathāpasādanaṃ jano;\
Tattha yo maṅku bhavati, paresaṃ pānabhojane;\
Na so divā vā rattiṃ vā, samādhiṃ adhigacchati.**

People give according to their faith and inclination. Whoever becomes attached to the food and drink of others, neither by day nor by night does he attain concentration. 施因信与乐。 沉迷于供食，彼于昼或夜，不得入三昧。

**16(250). Yassa c'etaṃ samucchinnaṃ, mūlaghaccaṃ samūhataṃ;\
Sa ve divā vā rattiṃ vā, samādhiṃ adhigacchati.**

But for whom this is completely cut off, its root and stem uprooted, He indeed, both day and night, attains concentration. 若斩断此心，拔根及除灭，则于昼或夜，彼得入三昧。

**17(251). Natthi rāgasamo aggi, natthi dosasamo gaho;\
Natthi mohasamaṃ jālaṃ, natthi** **taṇhāsamā nadi.**

There is no fire as consuming as passion (rāga), no noose as binding as aversion (dosa), no net as ensnaring as delusion (moha), and no river as overwhelming as craving (taṇhā). 没有比贪欲更炽烈的火焰，没有比憎恨更紧密的绳索，没有比迷惑更难逃的网罗，没有比渴爱更汹涌的河流。

**18(252). Sudassaṃ vajjam aññesaṃ, attano pana duddasaṃ;\
Paresaṃ hi so vajjāni, opunāti yathā bhusaṃ;\
Attano pana chādeti, kaliṃ 'va kitavā saṭho.**

It is easy to see the faults of others, but difficult to see one\'s own. For one easily points out the faults of others, like sifting chaff, but hides his own, like a deceitful thief hiding his stolen goods. 容易看到别人过失，却难看到自己缺点；人们很容易像扬弃谷壳一样指出别人的过错，却像一个狡诈的小偷藏匿赃物一样掩盖自己的错误。

**19(253). Paravajjānupassissa, niccaṃ ujjhānasaññino;\
Āsavā tassa vaḍḍhanti, ārā so āsavakkhayā.**

Always considering the faults of others and always having a sense of superiority, his defilements increase. He is far from the destruction of defilements. 关注他人过，总自以为是，彼烦恼增长，离断烦恼远。

**20(254). Ākāse padaṃ natthi, samaṇo natthi bāhire;\
Papañcābhiratā pajā, nippapañcā Tathāgatā.**

There is no footprint in the sky. External there is no samaṇa. People delight in the world of becoming, while the Tathagatas are free from it. 空中没有足迹，沙门不在外面；众生乐于世间，Tathāgatā出离世间。

**21(255). Ākāse padaṃ natthi, samaṇo natthi bāhire;\
Saṅkhārā sassatā natthi, natthi buddhānaṃ iñjitaṃ.**

There is no footprint in the sky. External there is no samaṇa. Saṅkhāra are not permanent, there is no Saṅkhāra (worldly formation) of the Buddhas. 空中没有足迹，沙门不在外面；诸行不恒常，诸佛不世行。

### **Summary of Mala Vagga**

Mala Vagga focuses on the various kinds of impurities that hinder spiritual progress, emphasizing the importance of self-purification and striving for liberation. Just as physical objects can be tarnished by impurities, so too can the mind be defiled by various negative qualities. By diligently removing these impurities through wisdom and effort, one can attain liberation.

- **Impermanence and the Need for Self-Reliance (Verses 235-238):** Life is fleeting, like a withered leaf. Death is approaching, and one should prepare by making oneself an \"island\" (a refuge within). By striving diligently and becoming wise, one can become free from impurities and attain the \"heavenly realm of the Ariyas\" (liberation), escaping future birth and old age. These verses emphasize the urgency of spiritual practice.

- **Gradual Self-Purification (Verse 239):** The wise person should gradually remove their impurities, moment by moment, like a smith removing dross from silver. This emphasizes the importance of consistent effort.

- **Self-Destructive Nature of Evil Deeds (Verse 240):** Just as rust eats away the iron it arises from, one\'s own evil deeds lead one to a bad destination. This highlights the karmic consequences of negative actions.

- **Various Impurities (Verses 241-242):** Various examples of impurities are given: lack of recitation for mantras, lack of upkeep for houses, laziness for beauty, negligence for a guard, misconduct for a woman, and stinginess for a giver. These verses illustrate that impurities can manifest in different areas of life. More generally, evil actions are impurities in this life and the next.

- **Ignorance as the Greatest Impurity (Verse 243):** Among all impurities, ignorance (avijja) is the greatest. By overcoming ignorance, one becomes truly stainless. This points to the central role of wisdom in purification.

- **The Easy Life of the Shameless (Verse 244) and the Difficult Life of the Conscientious (Verse 245):** It is easy for the shameless, arrogant, and corrupt to live, but difficult for the conscientious, pure, and observant. This highlights the contrast between those who prioritize worldly ease and those who prioritize spiritual development.

- **Examples of Misconduct (Verses 246-247):** Killing, lying, stealing, adultery, and consuming intoxicants are examples of misconduct that lead to negative consequences. These actions \"dig one\'s own grave.\"

- **The Consequences of Evil Deeds (Verse 248):** Evil deeds do not go unnoticed and lead to long-lasting suffering. This reinforces the principle of karma.

- **Attachment to Food and Drink (Verses 249-250):** Attachment to the food and drink of others hinders concentration. Cutting off this attachment leads to the attainment of concentration. This emphasizes the importance of detachment even from basic necessities.

- **The Most Powerful Impurities (Verse 251):** There is no fire like passion (raga), no grip like aversion (dosa), no net like delusion (moha), and no river like craving (tanha). These negative emotions are the most potent sources of defilement.

- **Seeing Others\' Faults and Hiding One\'s Own (Verses 252-253):** It is easy to see the faults of others but difficult to see one\'s own. Focusing on others\' faults increases one\'s own defilements and hinders progress towards liberation. This emphasizes the importance of introspection and self-awareness.

- **The Nature of the Enlightened (Verses 254-255):** There are no \"footprints\" of the enlightened in the sense that they do not leave traces of attachment or defilement. They are not defined by external practices alone. They have transcended the world of becoming (papañca) and are free from all conditioned existence (saṅkhāra).

In summary, the Mala Vagga emphasizes the importance of self-purification by overcoming various impurities, especially ignorance, craving, and negative emotions. It highlights the fleeting nature of life, the karmic consequences of actions, and the importance of self-reliance, consistent effort, and wisdom on the path to liberation.

[]{#_Toc188087628 .anchor}**19. Dhammaṭṭha Vagga -- The Righteous 具法者**

**1(256). Na tena hoti dhammaṭṭho, yen'atthaṃ sāhasā naye;\
yo ca atthaṃ anatthañca, ubho niccheyya paṇḍito.**

One is not righteous merely because one decides a dispute by force; but a wise one discriminates between right and wrong (in a case). 强权断案非具法，明辨是非方为智。

**2(257). Asāhasena dhammena, samena nayatī pare;\
Dhammassa gutto medhāvī, "dhammaṭṭho"ti pavuccati.**

Without force, by the Dhamma, equally he leads others; Of the Dhamma protected, wise (one), \"righteous one\" is said. 不以强力而以法，平等地引导他人；守护法的智者，被称为具法者。

**3(258). Na tena paṇḍito hoti, yāvatā bahu bhāsati;\
Khemī averī abhayo, "paṇḍito"ti pavuccati.**

Not by that does one become wise, to the extent that one speaks much; Secure, without enmity, fearless, \"wise person\" is said. 不以多言故，彼即为智者。安稳无怨惧，是名为智者。

**4(259). Na tāvatā dhammadharo, yāvatā bahu bhāsati;\
Yo ca appam pi sutvāna, dhammaṃ kāyena passata;\
Sa ve dhammadharo hoti, yo dhammaṃ nappamajjati.**

Not merely by that is one who upholds the Dhamma, to the extent that one speaks much; and whoever, having heard even a little, experiences the Dhamma in practice; He indeed is one who upholds the Dhamma, who does not neglect the Dhamma. 不以多言故，彼为具法者；若人少许闻，能以身见法；彼实具法者，于法不懈怠。

**5(260). Na tena thero hoti, yen'assa Pāḷitaṃ siro;\
Paripakko vayo tassa, "moghajiṇṇo"ti vuccati.**

Not by that does one become an elder, by which his head is gray; Matured (is) his age, \"empty old man\" is said. 不因彼白头，即是为长老。 彼年龄虚熟，徒有长老名。

**6(261). Yamhi saccañca dhammo ca, ahiṃsā saṃyamo damo;\
Sa ve vantamalo dhīro, "thero" iti pavuccati.**

In whom (are) truth and the Dhamma, non-harming, restraint, (and) taming; He indeed, having eliminated defilements, (is) wise, \"elder\" is said. 具真理和法，不害且自制，彼实为长老，已离垢智者。

**7(262). Na vākkaraṇamattena, vaṇṇapokkharatāya vā;\
Sādhurūpo naro hoti, issukī maccharī saṭho.**

One is not a good person merely by eloquence or a beautiful appearance, if one is jealous, stingy, and deceitful. 并非仅凭口才或外貌, 便可称之为善人；若心怀嫉妒、吝啬与欺诈，则不然。

**8(263). Yassa c'etaṃ samucchinnaṃ, mūlaghaccaṃ samūhataṃ;\
Sa vantadoso medhāvī, "Sādhurūpo"ti vuccati.**

And in whom this is completely cut off, cut at the root, completely removed; He indeed, having eliminated faults, (is) wise, \"good person\" is said. 若已断此心，拔根及除灭，彼实为善人, 已离恶智者。

**9(264). Na muṇḍakena samaṇo, abbato alikaṃ bhaṇaṃ;\
Icchālobhasamāpanno, samaṇo kiṃ bhavissati.**

One does not become a samana merely by having a shaven head, if one is undisciplined and speaks falsehood; possessed by desire and greed, how can such a person be a true samana? 剃除须发非真沙门，若不持戒且说妄语；若被贪欲占据，岂能称为沙门？

**10(265). Yo ca sameti pāpāni, aṇuṃ thūlāni sabbaso;\
Samitattā hi pāpānaṃ, "samaṇo"ti pavuccati.**

And whoever completely subdues evil deeds, both small and great; because of the subduing of evil deeds, he is indeed called a \"samana.\" 彼息灭诸恶, 无论大与小，因息灭诸恶，故名为沙门。

**11(266). Na tena bhikkhu so hoti, yāvatā bhikkhate pare;\
Vissaṃ dhammaṃ samādāya, bhikkhu hoti na tāvatā.**

One is not a bhikkhu merely by going for alms from others; Nor is one a bhikkhu merely by undertaking the whole Dhamma (intellectually). 并非仅靠向人乞食便是比丘；纵然受持全部佛法，亦不能仅因此便成为比丘。

**12(267). Yo'dha puññañca pāpañca, bāhetvā brahmacariyavā;\
Saṅkhāya loke carati, sa ce "bhikkhū"ti vuccati.**

He who in this world has abandoned both merit and demerit, and lives brahmacariya (a life of purity); he, conducting himself with wisdom in the world (acts with awareness and insight into the nature of reality, not impulsively or blindly), is truly called a \"bhikkhu.\" 已舍弃功德及恶业，是梵行者；以智慧在世间行事，彼实为比丘。

**13(268). Na monena muni hoti, mūḷharūpo aviddasu;\
Yo ca tulaṃ 'va paggayha, varam ādāya paṇḍito.**

One does not become a sage merely by silence, if one is foolish and unwise; But he who, as if holding a balance, chooses the best, is wise. 并非沉默寡言便是智者，若内心愚痴不明事理；唯有如持天平般权衡利弊，择善而行者，方为智者。

**14(269). Pāpāni parivajjeti, sa munī tena so munī;\
Yo munāti ubho loke, "munī" tena pavuccati.**

He who avoids evil deeds is thereby a sage; And whoever understands both worlds is therefore called a \"sage.\" 能够避免恶行者，因此被称为智者；能通晓两个世界，因此被称为智者。

**15(270). Na tena ariyo hoti, yena pāṇāni hiṃsati;\
Ahiṃsā sabbapāṇānaṃ, "ariyo"ti pavuccati.**

One is not ariya (noble) simply because one harms living beings; Non-harming of all living beings is what makes one \"noble.\" 并非因伤害众生而为圣者；对一切众生不加伤害，方可称为"圣者"。

**16(271). Na sīlabbatamattena, bāhusaccena vā pana;\
Atha vā samādhilābhena, vivitta sayanena vā.**

Not merely by precepts and observances, nor by great learning; Nor even by the attainment of samādhi, nor by living in seclusion. 并非仅凭持守戒律仪轨，亦非仅凭博学多闻； 亦非仅凭获得禅定，亦非仅凭独居静处。

**17(272). Phusāmi nekkhammasukhaṃ, aputhujjanasevitaṃ;\
Bhikkhu vissāsam āpādi, appatto āsavakkhayaṃ.**

The bhikkhu experiences the bliss of renunciation, a bliss not experienced by ordinary people; He has found confidence (in the path), though he has not yet attained the eradication of defilements. 比丘体验出离之乐，此乐非普通人能够体会；他已获得（对道的）信心，虽然尚未证得漏尽。

### **Summary of Dhammaṭṭha Vagga**

Dhammaṭṭha Vagga defines true righteousness, wisdom, elderhood, goodness, asceticism, and nobility, distinguishing them from mere outward appearances or claims. It emphasizes inner qualities, ethical conduct, and the practice of the Dhamma as the true marks of a virtuous person. True righteousness, wisdom, and spiritual attainment are not determined by outward appearances, eloquence, age, or mere intellectual understanding of the Dhamma. It emphasizes that true spiritual qualities are internal and based on ethical conduct, wisdom, non-harming, self-control, truthfulness, and the practice of the Dhamma. It warns against relying on outward appearances, intellectual understanding, or mere adherence to rules without inner transformation. The chapter encourages listeners to cultivate inner qualities and strive for complete freedom from defilements.

- **True Righteousness (Verses 256-257):** Righteousness is not about using force to win disputes but about wisely distinguishing right from wrong and leading others with fairness and justice, guided by the Dhamma.

- **True Wisdom (Verse 258):** Wisdom is not measured by eloquence but by inner peace, lack of enmity, and fearlessness.

- **True Upholding of the Dhamma (Verse 259):** Merely speaking much about the Dhamma does not make one a true upholder. True upholding comes from experiencing the Dhamma through practice, even if one has heard only a little.

- **True Elderhood (Verses 260-261):** Gray hair does not make one an elder. True elderhood is characterized by inner qualities such as truthfulness, adherence to the Dhamma, non-harming, self-restraint, and the eradication of defilements.

- **True Goodness (Verses 262-263):** Eloquence and outward appearance do not make one good. True goodness comes from eradicating negative qualities like jealousy, stinginess, and deceit.

- **True Asceticism (Verses 264-265):** Shaving one\'s head does not make one a true ascetic (samana) if one is undisciplined and speaks falsely, driven by desire and greed. True asceticism is the complete subduing of all evil deeds, both small and great.

- **True Bhikkhuhood (Verses 266-267):** Simply going for alms or intellectually understanding the Dhamma does not make one a true bhikkhu (monk). A true bhikkhu has abandoned both merit and demerit (good and bad actions) and lives a life of purity (brahmacariya), acting with wisdom and insight.

- **True Sagehood (Verses 268-269):** Silence alone does not make one a sage (muni) if one is foolish. A true sage is wise, chooses the best course of action, avoids evil deeds, and understands both this world and the next.

- **True Nobility (Verse 270):** Harming living beings does not make one noble (ariya). True nobility is characterized by non-harming towards all beings.

- **The Incompleteness of Outward Practices (Verses 271-272):** Mere adherence to precepts and observances, great learning, attainment of concentration, or living in seclusion does not guarantee liberation. Even experiencing the bliss of renunciation (nekkhammasukha) and having confidence in the path are not sufficient without the eradication of defilements (asavakkhaya). This emphasizes that true liberation requires complete eradication of all defilements, not just achieving certain states or adhering to external rules.

### **Summary of Magga Vagga**

Magga Vagga focuses on the Buddhist path to liberation, emphasizing the Eightfold Path, the Four Noble Truths, and the importance of wisdom, effort, and detachment. It also warns against negligence, attachment to worldly things, and the inevitability of death. The Eightfold Path is the only way to purify one\'s vision and attain liberation from suffering. It requires wisdom, effort, detachment from worldly attachments, and understanding the impermanent nature of all worldly things.

- **The Best of Paths and Truths (Verse 273):** The Eightfold Path is the best of all paths, the Four Noble Truths are the best of all truths, detachment is the best of all mental states, and the Buddha is the best of all beings. This establishes the framework for the chapter.

- **The One and Only Path (Verses 274-276):** The Eightfold Path is the only path to purification and overcoming Mara (death/temptation). The Tathagatas (Buddhas) are only proclaimers of the path; the effort to practice it must be made by oneself. Those who practice and meditate will be liberated.

- **The Three Characteristics of Existence (Verses 277-279):** Understanding the three characteristics of existence -- impermanence (anicca), unsatisfactoriness/suffering (dukkha), and non-self (anatta) -- with wisdom leads to disenchantment with the world and is the path to purity.

- **The Dangers of Negligence (Verse 280):** When one is young and strong but fails to exert effort due to laziness and wavering thoughts, one will not find the path to wisdom. This emphasizes the importance of timely and consistent effort.

- **Purifying the Three Actions (Verse 281):** One should purify one\'s actions in three areas: speech, mind, and body. This involves guarding one\'s speech, restraining the mind, and avoiding bodily misconduct. By purifying these three courses of action, one cultivates the path proclaimed by the seers (rishis/Buddhas).

- **Wisdom and Effort (Verse 282):** Wisdom arises from practice (yoga), and the lack of practice (ayoga) leads to the decline of wisdom. Understanding this twofold path, one should strive to increase wisdom.

- **Cutting Down the Forest of Desire (Verses 283-284):** One should cut down the "forest" of craving, not just individual "trees." Fear arises from the forest (of craving). As long as even a small amount of attachment to sensual pleasures remains, one\'s mind is bound.

- **Cutting Off Affection (Verse 285):** One should cut off affection (sineha) like plucking a water lily. This emphasizes the need for complete detachment from worldly attachments. One should cultivate the path to peace (Nibbana) taught by the Sugata (Buddha).

- **The Fool\'s Misunderstanding of Time (Verses 286-287):** A fool thinks they have plenty of time and makes plans for the future without realizing the imminence of death. Death can seize one at any moment, like a flood sweeping through a sleeping village.

- **Lack of Refuge in Relatives (Verse 288):** When death comes, neither sons, fathers, nor relatives can offer true protection. This emphasizes the importance of finding refuge in the Dhamma, not in worldly relationships.

- **Purifying the Path to Nibbana (Verse 289):** Having understood the inevitability of death and the lack of refuge in worldly things, a wise person, restrained by ethical conduct (sila), should quickly purify the path leading to Nibbana.

In summary, the Magga Vagga emphasizes the following key aspects of the Buddhist path:

- **The Eightfold Path as the Only Way:** It is the sole path to purification and liberation.

- **The Importance of Wisdom:** Understanding the three characteristics of existence is crucial.

- **The Necessity of Effort:** Consistent and timely effort is required for spiritual progress.

- **The Need for Detachment:** Overcoming attachment to worldly things, especially sensual pleasures and loved ones, is essential.

- **The Inevitability of Death:** Recognizing the impermanence of life motivates one to practice diligently.

The chapter encourages listeners to actively engage in the practice of the Eightfold Path to attain liberation from suffering.

### **Summary of Pakiṇṇaka Vagga**

Pakiṇṇaka Vagga covers a variety of topics related to the Buddhist path, emphasizing the importance of wisdom, effort, mindfulness, and ethical conduct. It also touches on the nature of suffering, the difficulty of renunciation, and the qualities of virtuous individuals. True happiness and liberation are found through wisdom, ethical conduct, mindfulness, and detachment from worldly concerns. Negligence, harmful actions, and attachment to fleeting pleasures lead to suffering.

- **Renouncing Lesser for Greater Happiness (Verse 290):** If by giving up a small happiness, one can attain a greater happiness, the wise person should renounce the small happiness for the sake of the greater. This emphasizes prioritizing long-term spiritual benefits over short-term gratification.

- **The Consequences of Harming Others (Verse 291):** Those who seek their own happiness by inflicting suffering on others become entangled in enmity and are never free from it. This highlights the karmic consequences of harmful actions.

- **The Increase of Defilements (Verse 292):** Neglecting what should be done and doing what should not be done leads to an increase in defilements for the arrogant and heedless. This emphasizes the importance of diligent practice and avoiding negative actions.

- **The End of Defilements (Verse 293):** For those who are mindful, constantly focused on the body (through practices like mindfulness of breathing), and actively engage in what should be done, defilements come to an end. This emphasizes the power of mindfulness and diligent practice.

- **Metaphorical Killing (Verses 294-295):** These verses use strong metaphors of killing one\'s mother, father, kings, kingdom, and a tiger to represent the eradication of various attachments and defilements. This should not be interpreted literally as advocating violence. Instead, it symbolizes the complete overcoming of obstacles to liberation, including attachment to family, worldly power, and the five hindrances (sensual desire, ill-will, sloth-torpor, restlessness-worry, and doubt).

- **Mindfulness of the Triple Gem and the Body (Verses 296-300):** The disciples of the Buddha are always fully awakened because their mindfulness is constantly directed towards the Buddha, the Dhamma, the Sangha, and the body. These verses highlight the importance of constant mindfulness of key aspects of Buddhist practice. Verse 300 adds mindfulness of non-harming (ahimsa).

- **Delight in Cultivation (Verse 301):** The disciples of the Buddha find delight in cultivation (bhavana, meditation). This emphasizes the importance of meditative practice.

- **The Difficulties of Renunciation and Worldly Life (Verse 302):** Renunciation is difficult, finding joy in it is difficult, and household life is full of suffering. Association with disharmonious people is painful, as is being constantly beset by suffering. Therefore, one should strive to avoid being caught up in worldly affairs and the cycle of suffering. This encourages seeking liberation.

- **The Qualities of a Respected Person (Verse 303):** One endowed with faith, virtue, fame, and wealth is honored wherever they go. This highlights the positive qualities that garner respect.

- **The Virtue of the Virtuous (Verse 304):** The virtuous shine from afar, like the Himalayas, while the unvirtuous remain hidden, like arrows shot in the night. This emphasizes the clear distinction between virtuous and unvirtuous individuals.

- **The Solitary Practice (Verse 305):** One should live simply, with one seat and one bed, wandering alone and untiringly taming oneself, finding delight in solitude (the forest). This emphasizes the value of solitary practice for those dedicated to intensive spiritual development.

In summary, the Pakiṇṇaka Vagga covers a range of topics, all pointing towards the importance of:

- **Prioritizing Spiritual Growth:** Choosing greater spiritual happiness over lesser worldly pleasures.

- **Ethical Conduct:** Avoiding harm to others and engaging in virtuous actions.

- **Mindfulness and Awareness:** Maintaining constant awareness of one\'s thoughts, feelings, and actions.

- **Detachment:** Overcoming attachment to worldly things and defilements.

- **Diligent Practice:** Consistent effort on the path to liberation.

The chapter serves as a collection of diverse teachings that reinforce the core principles of Buddhism and guide practitioners on the path to enlightenment.

**1(306). Abhūtavādī nirayaṃ upeti, yo v'āpi katvā na karomī c'āha;\
Ubho'pi te pecca samā bhavanti, nihīnakammā manujā parattha.**

The speaker of falsehood goes to hell, as does he who, having done (a deed), says \"I did not do it\"; Both of them, after death, become equal, people of base deeds in the next world. 说妄语者堕地狱，或已作言「我无作」。 此二恶业者死后，他世同受地狱苦。

**2(307). Kāsāvakaṇṭhā bahavo, pāpadhammā asaññatā;\
Pāpā pāpehi kammehi, nirayaṃ te upapajjare.**

Many wearing the yellow robe are of evil character and unrestrained; The evil ones, by their evil deeds, are reborn in hell. 身披袈裟者众多，品行恶劣不自律；恶人恶行招恶果，终将堕落于地狱。

**3(308). Seyyo ayoguḷo bhutto, tatto aggisikhūpamo;\
Yañce bhuñjeyya dussīlo, raṭṭhapiṇḍaṃ asaññato.**

Better to eat a red-hot iron ball, like a blazing flame, Than that an immoral, unrestrained person should eat the alms of the land. 宁吞炽热铁丸，犹如烈焰灼烧；莫食国人供养，破戒放逸之徒。

**4(309). Cattāri ṭhānāni naro pamatto, āpajjati paradārūpasevī;\
Apuññalābhaṃ na nikāmaseyyaṃ, nindaṃ tatiyaṃ nirayaṃ catutthaṃ.**

A negligent man who associates with others\' wives encounters four situations: Acquisition of demerit, an uncomfortable bed, blame as the third, and hell as the fourth. 放逸之人恋他妻，当受四种苦果报；恶业缠身不安寝，恶名昭彰堕地狱。

**5(310). Apuññalābho ca gatī ca pāpikā, bhītassa bhītāya ratī ca thokikā;\
Rājā ca daṇḍaṃ garukaṃ paṇeti, tasmā naro paradāraṃ na seve.**

Acquisition of demerit and an evil destiny, and the pleasure of the fearful is fleeting; And the king imposes severe punishment; therefore, a man should not associate with another\'s wife. 邪淫恶报及恶趣，恐惧欢娱瞬即逝；君王重罚加其身，是故切莫恋他妻。

**6(311). Kuso yathā duggahito, hattham evānukantati;\
Sāmaññaṃ dupparāmaṭṭhaṃ, nirayāyupakaḍḍhati.**

Just as kusa grass, badly grasped, cuts the hand, So the life of samana, wrongly practiced, drags one down to hell. 茅草若执之不当，反将割伤其手；沙门若修行不善，终将堕入地狱。

**7(312). Yaṃ kiñci sithilaṃ kammaṃ, saṅkiliṭṭhañca yaṃ vataṃ;\
Saṅkassaraṃ brahmacariyaṃ, na taṃ hoti mahapphalaṃ.**

Whatever action is loose, and whatever vow is defiled, A corrupted brahmacariya---that is not of great fruit. 任何懈怠行，及染污戒行，或染污梵行，难获大果报。

**8(313). Kayirā ce kayirāthenaṃ, daḷham enaṃ parakkame;\
Sithilo hi paribbājo, bhiyyo ākirate rajaṃ.**

If something is to be done, one should do it and undertake it firmly; For a negligent monk's life only heaps up more impurities. 若有所作当力行，坚定不移务精进；懈怠的出家生活，将更多积聚污垢。

**9(314). Akataṃ dukkataṃ seyyo, pacchā tapati dukkataṃ;\
Katañca sukataṃ seyyo, yaṃ katvā n'ānutappati.**

Better is a bad deed not done, for a bad deed torments afterwards; And better is a good deed done, which having done one does not regret. 与其作恶后追悔，不如当初莫妄为；与其无为空懊恼，不如行善心安然。

**10(315). Nagaraṃ yathā paccantaṃ, guttaṃ santarabāhiraṃ;\
Evaṃ gopetha attānaṃ, khaṇo vo mā upaccagā;\
Khaṇātītā hi socanti, nirayamhi samappitā.**

Just as a border city is guarded both inside and out, So should you guard yourselves; let not the moment pass you by; For those who have let the moment pass grieve, having fallen into hell. 如边城内外严守，当护己身莫蹉跎；失时之人徒悲叹，堕落恶道受苦磨。

**11(316). Alajjitāye lajjanti, lajjitāye na lajjare;\
Micchādiṭṭhisamādānā, sattā gacchanti duggatiṃ.**

They are ashamed of what is not shameful, and they are not ashamed of what is shameful; Holding wrong views, beings go to a bad destination. 不当羞处反觉羞，应感惭愧却不羞； 执持邪见诸众生，终将堕落恶趣途。

**12(317). Abhaye ca bhayadassino, bhaye cābhayadassino;\
Micchādiṭṭhisamādānā, sattā gacchanti duggatiṃ.**

Seeing fear where there is no fear, and not seeing fear where there is fear; Holding wrong views, beings go to a bad destination. 无畏之处偏生畏，当畏之处反不惧； 执持邪见诸众生，终将堕落恶趣里。

**13(318). Avajje vajjamatino, vajje cāvajjadassino;\
Micchādiṭṭhisamādānā, sattā gacchanti duggatiṃ.**

Seeing blame in what is blameless, and seeing blamelessness in what is blameworthy; Holding wrong views, beings go to a bad destination. 无罪之处偏认罪，有罪之行反认清； 执持邪见诸众生，终将堕落恶趣行。

**14(319). Vajjañca vajjato ñatvā, avajjañca avajjato;\
Sammādiṭṭhisamādānā, sattā gacchanti suggatiṃ.**

Knowing a fault as a fault, and what is blameless as blameless; Holding right views, beings go to a good destination. 知罪为罪知无罪，明辨是非持正见； 奉行正见诸众生，终将往生善趣间。

### **Summary of Niraya Vagga**

Niraya Vagga focuses on the consequences of negative actions and wrong views, emphasizing that such actions lead to suffering in this life and future rebirths in lower realms (hell). It stresses the importance of ethical conduct, self-control, and right understanding. Negative actions, particularly lying, immorality, and wrong views, lead to suffering and rebirth in hell. Ethical conduct, self-control, and right understanding are essential for avoiding such consequences.

- **Lying and Denial (Verse 306):** Those who speak falsehoods and those who deny having committed wrong deeds both face negative consequences in the afterlife, becoming \"people of base deeds in the next world.\" This emphasizes the importance of truthfulness and taking responsibility for one\'s actions.

- **Immoral Monks (Verse 307):** Many who wear the yellow robe (symbol of renunciation) are of evil character and unrestrained. Their evil deeds lead to rebirth in hell. This warns against hypocrisy and the importance of genuine moral conduct for those who have taken vows.

- **The Consequences of Immorality (Verse 308):** It is better to swallow a red-hot iron ball than for an immoral and unrestrained person to consume the alms offered by the faithful. This emphasizes the heavy karmic burden of those who abuse the trust placed in them as religious figures.

- **The Consequences of Adultery (Verses 309-310):** A negligent person who engages in adultery faces four consequences: loss of merit, an uncomfortable life (literal and metaphorical), public blame, and rebirth in hell. These verses emphasize the negative consequences of sexual misconduct in both this life and future lives.

- **The Danger of Wrong Practice (Verse 311):** Just as poorly grasped kusa grass can cut the hand, wrongly practiced spiritual life drags one down to hell. This emphasizes the importance of proper guidance and diligent practice.

- **The Importance of Firm Resolve (Verses 312-313):** Loose actions, defiled vows, and a corrupted spiritual life do not yield great results. One should undertake actions with firm resolve; a negligent monastic life only accumulates more impurities. This emphasizes the need for strong determination and consistent effort in spiritual practice.

- **The Value of Good Deeds (Verse 314):** It is better not to commit a bad deed, as it causes remorse later. It is better to do a good deed, which brings no regret. This emphasizes the importance of performing wholesome actions.

- **Guarding Oneself (Verse 315):** Just as a border city is guarded inside and out, one should guard oneself against negative influences. Do not waste time, as those who do will grieve in hell. This emphasizes the importance of vigilance and making the most of one\'s time for spiritual practice.

- **Wrong Views and Their Consequences (Verses 316-319):** These verses describe the dangers of wrong views (micchādiṭṭhi). Those with wrong views are ashamed of what is not shameful and not ashamed of what is shameful. They see fear where there is no fear and vice versa. They see fault in what is faultless and vice versa. These wrong perceptions lead to bad destinations. Conversely, those with right views (sammādiṭṭhi) understand what is truly blameworthy and blameless, leading to good destinations. This emphasizes the crucial role of right understanding in avoiding suffering.

In summary, the Niraya Vagga warns against the dangers of negative actions, particularly those related to speech, morality, and wrong views. It emphasizes the following:

- **The Importance of Ethical Conduct:** Avoiding lying, immoral behavior, and other harmful actions.

- **The Consequences of Negative Actions:** These actions lead to suffering in this life and future rebirths in lower realms.

- **The Importance of Right Understanding:** Cultivating right views is essential for avoiding negative actions and attaining liberation.

- **The Need for Diligence and Self-Control:** Consistent effort and self-discipline are necessary for spiritual progress.

The chapter serves as a strong reminder of the importance of living a virtuous life and cultivating right understanding to avoid suffering and attain liberation.

[]{#_Toc188087636 .anchor}**23. Nāga Vagga -- Nāga 蛇靈**

**1(320). Ahaṃ nāgo'va saṅgāme, cāpāto patitaṃ saraṃ;\
Ativākyaṃ titikkhissaṃ, dussīlo hi bahujjano.**

As a nāga in battle withstands an arrow shot from a bow, So will I endure abusive speech, for many people are of bad conduct. 如蛇靈战场忍箭，我亦忍受恶言语；世人多有不善者，故当忍辱以自持。

**2(321). Dantaṃ nayanti samitiṃ, dantaṃ rājābhirūhati;\
Danto seṭṭho manussesu, yo'tivākyaṃ titikkhati.**

The tamed are led to the assembly, the tamed the king mounts; The tamed is best among humans, who endures abusive speech. 调御之士受敬重，引至集会君王乘；能忍辱人中最胜，调伏之人最为尊。

**3(322). Varam assatarā dantā, ājānīyā ca sindhavā;\
Kuñjarā ca mahānāgā, attadanto tato varaṃ.**

Excellent are tamed mules, and thoroughbred Sindh horses; And but better than these great nāgas is one who has tamed himself. 善调骡马良骏马，辛度名驹亦如是；如大蛇靈虽威猛，自我调伏最为胜。

**4(323). Na hi etehi yānehi, gaccheyya agataṃ disaṃ;\
Yathā'ttanā sudantena, danto dantena gacchati.**

Not indeed by these vehicles could one go to an unreached destination; But by one\'s own self well-tamed (self-discipline, self-control, and the purification of one\'s mind), one goes, tamed by the tamed (guidance of those who have already achieved self-mastery). 非凭外物达彼岸，善调己心方能行；依调伏者而调伏，方能抵达解脱境。

**5(324). Dhanapāla nāma kuñjaro, kaṭukabhedano dunnivārayo;\
Baddho kabalaṃ na bhuñjati, sumarati nāgavanassa kuñjaro.**

The nāga named Dhanapala, fierce in breaking, difficult to restrain, Though bound, does not eat his food, but remembers the nāga forest. 名唤财护之蛇靈，性情凶猛难阻挡；纵然身陷囹圄中，不食但忆蛇靈林。

**6(325). Middhī yadā hoti mahagghaso ca, niddāyitā samparivattasāyī;\
Mahāvarāho'va nivāpapuṭṭho, punappunaṃ gabbham upeti mando.**

When one is slothful and a glutton, given to sleep and rolling about in bed, Like a great boar nourished with fodder, that fool enters the womb again and again. 若人懈怠又贪食，嗜睡懒卧常翻侧；犹如饱饲大野猪，愚者轮回复轮回。

**7(326). Idaṃ pure cittam acāri cārikaṃ,yena'icchakaṃ yatthakāmaṃ yathā sukhaṃ; Tadajj'ahaṃ niggahessāmi yoniso, hatthippabhinnaṃ viya aṅkusaggaho.**

Formerly this mind wandered as it liked, where it wished, according to its pleasure; But now I will wisely restrain it, as a trainer with a hook restrains a rutting elephant. 此心往昔任驰骋，随心所欲逐诸乐；今以智慧悉调伏，如驯象师制狂象。

**8(327). Appamādaratā hotha, sacittam anurakkhatha;\
Duggā uddharath'attānaṃ, paṅke sanno'va kuñjaro.**

Take delight in zeal, guard your mind well; Draw yourselves out of bad states, like an elephant sunk in mud. 当乐热忱莫放逸,善护己心勿迷失;如象陷泥奋力拔,当自勉力出恶处。

**9(328). Sace labetha nipakaṃ sahāyaṃ, saddhiṃ caraṃ sādhuvihāridhīraṃ; Abhibhuyya sabbāni parissayāni, careyya ten'attamano satīmā.**

If one finds a wise companion, a fellow traveler, righteous and steadfast; Having overcome all dangers, one should live with such a one, joyful and mindful. 若得贤友同行路，品行端方又坚固；共克诸难心欢喜，正念相随乐相助。

**10(329). No ce labetha nipakaṃ sahāyaṃ, saddhiṃ caraṃ sādhuvihāridhīraṃ; Rājā'va raṭṭhaṃ vijitaṃ pahāya, eko care mātaṅgaraññ'eva nāgo.**

If one does not find a wise companion, a fellow traveler, righteous and steadfast; Like a king who has abandoned his conquered kingdom, one should live alone, like a nāga in the Mātaṅga forest. 若无贤友可同行，品行端方难觅寻；当如胜王舍国土，亦如孤蛇住深林。

**11(330). Ekassa caritaṃ seyyo, natthi bāle sahāyatā;\
Eko care na ca pāpāni kayirā, appossukko mātaṅgaraññ'eva nāgo.**

It is better to live alone; there is no companionship with a fool; One should live alone and do no evil, without concern, like a nāga in the Mātaṅga forest. 独处胜于交愚者，远离恶行当独行；无忧无虑如蛇靈，自在逍遥乐清宁。

**\**

**12(331). Atthamhi jātamhi sukhā sahāyā, tuṭṭhī sukhā yā itarītarena; Puññaṃ sukhaṃ jīvitasaṅkhayamhi, sabbassa dukkhassa sukhaṃ pahānaṃ.**

When a need arises, friends are a source of happiness; contentment is happiness with whatever one has; Merit is happiness at the end of life; the abandoning of all suffering (nibbana) is (the highest form of) happiness. 患难之时良友乐，随遇而安亦是乐；临终之时积德乐，断尽诸苦最胜乐。

**13(332). Sukhā matteyyatā loke, atho petteyyatā sukha;\
Sukhā sāmaññatā loke, atho brahmaññatā sukhā.**

Happy in this world is being devoted to one\'s mother, and also happy is being devoted to one\'s father; Happy in this world is the life of a samana, and also happy is the life of a brahmana. 世间孝母乐，孝父亦复然；沙门生活乐，婆罗门亦然。

**14(333). Sukhaṃ yāva jarā sīlaṃ, sukhā saddhā patiṭṭhitā;\
Sukho paññāya paṭilābho, pāpānaṃ akaraṇaṃ sukhaṃ.**

Good is (to keep) sīla until old age, good is faith when established; Good is the gaining of wisdom, good is the non-doing of evil. 持戒至老乐,信心坚定乐；获得智慧乐,诸恶不作乐。

### **Summary of Nāga Vagga**

Nāga Vagga uses the imagery of elephants, both wild and tamed, to illustrate the importance of self-control, endurance, and the benefits of associating with wise companions. It also touches upon the dangers of laziness, greed, and the value of virtuous conduct. Just as a wild elephant needs to be tamed and trained, so too does the human mind need to be disciplined and controlled. Self-mastery, through the practice of the Dhamma, is far superior to any other form of control or worldly achievement.

- **Enduring Abusive Speech (Verse 320):** Just as a nāga in battle endures arrows, one should endure abusive speech, as many people are of bad conduct. This emphasizes the importance of patience and forbearance.

- **The Value of Taming (Verse 321):** The tamed are respected and honored. The truly tamed person, who endures abusive speech, is the best among humans. This emphasizes the value of self-discipline and control.

- **Superiority of Self-Taming (Verse 322):** Tamed mules, thoroughbred horses, and even great nāgas are valuable, but far superior is one who has tamed themselves. This highlights the supreme importance of self-mastery over external accomplishments.

- **The Destination Reached Through Self-Taming (Verse 323):** External vehicles cannot lead to the unreached destination (Nibbana), but self-taming can. This reinforces the idea that inner transformation is the key to liberation.

- **The nāga's Longing for Freedom (Verse 324):** The nāga Dhanapala, though bound, remembers his forest home and refuses to eat. This metaphor illustrates the mind\'s natural longing for freedom from the bonds of attachment.

- **The Foolish Cycle of Rebirth (Verse 325):** One who is slothful, gluttonous, and given to sleep will be reborn repeatedly, like a well-fed boar returning to the womb. This warns against the dangers of laziness and indulgence.

- **Taming the Mind (Verse 326):** The mind, which formerly wandered freely, must now be wisely restrained, like a trainer controlling a rutting elephant with a hook. This emphasizes the need for conscious effort in controlling the mind.

- **Delight in Zeal and Mindfulness (Verse 327):** One should take delight in zeal (appamada, diligence) and guard one\'s mind well. One should strive to escape from bad states, like an elephant stuck in mud pulls itself out. This emphasizes the importance of vigilance and effort in overcoming negative states.

- **The Value of Wise Companions (Verses 328-329):** If one finds a wise and righteous companion, one should live with them joyfully and mindfully. If one does not find such a companion, one should live alone, like an nāga in the forest. This emphasizes the importance of good company or, if necessary, solitude.

- **The Benefits of Solitude (Verse 330):** It is better to live alone than to associate with fools. One should live alone, doing no evil, without concern, like an nāga in the forest. This reinforces the value of solitude for those who cannot find suitable companions.

- **Sources of Happiness (Verse 331):** Friends are a source of happiness in times of need, contentment is happiness with what one has, merit is happiness at the end of life, and the abandoning of all suffering (Nibbana) is the highest happiness. This summarizes the different types of happiness, culminating in the ultimate happiness of liberation.

- **Happiness in Relationships and Ascetic Life (Verses 332-333):** Happiness is found in devotion to parents, in the life of a samana (ascetic), and in the life of a brahmana (one who lives a holy life). Good is the practice of sila (virtue) until old age, good is established faith, good is the gaining of wisdom, and good is the non-doing of evil. These verses emphasize the value of ethical conduct, faith, and wisdom throughout life.

In summary, the Nāga Vagga uses the nāga and elephant as a powerful symbol of the untamed mind and the importance of self-control. It emphasizes the following:

- **Self-Control and Endurance:** These are essential qualities for spiritual progress.

- **The Importance of Wise Companions or Solitude:** Choose good company if possible, or solitude if necessary.

- **The Dangers of Laziness and Greed:** These hinder spiritual development.

- **The Value of Ethical Conduct, Faith, and Wisdom:** These are the foundations of a happy and meaningful life.

The chapter encourages listeners to tame their minds, live virtuously, and strive for liberation from suffering.

[]{#_Toc188087638 .anchor}**24. Taṇhā Vagga -- Craving 渴爱**

**1(334). Manujassa pamattacārino, taṇhā vaḍḍhati māluvā viya;\
So plavatī hurāhuraṃ, phalam icchaṃ 'va vanasmi vānaro.**

The craving of one who lives negligently grows like a creeper; He wanders from here to there, like a monkey in the forest seeking fruit. 放逸之人之渴爱，犹如蔓草般滋长；彼如林中觅果猴，四处漂荡无安详。

**2(335). Yaṃ esā sahatī jammī, taṇhā loke visattikā;\
Sokā tassa pavaḍḍhanti, abhivaṭṭhaṃ 'va bīraṇaṃ.**

Whomever is overcome by this wretched, clinging worldly craving, his sorrows increase, like Bīraṇa grass after the rains. 若世间卑劣之渴爱，征服于人令其执着，则其忧愁日渐增长，如雨后比拉那草盛。

**3(336). Yo c'etaṃ sahatī jammiṃ, taṇhaṃ loke duraccayaṃ;\
Sokā tamhā papatanti, udabindu'va pokkharā.**

Whoever overcomes this wretched, clinging worldly craving, so difficult to overcome, From him sorrows fall away, like a water drop from a lotus leaf. 若人能胜此世间，卑劣难伏之渴爱， 则其忧愁尽脱落，如水珠落于荷叶。

**4(337). Taṃ vo vadāmi bhaddaṃ vo, yāvant'ettha samāgatā;\
Taṇhāya mūlaṃ khanatha, usīrattho'va bīraṇaṃ;\
Mā vo naḷaṃ 'va soto'va, māro bhañji punappunaṃ.**

Therefore, I say to you, blessings be upon all who are assembled here; Dig up the root of craving, as one seeking *usīra* (root) digs up Bīraṇa (grass); Lest Māra break you again and again, like a torrent breaks a reed. 我今告诫诸位，凡此集会皆吉祥；当掘渴爱之根源，如人求香掘香草；莫为死神频频摧，如苇遇流易折伤。

**5(338). Yathā'pi mūle anupaddave daḷhe, chinno'pi rukkho punareva rūhati; Evam pi taṇhānusaye anūhate, nibbattatī dukkham idaṃ punappunaṃ.**

Just as a tree, though cut down, grows again if its root is undamaged and strong, So too, if the underlying tendency of craving is not uprooted, this suffering arises again and again. 犹如树木虽被伐，根深未损仍复生；如是渴爱若未断，苦果亦将复重重。

**\**

**6(339). Yassa chattiṃsatī sotā, manāpassavanā bhusā;\
Māhā vahanti duddiṭṭhiṃ, saṅkappā rāganissitā.**

Whose thirty-six streams flow strongly towards pleasurable objects; Strong currents carry them to wrong views, their thoughts rooted in passion. 若人三十六欲流，奔流悦意诸境界；强大欲流引邪见，意念皆由贪欲生。

**7(340). Savanti sabbadhī sotā, latā uppajja tiṭṭhati;\
Tañca disvā lataṃ jātaṃ, mūlaṃ paññāya chindatha.**

Streams (flow of sensory input and the arising of desires) flow everywhere, and a creeper (the growth and entrenchment of craving) arises and remains; Having seen that creeper that has arisen, cut its root with wisdom. 欲流无处不奔腾，如藤蔓生缠绕生；见此藤蔓已滋长，当以智慧断其根。

**8(341). Saritāni sinehitāni ca, somanassāni bhavanti jantuno;\
Te sātasitā sukhesino, te ve jātijarūpagā narā.**

Attachments and affections, and pleasant feelings arise for beings; Those addicted to pleasure, seeking pleasure, those people indeed are subject to birth and old age. 众生常有诸执着，爱恋喜悦亦如是；耽溺于乐复求乐，彼等实受生老苦。

**9(342). Tasiṇāya purakkhatā pajā, parisappanti saso'va bandhito;\
Saṃyojanasaṅgasattā, dukkham upenti punappunaṃ cirāya.**

Driven by craving, beings wander about like a captured hare; Bound by the fetters of attachment, they undergo suffering again and again for a long time. 众生为渴爱所驱使，如受束缚之兔游荡；为诸结缚所缠缚，长久屡受诸痛苦。

**10(343). Tasiṇāya purakkhatā pajā, parisappanti saso'va bandhito;\
Tasmā tasiṇaṃ vinodaye, ākaṅkhanta virāgam attano.**

Driven by craving, beings wander about like a captured hare; Therefore, one should dispel craving, desiring detachment for oneself. 众生为渴爱所驱使，如陷樊笼之兔游荡；是故当除此渴爱，希求自身得离欲。

**11(344). Yo nibbanatho vanādhimutto, vanamutto vanam eva dhāvati;\
Taṃ puggalam etha passatha, mutto bandhanam eva dhāvati.**

He who, having renounced the world, returns to it; Come, behold that person: though freed, he runs back to bondage. 若人已离于林野，复又驰还于林野；汝等来看此等人，虽得解脱复奔缚。

**12(345). Na taṃ daḷhaṃ bandhanam āhu dhīrā, yadāyasaṃ dārujapabbajañca; Sārattarattā maṇikuṇḍalesu, puttesu dāresu ca yā apekhā.**

The wise do not call that a strong bond which is made of iron, wood, or reeds; The attachment and longing for jewels and ornaments, for sons and wives --- *that* is the strong bond. 智者不言铁木草，是为坚牢之系缚；耽溺珍宝恋子妻，此乃真固之系缚。

**13(346). Etaṃ daḷhaṃ bandhanam āhu dhīrā, ohārinaṃ sithilaṃ duppamuñcaṃ, Etam pi chetvāna paribbajanti, anapekkhino kāmasukhaṃ pahāya.**

The wise say that this is a strong bond: dragging down, slack, and difficult to release. Even this they cut and go forth (renounce the world), ), without longing (for sensual pleasures), and relinquishing sensual pleasures. 智者谓此坚固缚，能使人堕落，看似松弛实难解脱；纵然如此亦当断，舍弃欲乐而出家。

**14(347). Ye rāgarattānupatanti sotaṃ, sayaṃ kataṃ makkaṭako'va jālaṃ;\
Etam pi chetvāna vajanti dhīrā, anapekkhino sabbadukkhaṃ pahāya.**

Those who are infatuated with passion fall into the stream, like a spider into its own web; Having cut this (web), the wise go (renounce the world), without longing (for sensual pleasures), having abandoned all sufferings. 耽溺于欲者，没入欲流中，如蛛自缠网；智者断此网，舍弃一切苦，无求而前行。

**15(348). Muñca pure muñca pacchato, majjhe muñca bhavassa pāragū;\
Sabbattha vimuttamānaso, na puna jātijaraṃ upehisi.**

Let go of the past, let go of the future, let go of the present; having gone beyond (worldly) existence, With a mind completely liberated in all respects (from this world), you will not again undergo birth and old age. 舍弃过去，舍弃未来，亦舍弃现在；已超越世间存在，心已解脱世间一切，不再重受生老苦。

**16(349). Vitakkapamathitassa jantuno, tibbarāgassa subhānupassino;\
Bhiyyo taṇhā pavaḍḍhati, esa kho daḷhaṃ karoti bandhanaṃ.**

For a being whose thoughts are agitated, who has intense passion, and who contemplates the attractive, Craving increases all the more; this indeed makes the bond strong. 若人心为妄念扰，又复耽溺于美妙，彼之渴爱益增长，此实能坚固其缚。

**\**

**17(350). Vitakkūpasame ca yo rato, asubhaṃ bhāvayate sadā sato;\
Esa kho byantikāhiti, esa checchati mārabandhanaṃ.**

He who delights in the calming of thoughts, and who, ever mindful, cultivates contemplation of the impure (asubha); He indeed will make an end (of craving), he will cut the bonds of Māra. 若人乐于息止妄念，常以正念修不净观，彼必能灭诸烦恼，亦将斩断死神缚。

**18(351). Niṭṭhaṅgato asantāsī, vītataṇho anaṅgaṇo;\
Acchindi bhavasallāni, antimo'yaṃ samussayo.**

He who has reached completion, is fearless, free from craving, and without defilements; He has cut the arrows of existence; this is his last accumulation (of kamma). 已达究竟无畏惧，离欲无垢清净者；已断轮回诸箭矢，此乃最后之业聚。

**19(352). Vītataṇho anādāno, niruttipadakovido;\
Akkharānaṃ sannipātaṃ, jaññā pubbaparāni ca;\
Sa ve "antimasārīro, mahāpañño mahāpuriso"ti vuccati.**

He who is free from craving and attachment, skilled in the meaning of words and texts, Who understands the arrangement of syllables, both what precedes and what follows; He, indeed, is said to be \"one with his last body, a person of great wisdom, a great being.\" 离欲不执通词义，明了字句前后理；彼诚名为最后身，大慧大士世所称。

**20(353). Sabbābhibhū sabbavidū'ham asmi, sabbesu dhammesu anūpāḷitto; Sabbañjaho taṇhakkhaye vimutto, sayaṃ abhiññāya kam uddiseyyaṃ.**

I am the conqueror of all, the knower of all, to all things unattached; Having renounced all, liberated through the destruction of craving, having directly known (the truth) by myself, to whom should I point (as my teacher)? 我征服一切，遍知一切法，不染着一切； 已舍离一切，灭尽渴爱得解脱，我已亲证，自证谁称师？

**21(354). Sabbadānaṃ dhammadānaṃ jināti, sabbaṃ rasaṃ dhammaraso jināti; Sabbaṃ ratiṃ dhammaratī jināti, taṇhakkhayo sabbadukkhaṃ jināti.**

The dāna of the Dhamma surpasses all dānas; the taste of the Dhamma surpasses all tastes; The delight in the Dhamma surpasses all delights; the destruction of craving surpasses all suffering. 诸布施中法施胜，诸味之中法味胜，诸乐之中法乐胜，灭尽渴爱胜诸苦。

**\**

**22(355). Hananti bhogā dummedhaṃ, no ve pāragavesino;\
Bhogataṇhāya dummedho, hanti aññe'va attanā.**

Wealth harms the unwise, but not those who seek the far shore; Through craving for wealth, the unwise harms himself as if he were harming others. 财富害愚人，不害求彼岸者；愚人因贪财，自害如害人。

**23(356). Tiṇadosāni khettāni, rāgadosā ayaṃ pajā;\
Tasmā hi vītarāgesu, dinnaṃ hoti mahapphalaṃ.**

Fields are ruined by weeds; mankind is ruined by rāga and dosa; Therefore, indeed, what is given to those free from rāga and dosa is of great fruit (highly meritorious). 田地为杂草所害，众生为贪欲所害；是故于离贪欲者，将得大果报。

**24(357). Tiṇadosāni khettāni, dosadosā ayaṃ pajā;\
Tasmā hi vītadosesu, dinnaṃ hoti mahapphalaṃ.**

Fields are ruined by weeds; mankind is ruined by dosa; Therefore, indeed, what is given to those free from dosa is of great fruit. 田地为杂草所害，众生为嗔心所害；是故于离嗔心者，将得大果报。

**25(358). Tiṇadosāni khettāni, mohadosā ayaṃ pajā;\
Tasmā hi vītamohesu, dinnaṃ hoti mahapphalaṃ.**

Fields are ruined by weeds; mankind is ruined by moha; Therefore, indeed, what is given to those free from moha is of great fruit. 田地为杂草所害，众生为痴心所害；是故于离痴心者，将得大果报。

**26(359). Tiṇadosāni khettāni, icchādosā ayaṃ pajā;\
Tasmā hi vigaticchesu, dinnaṃ hoti mahapphalaṃ.**

Fields are ruined by weeds; mankind is ruined by icchā (desire); Therefore, indeed, what is given to those free from icchā is of great fruit. 田地为杂草所害，众生为欲望所害；是故于离欲望者，将得大果报。

### **Summary of Taṇhā Vagga**

Taṇhā Vagga focuses on the pervasive nature of craving (taṇhā) and its role in perpetuating suffering. It emphasizes the importance of eradicating craving to achieve liberation. Craving is the root cause of samsāra, an involuntary cycle of birth and death in this world. By understanding its nature, digging up its root, and cultivating detachment, one can attain liberation from the cycle of rebirth.

- **The Growth of Craving (Verse 334):** Craving grows like a creeper in a negligent person, leading them to wander aimlessly, like a monkey seeking fruit in a forest. This highlights the insidious nature of craving and the importance of vigilance.

- **Craving and Sorrow (Verses 335-336):** Those overcome by worldly craving experience increasing sorrow, like grass flourishing after rain. Conversely, those who overcome craving find their sorrows falling away, like water drops from a lotus leaf. This illustrates the direct link between craving and suffering, and the possibility of liberation through overcoming it.

- **Uprooting Craving (Verse 337):** The Buddha urges the assembly to dig up the root of craving, like one digging up the roots of Bīraṇa grass. This emphasizes the need for a complete eradication of craving, not just temporary suppression. He warns against being repeatedly broken by Māra (death/temptation), like a reed broken by a torrent.

- **The Re-arising of Suffering (Verse 338):** Just as a cut tree can grow again if its roots are intact, suffering arises again and again if the underlying tendency of craving is not uprooted. This emphasizes the importance of addressing the root cause of suffering.

- **The Streams of Craving (Verses 339-340):** The thirty-six streams of craving flow towards pleasurable objects, leading to wrong views and thoughts rooted in passion. These streams must be seen and their root cut with wisdom. This highlights the diverse ways craving manifests and the importance of wisdom in overcoming it.

- **Attachment and Affection (Verse 341):** Attachments, affections, and pleasant feelings bind beings to the cycle of birth and old age. This points out that even seemingly positive emotions can be a source of attachment and suffering.

- **The Wanderings Driven by Craving (Verses 342-343):** Driven by craving, beings wander like captured hares, bound by the fetters of attachment, experiencing repeated suffering. Therefore, craving must be dispelled to attain detachment. This emphasizes the restless and unsatisfying nature of a life driven by craving.

- **Returning to Bondage (Verse 344):** One who renounces the world but returns to it is like someone who, though freed, runs back to bondage. This warns against half-hearted renunciation and the pull of worldly attachments.

- **The Strong Bond of Attachment (Verses 345-347):** The wise do not consider physical bonds (iron, wood) the strongest, but rather the attachment to possessions, loved ones, and sensual pleasures. These attachments are difficult to release and drag one down into suffering. The wise cut these bonds and go forth, abandoning all suffering. The image of a spider caught in its own web is used to illustrate how attachment traps individuals.

- **Letting Go of Past, Present, and Future (Verse 348):** To transcend existence, one must let go of attachment to the past, present, and future, achieving complete liberation. This emphasizes the need for complete detachment from the temporal world.

- **The Increase of Craving Through Wrong Contemplation (Verses 349-350):** For those with agitated thoughts, intense passion, and who dwell on attractive things, craving increases, strengthening the bonds of suffering. Conversely, those who calm their thoughts and contemplate the impure (asubha) will end craving and cut the bonds of Māra. This highlights the importance of right contemplation and mental discipline.

- **The Characteristics of the Liberated One (Verses 351-352):** The liberated one is fearless, free from craving and defilements, having cut the arrows of existence. They are skilled in the meaning of words and texts and understand the nature of reality. They are known as \"one with their last body,\" a person of great wisdom and a great being. This describes the qualities of an enlightened individual.

- **The Superiority of the Dhamma (Verses 353-354):** The Buddha declares himself the conqueror of all, the knower of all, unattached to all things, having achieved liberation through the destruction of craving. The gift of the Dhamma surpasses all gifts, its taste surpasses all tastes, its delight surpasses all delights, and the destruction of craving surpasses all suffering. This emphasizes the supreme value of the Dhamma.

- **The Harm of Wealth (Verses 355-359):** Wealth harms the unwise, but not those seeking liberation. Craving for wealth harms oneself. Fields are ruined by weeds, and mankind is ruined by rāga (passion/lust), dosa (hatred/aversion), moha (delusion/ignorance), and icchā (desire/longing). Therefore, giving to those free from these defilements yields great merit. This emphasizes the dangers of attachment to material possessions and the importance of giving to those who have overcome defilements.

In summary, the Taṇhā Vagga provides a comprehensive analysis of craving, its consequences, and the path to its eradication. It emphasizes the need for wisdom, effort, detachment, and right contemplation to achieve liberation from suffering.

[]{#_Toc188087640 .anchor}**25. Bhikkhu Vagga -- Bhikkhu 比丘**

**1(360). Cakkhunā saṃvaro sādhu, sādhu sotena saṃvaro;\
Ghāṇena saṃvaro sādhu, sādhu jivhāya saṃvaro.**

Good is restraint in the eye; good is restraint in the ear; good is restraint in the nose; good is restraint in the tongue. 眼摄护为善，耳摄护为善；鼻摄护为善，舌摄护为善。

**2(361). Kāyena saṃvaro sādhu, sādhu vācāya saṃvaro;\
Manasā saṃvaro sādhu, sādhu sabbattha saṃvaro;\
Sabbattha saṃvuto bhikkhu, sabbadukkhā pamuccati.**

Good is restraint in deed; good is restraint in speech; good is restraint in will; good is restraint in everything. The bhikkhu, restrained in everything, is freed from all impurities. 身摄护为善，语摄护为善；意摄护为善，一切处摄护为善；一切处皆摄护之比丘，得脱一切诸苦。

**3(362). Hatthasaṃyato pādasaṃyato, vācāyasaṃyato saṃyatuttamo;\
Ajjhattarato samāhito, eko santusito tam āhu bhikkhuṃ.**

Restrained in hand and foot, restrained in speech, the most restrained; Delighting in inner peace, composed, solitary, content---him they call a bhikkhu. 手足善摄护，言语善摄护，最上摄护者； 乐于内寂静，心定独知足，彼即名比丘。

**4(363). Yo mukhasaṃyato bhikkhu, mantabhāṇī anuddhato;\
Atthaṃ dhammañca dīpeti, madhuraṃ tassa bhāsitaṃ.**

The bhikkhu who is restrained in speech, who speaks wisely and is not arrogant; He explains the meaning and the Dhamma, sweet is his speech. 若比丘善护其口，言谈智慧不骄慢；阐释义理及佛法，其语甘美悦人心。

**5(364). Dhammārāmo dhammarato, dhammaṃ anuvicintayaṃ;\
Dhammaṃ anussaraṃ bhikkhu, saddhammā na parihāyati.**

He who delights in the Dhamma, who is devoted to the Dhamma, constantly reflecting on the Dhamma; A bhikkhu who remembers the Dhamma does not fall away from the true Dhamma. 乐于佛法，欣乐于法，常思惟法； 比丘常忆念法，不从正法退失。

**6(365). Salābhaṃ nātimaññeyya, n'āññesaṃ pihayaṃ care;\
Aññesaṃ pihayaṃ bhikkhu, samādhiṃ n'ādhigacchati.**

One should not despise one\'s own gains, nor live desiring the gains of others; A bhikkhu who desires the gains of others does not attain samādhi. 莫轻己所得，莫羡他人有；比丘羡他人，则不得入定。

**7(366). Appalābho'pi ce bhikkhu, salābhaṃ n'ātimaññati;\
Taṃ ve devā pasaṃsanti, suddhājīviṃ atanditaṃ.**

Even if a monk receives little, he does not despise what he has received; Him indeed the devas praise, one who lives purely and is untiring. 纵使比丘所得少，亦不轻视己所得；诸天咸赞彼，清净活命不懈怠。

**8(367). Sabbaso nāmarūpasmiṃ, yassa natthi mamāyitaṃ;\
Asatā ca na socati, sa ve "bhikkhū"ti vuccati.**

He who has no sense of \"mine\" with regard to all nāmarūpa; And who does not grieve for what is unreal, he is truly called a \"bhikkhu.\" 一切名色法中，若人没有我执；不为非真悲伤，彼实名为比丘。

**9(368). Mettāvihārī yo bhikkhu, pasanno Buddhasāsane;\
Adhigacche padaṃ santaṃ, saṅkhārūpasamaṃ sukhaṃ.**

The bhikkhu who dwells in loving-kindness, who has confidence in the Buddha\'s teaching; May he attain the peaceful state, the happiness of the subsiding of formations. 若有比丘住慈心，信乐佛陀之教法；当可证得寂静地，和诸行止息之乐。

**10(369). Siñca bhikkhu imaṃ nāvaṃ, sittā te lahum essati;\
Chetvā rāgaṃ ca dosañca, tato nibbānam ehisi.**

Empty this boat, O bhikkhu! Emptied, it will move swiftly; Having cut off rāga and dosa, then you will reach Nibbana. 比丘！清空此船，清空后，它将迅速前行；断除贪与嗔，你将去到涅槃。

**11(370). Pañca chinde pañca jahe, pañca c'uttari bhāvaye;\
Pañca saṅgātigo bhikkhu, "oghatiṇṇo"ti vuccati.**

Five he should cut off, five he should abandon, and five more he should cultivate; A bhikkhu who has overcome five attachments is called \"one who has crossed the flood.\" 应断五支，应舍五支，更应修习五支；超越五结之比丘，被称为"已度瀑流者"。

 **\"Five he should cut off\":** These are the five lower fetters (*orambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni*):

> 1**. Sakkāyadiṭṭhi:** Body-view, belief in the body (as self).

- **Sakāya:** Of the body, belonging to the body.

- **Diṭṭhi:** View, belief, doctrine.

> Attachment to the physical body as being the \"self.\" the body is often the primary basis upon which people construct their sense of self. People identify with their physical appearance, their physical sensations, and their physical capabilities. This identification with the body can lead to attachment, craving for physical pleasures, fear of aging and death, and other forms of suffering.
>
> 2\. **Vicikicchā:** Doubt (especially about the Buddha, Dhamma, and Sangha).
>
> 3\. **Sīlabbataparāmāsa:** Clinging to rites and rituals (mere rituals can lead to liberation).
>
> 4\. **Kāmarāga:** Sensual desire.
>
> 5\. **Vyāpāda:** Ill-will, hatred.

 **\"five he should abandon\":** The five higher fetters (*uddhambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni*):

1.  **Rūparāga:** Attachment to the rūpa form (the fine-material).

2.  **Arūparāga:** Attachment to the arūpa formless (the immaterial).

3.  **Māna:** Conceit, pride.

4.  **Uddhacca:** Restlessness, agitation.

5.  **Avijjā:** Ignorance.

 **\"and five more he should cultivate\":** The five spiritual faculties (*pañcindriyāni*):

1.  **Saddhā:** Faith.

2.  **Viriya:** Energy/effort.

3.  **Sati:** Mindfulness.

4.  **Samādhi:** Concentration.

5.  **Paññā:** Wisdom.

**12(371). Jhāya bhikkhu mā pāmado, mā te kāmaguṇe ramessu cittaṃ;\
Mā lohaguḷaṃ gilī pamatto, mā kandi "dukkham idan"ti ḍayhamāno.**

Meditate, O bhikkhu! Do not be negligent, do not let your mind delight in sensual pleasures; Do not, being negligent, swallow a red-hot iron ball, lest you cry out, \"This is suffering,\" while being burned. 比丘！当勤禅修莫放逸，莫令心沉迷五欲； 莫因放逸而吞热铁丸，受焚烧之苦而哭喊"此是苦！"

**13(372). Natthi jhānaṃ apaññassa, paññā natthi ajhāyato;\
Yamhi jhānañca paññā ca, sa ve nibbānasantike.**

There is no concentration in one who lacks wisdom, nor is there wisdom in him who lacks concentration. In whom are both concentration and wisdom, he is truly near nibbāna. 无慧者无定，无定者无慧。 兼具定与慧，彼实近涅槃。

**14(373). Suññāgāraṃ paviṭṭhassa, santacittassa bhikkhuno;\
Amānusī rati hoti, sammā dhammaṃ vipassato.**

For a monk who has entered a secluded dwelling, whose mind is tranquil; There is a non-human (transcendent) joy, for one who rightly contemplates the Dhamma. 若比丘入僻静处，心寂静；如实观照正法，则有超人之喜悦。

**15(374). Yato yato sammasati, khandhānaṃ udayabbayaṃ;\
Labhatī pīti pāmojjaṃ, amataṃ taṃ vijānataṃ.**

Whenever one thoroughly understands the arising and passing away of the aggregates; He obtains joy and gladness; that is the deathless state for those who understand. 无论何时如实观照五蕴的生灭；便能获得喜悦与欢欣，此即智者所证的不死之境。

**16(375). Tatrāyam ādi bhavati, idha paññassa bhikkhuno;\
Indriyagutti santuṭṭhī, pātimokkhe ca saṃvaro.**

This is the beginning here for the wise bhikkhu: Guarding of the senses, contentment, and restraint in the Patimokkha. 于此，智慧比丘之修行开始：守护诸根，知足常乐，自制持守比丘戒律。

**17(376). Mitte bhajassu kalyāṇe, suddh'ājīve atandite;\
Paṭisanthāravuty'assa, ācārakusalo siyā;\
Tato pāmojjabahulo, dukkhass'antaṃ karissati.**

Associate with good friends, who are pure in livelihood and diligent; He should have friendly conduct and be skilled in proper behavior; Then, abounding in joy, he will bring about the end of suffering. 应亲近善良、以清净为业且精进的朋友；应具友善行为，并善于待人接物； 如此便能常怀欢悦，终将断除痛苦。

**18(377). Vassikā viya pupphāni, maddavāni pamuñcati;\
Evaṃ rāgañca dosañca, vippamuñcetha bhikkhavo.**

Just as jasmine flowers shed their withered flowers, So should you free yourselves from rāga and dosa, O bhikkhus! 犹如雨季的花朵脱落其枯萎的花瓣， 比丘们，你们也应如此舍离贪欲与嗔恚。

**19(378). Santakāyo santavāco, santavā susamāhito;\
Vantalokāmiso bhikkhu, "upasanto"ti vuccati.**

He whose body is tranquil, whose speech is tranquil, who is tranquil and well-composed; A monk who has rejected worldly desires is called \"one who is calmed.\" 身语寂静，内心寂静，善于摄心；舍离世间欲的比丘，被称为"寂静者"。

**20(379). Attanā coday'attānaṃ, paṭimaṃsetha attanā;\
So attagutto satimā, sukhaṃ bhikkhu vihāhisi.**

Urge your self by your self, examine your self by your self; He who is self-guarded and mindful, (self-exhortation, self-examination, and self-reliance), O bhikkhu, you will live happily. 当以自身策励自身，当以自身反省自身；彼能自我守护且具正念者，比丘，你将安乐地生活。

**21(380). Attā hi attano nātho, attā hi attano gati;\
Tasmā saṃyamamattānaṃ, assaṃ bhadraṃ 'va vāṇijo.**

The self indeed is the protector of oneself, the self indeed is one\'s own refuge; Therefore, control yourself as a merchant controls a good horse. 自我是自我的救护者，自我是自我的皈依；因此，应当像商人调御良马一样调御自我。

**22(381). Pāmojjabahulo bhikkhu, pasanno buddhasāsane;\
Adhigacche padaṃ santaṃ, saṅkhārūpasamaṃ sukhaṃ.**

A bhikkhu abounding in joy, who is pleased in the Buddha\'s teaching; Would attain the peaceful state, the happiness of the stilling of formations. 常怀欢喜的比丘，信受佛陀的教导；将证寂静的境界，获诸行止息之乐。

**23(382). Yo have daharo bhikkhu, yuñjati Buddhasāsane;\
So'maṃ lokaṃ pabhāseti, abbhā mutto'va candimā.**

Whatever young bhikkhu applies himself to the Buddha\'s teaching; He illuminates this world, like the moon released from a cloud. 若有年轻的比丘，精勤修习佛陀的教法；他便能照耀这个世间，犹如从云雾中脱离的明月。

### **Summary of Bhikkhu Vagga**

Bhikkhu Vagga focuses on the qualities and practices of a true bhikkhu (monk), emphasizing self-control, mindfulness, contentment, and devotion to the Dhamma. True bhikkhuhood is not merely about wearing robes but about cultivating inner qualities such as restraint of the senses, contentment, devotion to the Dhamma, and self-mastery.

- **Restraint of the Senses (Verses 360-362):** Restraint of the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, speech, and mind is essential. A bhikkhu restrained in all these aspects is freed from all suffering. The ideal bhikkhu is described as restrained in hand and foot (actions), restrained in speech, delighting in inner peace, composed, solitary, and content.

- **Wise and Sweet Speech (Verse 363):** A bhikkhu should be restrained in speech, speaking wisely and without arrogance, explaining the meaning and the Dhamma with sweet words.

- **Devotion to the Dhamma (Verse 364):** A bhikkhu should delight in the Dhamma, be devoted to it, constantly reflect on it, and remember it, thereby not falling away from the true Dhamma.

- **Contentment and Non-Covetousness (Verses 365-367):** One should not despise one\'s own gains nor desire the gains of others. A bhikkhu who desires others\' gains does not attain concentration (samādhi). Even with little, a bhikkhu should be content; such a pure and untiring person is praised by the devas. One who has no sense of \"mine\" regarding nāmarūpa (name and form/mind and body) and does not grieve for what is unreal is truly called a bhikkhu.

- **Dwelling in Loving-Kindness (Verse 368):** A bhikkhu who dwells in loving-kindness and has confidence in the Buddha\'s teaching will attain the peaceful state and the happiness of the subsiding of formations (saṅkhārūpasamaṃ sukhaṃ).

- **Emptying the Boat (Verse 369):** The metaphor of emptying a boat is used to illustrate the need to eliminate rāga (passion/lust) and dosa (hatred/aversion) to reach Nibbana quickly.

- **Cutting Off, Abandoning, and Cultivating Five (Verse 370):** A bhikkhu is described as \"one who has crossed the flood\" by cutting off five lower fetters (attachment to self, doubt, clinging to rituals, sensual desire, ill-will), abandoning five higher fetters (attachment to form and formless realms, conceit, restlessness, ignorance), and cultivating five spiritual faculties (faith, effort, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom).

- **The Dangers of Negligence and Sensual Pleasures (Verse 371):** Bhikkhus are urged to meditate and not be negligent or delight in sensual pleasures. Negligence is likened to swallowing a red-hot iron ball, leading to suffering.

- **The Interdependence of Wisdom and Concentration (Verse 372):** There is no concentration without wisdom, and no wisdom without concentration. One who possesses both is near Nibbana.

- **The Joy of Solitude and Contemplation (Verses 373-374):** A bhikkhu who enters a secluded dwelling with a tranquil mind experiences transcendent joy through rightly contemplating the Dhamma, especially the arising and passing away of the aggregates (khandhas). Understanding this leads to joy, gladness, and the deathless state.

- **The Beginning of the Bhikkhu\'s Practice (Verse 375):** The beginning of a wise bhikkhu\'s practice involves guarding the senses, contentment, and restraint according to the Patimokkha (monastic rules).

- **Associating with Good Friends (Verse 376):** Bhikkhus should associate with good, pure, and diligent friends, have friendly conduct, and be skilled in proper behavior. This leads to abundant joy and the end of suffering.

- **Shedding Defilements (Verse 377):** Just as jasmine flowers shed their withered blossoms, bhikkhus should shed rāga (passion/lust) and dosa (hatred/aversion).

- **The Calmed Bhikkhu (Verse 378):** A bhikkhu whose body, speech, and mind are tranquil and who has rejected worldly desires is called \"one who is calmed.\"

- **Self-Exhortation and Self-Reliance (Verses 379-380):** One should urge and examine oneself. Self-reliance and mindfulness lead to a happy life. The self is one\'s own protector and refuge; therefore, one should control oneself like a merchant controls a good horse.

- **Joy and the Stilling of Formations (Verse 381):** A bhikkhu filled with joy and faith in the Buddha\'s teachings will attain the peaceful state and the happiness of the stilling of formations.

- **The Young Bhikkhu\'s Illumination (Verse 382):** A young bhikkhu who diligently applies themselves to the Buddha\'s teachings illuminates the world like the moon freed from clouds.

In summary, the Bhikkhu Vagga provides a comprehensive guide for monastic life, emphasizing inner transformation through self-discipline, mindfulness, wisdom, and devotion to the Dhamma. It highlights the importance of ethical conduct, contentment, and the pursuit of liberation from worldly suffering.

[]{#_Toc188087642 .anchor}**26. Brāhmaṇa Vagga -- Brāhmana 梵人**

**1(383). Chinda sotaṃ parakkamma, kāme panuda Brāhmaṇa;\
Saṅkhārānaṃ khayaṃ ñatvā, akataññū'si Brāhmaṇa.**

Striving, cut off the stream, drive away sensual pleasures, O brāhmana; Having known the destruction of formations, you are one who knows the uncreated, O brāhmana. 努力断除欲流，驱除诸欲，婆罗门！了知诸行消灭，你便证知无为法，婆罗门！

**2(384). Yadā dvayesu dhammesu, pāragū hoti brāhmaṇo;\
Atha'ssa sabbe saṃyogā, atthaṃ gacchanti jānato.**

When a brāhmana is going to the other shore in regard to two dhammas (this shore and other shore); Then all his bonds go to an end, for the one who knows. 对于二法，婆罗门选择去往彼岸；对于了知者而言，他的一切系缚都灭尽。

**3(385). Yassa pāraṃ apāraṃ vā, pārāpāraṃ vā vijjati;\
Vītaddaraṃ visaṃyuttaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who knows the other shore, this shore, or both shores, who is free from craving and unbound --- him I call a brāhmana. 此岸与彼岸，两岸皆洞悉，离贪无系缚，是谓婆罗门。

**4(386). Jhāyiṃ virajam āsīnaṃ, katakiccaṃ** **anāsavaṃ;\
Uttamatthaṃ anuppattaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

Him who is meditative, stainless, secluded, who has done his duty, free from influxes; Having attained the highest goal, him I call a brāhmana. 禅思无垢安住，应办已办无漏； 已证得至上者，我称他婆罗门。

**5(387). Divā tapati ādicco, rattimābhāti candimā;\
Sannaddho khattiyo tapati, jhāyī tapati brāhmaṇo;\
Atha sabbam ahorattiṃ, buddho tapati tejasā.**

By day the sun shines, by night the moon shines; The warrior shines when armed, the brāhmana shines when meditating; But the Buddha shines with splendor all day and night. 白天太阳照耀，夜晚月亮照耀，武士武装辉耀；婆罗门禅定光明；佛陀的光辉昼夜照耀。

**6(388). Bāhitapāpo'ti brāhmaṇo, samacariyā samaṇo'ti vuccati;\
Pabbājay'attano malaṃ, tasmā "pabbajito"ti vuccati.**

He is called a brāhmana because he has banished evil, he is called a samana because of his righteous conduct; For he renunciates (pabbajeti) his own (attano) defilements (mala), therefore he is called \" a renunciate (pabbajja), one who has gone forth.\" 因已去除恶行，故称为婆罗门；因以正行生活，故称为沙门；因令自我出离污垢，因此被称为"出家者"。

**7(389). Na Brāhmaṇassa pahareyya, n'āssa muñcetha brāhmaṇo;\
Dhī Brāhmaṇassa hantāraṃ, tato dhī yassa muñcati.**

One should not strike a brāhmana, nor should a brāhmana vent (his wrath) on one who has struck him. Shame on him who strikes a brāhmana! More shame on him who gives vent (to his wrath)! 不应伤害婆罗门，婆罗门也不应报复； 伤害婆罗门者可悲，而报复者更可悲。

**8(390). Na Brāhmaṇass'etadakiñci seyyo, yadā nisedho manaso piyehi;\
Yato yato hiṃsamano nivattatim, tato tato sammatimeva dukkhaṃ.**

Nothing is better for a brāhmana than restraint of mind from pleasant things; wherever the harmful mind (a mind filled with negative emotions like craving, aversion, and delusion) turns away, there suffering (dukkha) is calmed. 对于婆罗门而言，没有什么比制止心念执着于可爱之物更好；无论有害之心从何处退回，痛苦便在那里平息。

**9(391). Yassa kāyena vācāya, manasā natthi dukkataṃ;\
Saṃvutaṃ tīhi ṭhānehi, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who does no bad thing through body, speech or mind, who is restrained in these three ways --- him I call a brāhmana. 不以身语意,行作诸恶业,此三处皆防护者,我称之为婆罗门。

**10(392). Yamhā dhammaṃ vijāneyya, sammāsambuddha desitaṃ;\
Sakkaccaṃ taṃ namasseyya, aggihuttaṃ 'va brāhmaṇo.**

From whomever one learned the dhamma taught by the Fully Enlightened Buddha, you should duly pay your respects to him, as a brāhmana venerates the sacrificial fire. 正等觉者所说法，不论从何人得闻，于彼说者应敬礼，如婆罗门敬圣火 。

**11(393). Na jaṭāhi na gottena, na jaccā hoti brāhmaṇo;\
Yamhi** **saccañca dhammo ca, so sucī so ca brāhmaṇo.**

Not by matted hair, nor by family, nor by birth does one become a brāhmana. Who has the truth and the dhamma, is pure, he is a brāhmana. 不因髻发与种族，亦非生为婆罗门。唯具真理与正法，清净方是婆罗门。

**12(394). Kiṃ te jaṭāhi dummedha, kiṃ te ajinasāṭiyā;\
Abbhantaraṃ te gahaṇaṃ, bāhiraṃ parimajjasi.**

What use is your matted hair, O fool? What use is your deer skin garment? Your inside is caught (by defilements), while you purify the outside. 愚人啊，发髻对你有什么用？鹿皮衣对你有什么用？ 你的内心被束缚着，却只清洁外表。

**\**

**13(395). Paṃsukūladharaṃ jantuṃ, kisaṃ dhamanisanthataṃ;\
Ekaṃ vanasmiṃ jhāyantaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who wears just rags from a dust heap, is lean, with veins showing, who meditates alone in the forest --- him I call a brāhmana. 诸着粪扫衣，消瘦露经脉，林中独入定，是谓婆罗门。

**14(396). Na c'āhaṃ Brāhmaṇaṃ brūmi, yonijaṃ mattisambhavaṃ;\
Bhovādi nāma so hoti, sa ce hoti sakiñcano;\
Akiñcanaṃ anādānaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

I do not call him a brāhmana merely because he is born of such a womb, born from such a mother. If he has attachments, he is to be called with a title only. He who has no attachments, is free from clinging --- him I call a brāhmana. 并非因其出生而称之为婆罗门；若其人有执着（世界，此岸），则不过是徒有虚名；唯有无染着、不执取者，我才称之婆罗门。

**15(397). Sabbasaṃyojanaṃ chetvā, yo ve na paritassati;\
Saṅgātigaṃ visaṃyuttaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who has cut off all bonds, who does not tremble, who has overcome attachments and is unbound --- him I call a brāhmana. 断除一切结，彼已无恐惧，无着得解脱，是谓婆罗门。

**16(398). Chetvā naddhiṃ varattañca, sandānaṃ sahanukkamaṃ;\
Ukkhittapaḷighaṃ buddhaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who has cut off the strap and rope, fetter together with a bridle, who has all the obstacles removed and is buddha --- him I call a brāhmana. 断除皮带和绳索，以及枷锁和缰绳；已舍除障碍之佛陀，我称之为婆罗门。

**17(399). Akkosaṃ vadhabandhañca, aduṭṭho yo titikkhati;\
Khantibalaṃ balānīkaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who, without anger, endures abuse, violence and imprisonment, Whose strength is patience, whose army is fortitude --- him I call a brāhmana. 能忍受辱骂、暴力和禁锢，而不怀恶意的人； 以忍耐为力量，以坚忍为军队的人，我称之为婆罗门。

**18(400). Akkodhanaṃ vatavantaṃ, sīlavantaṃ anussadaṃ**;**\
Dantaṃ antimasārīraṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

Him who is without anger, virtuous, constantly moral (sīla); Tamed, in his last body--- him I call a brāhmana. 不嗔怒、具德行、常持戒律； 已调伏身心，只有最后一具身体者，我称之为婆罗门。

**\**

**19(401). Vāripokkharapatt'eva, āragger'iva sāsapo;\
Yo na limpati kāmesu, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

Like water on a lotus leaf, like a mustard seed on an needle\'s point, he who clings not to sense pleasures, him I call a brāhmana. 犹如水落于莲叶，如置芥子于针锋，不染着于爱欲者─我称彼为婆罗门。

**20(402). Yo dukkhassa pajānāti, idh'eva khayam attano;\
Pannabhāraṃ visaṃyuttaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who understands, right here in this very life, the destruction of his dukkha; Who has laid down the burden and is freed--- him I call a brāhmana. 现世了知苦灭尽，放下重担得解脱─我称彼为婆罗门。

**21(403). Gambhīrapaññaṃ medhāviṃ, maggāmaggassa kovidaṃ;\
Uttamatthaṃ anuppattaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He whose knowledge is deep, who is intelligent, who knows what is the path and what is not, who has reached the highest goal --- him I call a brāhmana. 有甚深智慧，善辨道非道，证最高目标，是谓婆罗门。

**22(404). Asaṃsaṭṭhaṃ gahaṭṭhehi, anāgārehi c'ūbhayaṃ;\
Anokasāriṃ appicchaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who is not mixed with householders, nor the homeless wanderers (monks), nor both, free from worldly attachment, easily satisfied --- him I call a brāhmana. 不与俗人混，不与僧相杂，无家无欲者，是谓婆罗门。

**23(405). Nidhāya daṇḍaṃ bhūtesu, tasesu thāvaresu ca;\
Yo na hanti na ghāteti, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

Having laid aside violence towards all beings, whether feeble or strong; He who neither kills nor causes others to kill---him I call a brāhmana. 一切强弱有情，彼人尽弃刀杖，不杀不教他杀──我称彼婆罗门。

**24(406). Aviruddhaṃ viruddhesu, attadaṇḍesu nibbutaṃ;\
Sādānesu anādānaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who is friendly among the hostile, peaceful among the violent, unattached among the attached --- him I call a brāhmana. 在敌对中保持友善，在暴力中保持平静； 在执着人群中保持无着，我称此人婆罗门。

**\**

**25(407). Yassa rāgo ca doso ca, māno makkho ca pātito;\
Sāsapor'iva āraggā, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He whose raga, dosa, conceit and hypocrisy, have fallen away; like a mustard seed from the point of a needle --- him I call a brāhmana. 贪欲瞋恚和慢心，以及虚伪皆脱落，犹如芥子落针锋──我称彼为婆罗门。

**26(408). Akakkasaṃ viññāpaniṃ, giraṃ saccaṃ udīraye;\
Yāya n'ābhisaje kañci, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who speaks gentle, informative, true words, by which he does not offend anyone --- him I call a brāhmana. 言语柔和真实义，益语不伤害他人，我称彼为婆罗门。

**27(409). Yo'dha dīghaṃ va rassaṃ vā, aṇuṃ thūlaṃ subhāsubhaṃim;\
Loke adinnaṃ nādiyati, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who in this world takes nothing that is not given, be it long or short, small or great, pleasant or unpleasant --- him I call a brāhmana. 无论长短、粗细、好坏，于世间不取不予之物者； 我称此人为婆罗门。

**28(410). Āsā yassa na vijjanti, asmiṃ loke paramhi ca;\
Nirāsāmsaṃ visaṃyuttaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who has no hopes in this world or the next, free from longing and all fetters --- him I call a brāhmana. 无论此世或来世，都不存有期待者； 无所希求解脱者，我称之为婆罗门。

**29(411). Yassālayā na vijjanti, aññāya akathaṃkathī;\
Amatogadhaṃ anuppattaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He in whom no attachments are found, who through knowledge is free from doubt; Who has attained the Deathless--- him I call a brāhmana. 已断除一切执着，以智慧断疑惑；已证不死之境者，我称彼为婆罗门。

**30(412). Yo'dha puññañca pāpañca, ubho saṅgaṃ upaccagā;\
Asokaṃ virajaṃ suddhaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who here has transcended both merit and demerit (in this world), attachment to both; Sorrowless, stainless, pure--- him I call a brāhmana. 超越此世善与恶，以及二者之执着；无忧清净更无染，我称彼为婆罗门。

**\**

**31(413). Candaṃ 'va vimalaṃ suddhaṃ, vippasannam anāvilaṃ;\
Nandībhavaparikkhīṇaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

Like the moon, pure, clean, serene, undisturbed; He in whom delight in (worldly) existence is destroyed--- him I call a brāhmana. 如月净无瑕，澄静而清明，灭于再生欲，是谓婆罗门。

**32(414). Yomaṃ palipathaṃ duggaṃ, saṃsāraṃ moham accagā;\
Tiṇṇo pāragato jhāyī, anejo akathaṃkathī;\
Anupādāya nibbuto, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

Who has escaped the hardship, bad place, samsāra and moha (delusion), who has crossed, reached the other side, meditating, free from craving, without doubts, not attached (to the world), who is liberated - him I call a brāhmana. 超越险峻难行的轮回之路，以及其中的愚痴迷惑； 已到达彼岸，禅定，无欲无惑； 不执着，已解脱者，我称此人为婆罗门。

**33(415). Yo'dha kāme pahamtvāna, anāgāro paribbaje;\
Kāmabhavaparikkhīṇaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

Whatever worldly sense pleasures he has abandoned, a homeless wanderer, completely removed sensual existence--- him I call a brāhmana. 弃舍欲乐于此世，出家而成无家人，除灭欲乐生起者──我称彼为婆罗门。

**34(416). Yo'dha taṇhaṃ pahantvāna, anāgāro paribbaje;\
Taṇhābhavaparikkhīṇaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

Whatever in this world he has abandoned craving for, a homeless wanderer, completely removed craving for (worldly) existence --- him I call a brāhmana. 弃舍爱欲于此世，出家而成无家人，除灭爱欲生起者──我称彼为婆罗门。

**35(417). Hitvā mānusakaṃ yogaṃ, dibbaṃ yogaṃ upaccagā;\
Sabbayogavisaṃyuttaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who has given up bondage with mankind and heavenly beings as well, is detached from all bonds --- him I call a brāhmana. 远离人间缚，超越天上缚，除一切缚者，是谓婆罗门。

**36(418). Hitvā ratiñca aratiñca, sītibhūtaṃ nirūpadhiṃ;\
Sabbalokābhibhuṃ vīraṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

Having abandoned both delight and dislike, peaceful and free from attachments; The hero, conqueror of all the world--- him I call a brāhmana. 舍弃喜爱与厌恶，已得平静无执着； 征服世间的勇者，我称彼为婆罗门。

**\**

**37(419). Cutiṃ yo vedi sattānaṃ, upapattiñca sabbaso;\
Asattaṃ sugataṃ buddhaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who knows all about the passing away and arising of beings completely, who is unattached, sugata (going to wholesomeness and virtuousness), and buddha --- him I call a brāhmana. 遍知一切有情死与生，无执着、善至、佛陀，是谓婆罗门。

**38(420). Yassa gatiṃ na jānanti, devā gandhabbamānusā;\
Khīṇāsavaṃ arahantaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

Whose destination is not known by devas, Gandhabbas, or manussa; The one whose taints are destroyed, an Arahant --- him I call a brāhmana. 其去向，天、音乐神与人族都不知晓；诸漏已尽的阿罗汉，我称此人为婆罗门。

**39(421). Yassa pure ca pacchā ca, majjhe ca natthi kiñcanaṃ;\
Akiñcanaṃ anādānaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who has no clinging to anything of past, present, or future, who is without clinging--- him I call a brāhmana. 无论过去、现在或未来，都不执着任何事物；不执取任何事物者，我称此人为婆罗门。

**40(422). Usabhaṃ pavaraṃ vīraṃ, mahesiṃ vijitāvinaṃ;\
Anejaṃ nahātakaṃ buddhaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

The strong, noble hero, the great seer (ṛṣi 仙), the victorious one; Free from craving, the purified, the buddha --- him I call a brāhmana. 他是强而有力的、殊胜的勇士，伟大的仙人，胜利者；无欲无求，已得清净，佛陀，我称此人为婆罗门。

**41(423). Pubbenivāsaṃ yo vedī, saggāpāyañca passata;\
Atho jātikkhayaṃ patto, abhiñjāvosito muni;\
Sabbavositavosānaṃ, tam ahaṃ brūmi Brāhmaṇaṃ.**

He who knows his former abodes (lives), and sees heaven and the lower realms; Moreover, has attained the destruction of birth, the sage who has attained supernormal knowledges; Who has attained all accomplishments--- him I call a brāhmana. 他能知晓过去世，并能看见天界与恶道；而且证得生之灭，已得神通的圣者；已达到一切圆满成就------我称此人为婆罗门。

### **Summary of Brāhmaṇa Vagga**

Brāhmaṇa Vagga redefines the concept of a \"Brahmana\" not by birth or lineage, as was the traditional understanding in ancient India, but by inner qualities, spiritual attainment, and liberation from defilements. True \"Brahmanahood\" is not determined by birth into a priestly caste but by one\'s moral conduct, wisdom, self-mastery, and liberation from craving and suffering.

- **Effort and Liberation (Verses 383-384):** A true Brahmana strives to cut off the stream of craving, drives away sensual pleasures, and understands the destruction of all formations, thus knowing the uncreated (Nibbana). When one transcends the duality of \"this shore\" (samsara) and \"the other shore\" (Nibbana), all bonds are broken.

- **Transcending All Shores (Verse 385):** A Brahmana is one who knows all shores (this shore, the other shore, and both), is free from craving, and is unbound. This emphasizes complete transcendence of all worldly attachments.

- **Meditation and Purity (Verse 386):** A Brahmana is meditative, stainless, secluded, has fulfilled their duties, is free from influxes (asavas), and has attained the highest goal. This highlights the importance of meditative practice and purity of mind.

- **The Radiance of the Buddha (Verse 387):** While the sun shines by day, the moon by night, warriors in armor, and Brahmanas in meditation, the Buddha shines with splendor both day and night. This emphasizes the supreme enlightenment of the Buddha.

- **The Meaning of \"Brahmana,\" \"Samana,\" and \"Renunciate\" (Verse 388):** These terms are defined by inner qualities: banishing evil, righteous conduct, and renouncing defilements, respectively. This shifts the focus from external status to internal transformation.

- **Non-Violence and Restraint (Verses 389-390):** One should not harm a Brahmana, nor should a Brahmana retaliate. Restraining the mind from pleasant things is the best for a Brahmana; calming the harmful mind brings the cessation of suffering. This emphasizes non-violence and mental discipline.

- **Restraint in Three Ways (Verse 391):** A Brahmana is restrained in body, speech, and mind, doing no evil through these three doors.

- **Respect for the Teacher (Verse 392):** One should respect the teacher from whom one learns the Dhamma, just as a Brahmana venerates the sacrificial fire.

- **True Brahmanahood is Not by Birth (Verses 393-396):** One does not become a Brahmana by matted hair, lineage, or birth. True Brahmanahood is defined by truthfulness, adherence to the Dhamma, purity, detachment, and freedom from clinging. External appearances are meaningless without inner transformation.

- **Cutting Off All Bonds (Verses 397-398):** A Brahmana has cut off all bonds, does not tremble (from fear or craving), has overcome attachments, is unbound, has removed all obstacles, and is a Buddha (enlightened).

- **Endurance and Patience (Verse 399):** A Brahmana endures abuse, violence, and imprisonment without anger, with patience as their strength and fortitude as their army.

- **Virtue and Taming (Verse 400):** A Brahmana is without anger, virtuous, constantly moral, tamed, and in their last body (having reached the end of rebirth).

- **Non-Attachment to Sensual Pleasures (Verse 401):** A Brahmana is like water on a lotus leaf or a mustard seed on a needle\'s point---they do not cling to sensual pleasures.

- **Understanding the Destruction of Suffering (Verse 402):** A Brahmana understands the destruction of suffering in this very life, has laid down the burden (of defilements), and is freed.

- **Deep Wisdom and Knowledge of the Path (Verse 403):** A Brahmana possesses deep wisdom, is intelligent, knows the right path and the wrong path, and has reached the highest goal.

- **Non-Association and Contentment (Verse 404):** A Brahmana is not mixed with householders or homeless wanderers, is free from worldly attachments, and is easily satisfied.

- **Non-Violence Towards All Beings (Verse 405):** A Brahmana has laid aside violence toward all beings, neither killing nor causing others to kill.

- **Friendliness and Peace (Verse 406):** A Brahmana is friendly among the hostile, peaceful among the violent, and unattached among the attached.

- **Overcoming Defilements (Verse 407):** A Brahmana has completely eradicated rāga (passion/lust), dosa (hatred/aversion), māna (conceit/pride), and makkha (hypocrisy).

- **Truthful and Gentle Speech (Verse 408):** A Brahmana speaks gentle, informative, and truthful words that do not offend anyone.

- **Not Taking What is Not Given (Verse 409):** A Brahmana does not take anything that is not freely given.

- **Freedom from Hopes and Longing (Verse 410):** A Brahmana has no hopes or expectations in this world or the next, being free from longing and all fetters.

- **Freedom from Attachments and Doubt (Verse 411):** A Brahmana has no attachments and is free from doubt through direct knowledge, having attained the Deathless (Nibbana).

- **Transcending Merit and Demerit (Verse 412):** A Brahmana has transcended both merit and demerit and their associated attachments, being sorrowless, stainless, and pure.

- **Purity and Freedom from Delight in Existence (Verse 413):** A Brahmana is like the pure, clean, serene, and undisturbed moon, having destroyed delight in worldly existence.

- **Escaping Samsara and Delusion (Verse 414):** A Brahmana has escaped the difficult path of samsara (the cycle of rebirth) and delusion (moha), has crossed over to the other side (Nibbana), is meditative, free from craving, without doubts, unattached, and liberated.

- **Abandoning Sensual Pleasures and Craving (Verses 415-416):** A Brahmana has abandoned all sensual pleasures and craving, becoming a homeless wanderer and completely eliminating the causes of future existence.

- **Transcending All Bonds (Verses 417-418):** A Brahmana has given up all bonds, both human and divine, and has abandoned both delight and dislike, being peaceful, free from attachments, and a conqueror of the world.

- **Knowing the Passing Away and Arising of Beings (Verse 419):** A Brahmana fully understands the cycle of birth and death, is unattached, Sugata (well-gone), and a Buddha (enlightened).

- **Unknown Destination and Destruction of Taints (Verse 420):** A Brahmana\'s destination is unknown to gods, Gandharvas (celestial musicians), and humans, having destroyed all taints (asavas) and become an Arahant.

- **No Clinging to Past, Present, or Future (Verse 421):** A Brahmana has no clinging to anything in the past, present, or future.

- **The Noble Hero, Seer, and Buddha (Verse 422):** A Brahmana is a strong, noble hero, a great seer (mahesi), a victor, free from craving, purified, and a Buddha.

- **Knowledge of Past Lives and Attainment of Liberation (Verse 423):** A Brahmana knows their past lives, sees heaven and the lower realms, has attained the destruction of birth, possesses supernormal knowledge, and has achieved all accomplishments.

In essence, the Brāhmaṇa Vagga presents a redefinition of \"Brahmana\" based on spiritual attainment rather than birth. It emphasizes the importance of inner purity, self-mastery, wisdom, detachment and liberation from worldly suffering as the true hallmarks of a holy person.
